Definition

Payroll is the process by which employers pay an employee for the work they have performed. It includes calculating wages, withholding taxes and deductions, and distributing payments. In the United States, payroll taxes include federal income tax, state income tax, Social Security, and Medicare contributions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q How do I calculate my net pay from gross salary?

To calculate net pay, start with your gross salary and subtract all deductions: federal income tax (based on tax brackets and filing status), state income tax (varies by state), Social Security tax (6.2% up to the wage base), Medicare tax (1.45% plus 0.9% additional for high earners), and any other pre-tax deductions. The remaining amount is your net (take-home) pay.

Q What are the 2026 federal tax brackets?

The 2026 federal tax brackets for single filers are: 10% up to $11,925, 12% from $11,926 to $48,475, 22% from $48,476 to $103,350, 24% from $103,351 to $197,300, 32% from $197,301 to $250,525, 35% from $250,526 to $626,350, and 37% over $626,350. Brackets for married filing jointly are approximately double these amounts.

Q What is the Social Security tax rate for 2026?

The Social Security tax rate for employees is 6.2% of gross wages up to the Social Security wage base limit of $176,100 for 2026. Employers match this amount, paying an additional 6.2%. Self-employed individuals pay both halves for a total of 12.4%.

Q What is the Medicare tax rate for 2026?

The Medicare tax rate for employees is 1.45% with no wage base limit. An additional Medicare tax of 0.9% applies to wages exceeding $200,000 for single filers or $250,000 for married filing jointly. Employers match only the base 1.45% rate.

Q Which states have no income tax?

Nine US states have no state income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. New Hampshire and Tennessee previously taxed only investment income but have phased that out. Residents of these states keep more of their gross pay.

Q How is overtime pay calculated?

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), overtime pay is calculated at 1.5 times the regular hourly rate for all hours worked over 40 in a workweek. For example, if your regular rate is $20/hour, overtime is $30/hour. Some states like California also require daily overtime for hours exceeding 8 in a single day.

Q What is the difference between gross pay and net pay?

Gross pay is your total earnings before any deductions. Net pay is what you actually take home after all deductions are subtracted. Deductions include federal income tax, state income tax, Social Security (FICA), Medicare, and any voluntary deductions like 401(k) contributions or health insurance premiums.

Q How does filing status affect my paycheck?

Filing status determines which tax bracket thresholds and standard deduction apply to your income. Single filers use narrower brackets, while Married Filing Jointly filers benefit from wider brackets, effectively being taxed at lower rates for the same income. Head of Household falls between the two, with its own set of brackets.

Q What is the standard deduction for 2026?

The standard deduction for 2026 is $15,000 for single filers, $30,000 for married filing jointly, and $22,500 for head of household. These amounts reduce your taxable income before tax brackets are applied.
