Torque to Power Conversion

Power is the product of torque and angular velocity: P = τω. Enter a torque and a rotational speed (RPM) to convert to watts and horsepower. This is one of the most important relationships in mechanical engineering. Every electric motor, every internal combustion engine, every wind turbine operates within this relationship.

Engine Torque vs Horsepower

In automotive engineering, torque and horsepower are related by RPM. Horsepower = Torque (ft·lbs) × RPM / 5252. At 5,252 RPM, torque and horsepower are always numerically equal (when measured in ft·lbs and HP respectively). This is a mathematical identity, not a coincidence. It falls out of the unit definitions.

Understanding the Crossover

When you look at a dyno chart, the torque and horsepower curves always cross at exactly 5,252 RPM. Below that RPM, torque is numerically higher than horsepower. Above it, horsepower is numerically higher. Diesel truck engines produce peak torque at low RPM (1,500-2,500 RPM), giving them enormous pulling force at low speeds. Sport car engines produce peak power at high RPM (6,000-8,000 RPM), giving them high top speed.

HP = τ(ft·lbs) × RPM / 5252. The constant 5252 comes from 33,000 ft·lbs/min (definition of 1 HP) divided by 2π. This isn't a rule of thumb. It is an exact mathematical relationship.

Gear Ratio Torque Multiplication

Gears trade speed for torque (or vice versa). A gear ratio of 3:1 means the output shaft turns three times slower but has three times the torque of the input. This is the foundation of transmissions, differentials, and reduction gearboxes. Power in equals power out (minus friction losses), but the distribution between torque and speed changes.

Practical Applications

Automotive Wheel Lug Nuts

Most passenger cars require lug nuts torqued to 80-120 N·m (59-89 ft·lbs). Trucks and SUVs typically require 135-200 N·m. Over-torquing can warp brake rotors and strip threads. Under-torquing can cause wheels to loosen. A calibrated torque wrench is not optional for this job. I've seen warped rotors from impact guns used without a torque stick more times than I can count.

Automotive Cylinder Head Bolts

Head bolts on modern engines are torqued in specific sequences and often require a torque-to-yield (TTY) procedure where the bolt is first torqued to a value (for example 50 N·m) and then turned an additional 90 degrees. TTY bolts stretch permanently and must not be reused. The torque sequence ensures even clamping pressure across the head gasket surface.

Construction Structural Bolts

High-strength structural bolts (ASTM A325 and A490) are critical in steel construction. A325 bolts are typically torqued to 70% of their proof load. For a 3/4 inch A325 bolt, that is approximately 254 ft·lbs. These values are specified in the AISC Steel Construction Manual and must be verified on every structural connection.

Bicycle Maintenance

Carbon fiber bicycle components are extremely sensitive to over-torquing. Stem bolts on a carbon steerer tube are typically 4-6 N·m. Seatpost clamp bolts: 5-7 N·m. Handlebar clamp bolts: 5-7 N·m. Exceeding these values by even 2-3 N·m can crack a carbon component, which is a catastrophic and expensive failure. Tiny torque wrenches calibrated for 1-10 N·m are essential for carbon bike work.

Electric Motors

Servo motors and stepper motors are specified by their continuous torque and peak torque ratings. A NEMA 23 stepper motor typically produces 1.0-2.5 N·m of holding torque. Selecting the right motor for a CNC machine or robot joint requires matching the required torque (including acceleration torque) to the motor's torque curve across the operating speed range.

Formulas Explained

Basic Torque Formula

Torque is the cross product of the position vector and the force vector:

τ = r × F × sin(θ)

Where r is the distance from the pivot (lever arm), F is the applied force, and θ is the angle between the force direction and the lever arm. Maximum torque occurs when θ = 90 degrees (force perpendicular to lever arm). At θ = 0 or 180 degrees (force parallel to lever arm), torque is zero because the force passes through the pivot.

Newton's Second Law for Rotation

τ = I × α

Net torque equals moment of inertia times angular acceleration. This is the rotational analog of F = ma. The moment of inertia I depends on both the mass and how that mass is distributed relative to the axis of rotation. A solid cylinder has I = 0.5 m r². A hollow cylinder has I = m r². This is why a flywheel stores more energy than a solid disk of the same mass and radius.

Torque and Power

P = τ × ω

Power (in watts) equals torque (in N·m) times angular velocity (in radians per second). Since ω = 2π × RPM / 60:

P = τ × 2π × RPM / 60

And the automotive horsepower formula (using imperial units):

HP = τ(ft·lbs) × RPM / 5252

Gear Ratio Torque Multiplication

τ_out = τ_in × gear_ratio × efficiency
ω_out = ω_in / gear_ratio

A speed reduction gear increases torque by the same factor it reduces speed. The efficiency factor accounts for friction losses in the gear teeth, bearings, and lubrication. spur gears 95-98%, worm gears 40-90%, planetary gears 90-97%.

Wrench Extension Correction

Torque_setting = Torque_desired × L / (L + E)

Where L is the original wrench length and E is the extension length. When you add an extension in line with the wrench (extending the lever arm), you set the torque wrench lower because the longer arm produces more torque for the same dial reading.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is torque?+
Torque is a rotational force that causes an object to rotate about an axis. It equals the force applied multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation (lever arm) and the sine of the angle between them: τ = r F sin(θ). The SI unit is the newton-meter (N·m).
What is the formula for torque?+
Torque = Force × Distance × sin(Angle), or τ = rF sin(θ). When the force is perpendicular to the lever arm (θ = 90 degrees), it simplifies to τ = rF. This is the most common case in practical applications like tightening bolts.
How do I convert N·m to ft·lbs?+
Multiply newton-meters by 0.7376 to get foot-pounds. For example, 100 N·m = 73.76 ft·lbs. To convert the other way, multiply ft·lbs by 1.3558 to get N·m.
Why does a longer wrench require less force?+
Because torque = force × distance. If you double the wrench length (distance), you only need half the force to produce the same torque. This is the principle of mechanical advantage. It is why breaker bars are long, and why you can loosen a stuck bolt by slipping a pipe over your wrench handle.
What is the difference between torque and power?+
Torque is a rotational force (measured in N·m or ft·lbs). Power is the rate of doing work, equal to torque times angular velocity: P = τω. An engine can have high torque at low RPM (diesel truck) or lower torque at high RPM (sport car) and still produce the same peak power. Torque determines how hard you can push. Power determines how fast you can push hard.
Should bolts be lubricated before torquing?+
It depends on the application. Lubricated bolts achieve higher clamping force at the same torque value because less energy is lost to friction. If the torque specification assumes dry bolts and you use lubricated bolts at the same torque, you can overstress the bolt. Reduce torque by 20-25% for lubricated fasteners unless the spec explicitly says "lubricated."
What torque spec for lug nuts?+
Most passenger cars: 80-120 N·m (59-89 ft·lbs). Light trucks and SUVs: 135-200 N·m (100-148 ft·lbs). Heavy trucks: 400-600 N·m. Always check your vehicle's manual for the exact specification.
How does a gear ratio multiply torque?+
A gear ratio multiplies torque by the ratio. A 3:1 ratio triples the torque but reduces the speed by one-third. Power (minus friction losses) stays the same: P = τ_in × ω_in = τ_out × ω_out. First gear in a car has a high ratio (large torque multiplication) for starting from a stop. Top gear has a low ratio (near 1:1) for cruising speed.
Why do torque and horsepower cross at 5,252 RPM?+
Because HP = Torque(ft·lbs) × RPM / 5252. At RPM = 5252, the equation becomes HP = Torque × 5252 / 5252 = Torque. So the numerical values are equal. The constant 5252 comes from 33,000 ft·lbs/min (definition of horsepower) divided by 2π. It is a mathematical identity, not a design choice.
