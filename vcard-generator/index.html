How to Create Digital Contact Cards

Follow these steps to generate professional vCard files and QR codes for easy contact sharing.

Step 1: Enter Your Name and Organization

Fill in your full name (prefix, first, middle, last, suffix) along with your organization and job title. These are the primary fields that appear when someone imports your contact.

Step 2: Add Contact Methods

Add your email addresses, phone numbers, and physical addresses. Each field supports type labels (work, home, mobile, etc.) so recipients know which number or email to use for different purposes.

Step 3: Add Social Media and Websites

Include links to your LinkedIn, Twitter/X, GitHub, Instagram profiles, and any websites. These are embedded in the vCard and appear in the contact details on modern devices.

Step 4: Upload a Photo (Optional)

Upload a profile photo or provide a URL. The photo is encoded directly into the vCard file so it appears as the contact image on the recipient's device.

Step 5: Choose vCard Version

Select vCard 3.0 for maximum compatibility with older devices, or vCard 4.0 for modern applications with better feature support.

Step 6: Download or Share via QR Code

Download the .vcf file to share via email or messaging, or use the auto-generated QR code that others can scan to instantly save your contact. You can also create multiple contacts and download them all at once.

About the vCard (VCF) Format

The vCard format (Virtual Contact File), commonly saved with the .vcf file extension, is the standard file format for electronic business cards. The format was originally developed by Versit Consortium in 1995 and is now maintained as an Internet standard by the IETF.

vCard 3.0 (RFC 2426, published 1998) is the most widely supported version, compatible with virtually all contact management applications, email clients, and mobile devices. It supports structured name fields, multiple phone numbers, email addresses, physical addresses, organization information, photos, and notes.

vCard 4.0 (RFC 6350, published 2011) introduced improvements including better internationalization support (UTF-8 by default), additional property types, the KIND property for distinguishing individuals from organizations, and improved handling of instant messaging and social media profiles.

The vCard format is text-based and human-readable, using a property:value structure. Properties can have parameters (such as TYPE=WORK or TYPE=HOME) that provide additional context. Binary data like photos can be embedded using Base64 encoding or referenced via URLs.

vCards are universally supported across platforms including iOS Contacts, Android Contacts, Google Contacts, Microsoft Outlook, Apple Mail, Thunderbird, and nearly all CRM and contact management software.

Common Use Cases and Tips

Networking Events and Conferences

Generate a QR code of your vCard and display it on your phone or print it on your physical business card. Contacts simply scan the code to save your full contact details, eliminating the need for manual entry.

Email Signatures

Attach your VCF file to your email signature so every recipient can save your contact with one click. This is particularly effective for sales teams and customer-facing roles where contact accessibility matters.

Company Employee Directory

Use the batch generation feature to create vCards for your entire team. Download them all as a single VCF file for easy distribution or import into a CRM system.

Embedding Contact Info in NFC Tags

Write the vCard data or a URL pointing to your VCF file onto NFC-enabled business cards or stickers. When someone taps their phone against it, your contact information appears ready to save.

Personal Website Contact Page

Host your VCF file on your website and provide a download link. Visitors can save your contact without manually copying phone numbers and email addresses.

Pro Tip: Photo Size

Keep profile photos under 100KB for best compatibility. Large photos increase the VCF file size and QR code complexity. A square image (e.g., 200x200 pixels) in JPEG format works well across all platforms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a vCard (VCF) file?

A vCard is a digital contact card file format (.vcf) used to share contact information electronically. It stores details such as name, phone numbers, email addresses, organization, photos, and more. vCards are supported by virtually all devices, email clients, and contact management applications worldwide.

What is the difference between vCard 3.0 and 4.0?

vCard 3.0 (RFC 2426) is the most widely compatible version, supported by all major platforms. vCard 4.0 (RFC 6350) adds improvements like default UTF-8 encoding, the KIND property, better support for instant messaging and social profiles, and more flexible data types. Use 3.0 for maximum compatibility with older devices, and 4.0 for modern applications.

How do I share a vCard with someone?

You can share vCards in several ways: email attachment, messaging apps (iMessage, WhatsApp, Telegram), AirDrop (Apple devices), NFC tags on physical cards, QR codes that can be scanned, or by hosting the VCF file on a website with a download link. QR codes are especially popular for in-person networking.

How do QR code contact cards work?

A QR code contact card encodes the vCard data into a scannable QR code. When someone scans it with their phone camera or a QR reader app, the vCard data is parsed automatically and they can save the contact with a single tap. This tool generates QR codes in real time as you fill in your contact details.

How do I import a VCF file to my phone?

On iPhone: Open the VCF file from Mail, Files, or Safari, then tap "Add to Contacts" or "Create New Contact." On Android: Open the Contacts app, go to Settings or Manage contacts, tap Import, and select the VCF file. You can also simply open a VCF file from email attachments or messaging apps on both platforms.

Can I use a vCard in my email signature?

Yes, attaching a VCF file to your email signature is a professional way to share contact details. In Outlook, go to Signature settings and add the VCF as an attachment. In Apple Mail, you can drag a contact from Contacts into the signature editor. Recipients can click the attachment to save your details instantly.

Does vCard work with Apple Wallet?

Apple Wallet does not natively import VCF files directly. However, you can use NFC-enabled cards or stickers that trigger a vCard download, or create QR codes linking to a hosted VCF file. Third-party services also exist that can create Apple Wallet passes from vCard data for convenient sharing via AirDrop.

Browser Compatibility
