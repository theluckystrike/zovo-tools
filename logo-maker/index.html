How to Use This Free Logo Maker

Creating a logo with this tool takes just a few steps. You do not need any design experience or software downloads. Every feature runs directly inside your web browser, and the final result is yours to use anywhere you want.

Step 1 Enter Your Brand Name

Start by typing your company name, project title, or personal brand into the text input field. The canvas updates in real time as you type, so you can see exactly how your text looks at every stage. You can adjust the font size, letter spacing, and weight using the sliders below the text input. Increasing letter spacing works well for uppercase logos that need a more spacious, premium feel. Tighter spacing suits bold, compact wordmarks.

Step 2 Choose a Font

The font selector includes more than 20 popular Google Fonts. Each font loads dynamically when you select it, so you can preview every option on your canvas without refreshing the page. Sans-serif fonts like Montserrat, Poppins, and Inter work well for modern, clean brands. Serif fonts like Playfair Display and Merriweather add a classic, trustworthy feel. Script fonts like Pacifico suit creative or lifestyle brands. Display fonts like Bebas Neue and Archivo Black are headlines and bold statements.

Step 3 Pick an Icon

Browse through the icon library to find a shape that represents your brand. The collection includes geometric shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and hexagons. You will also find symbolic icons such as stars, hearts, shields, lightning bolts, arrows, checkmarks, and more. Each icon renders directly on the canvas using vector paths, which means they stay sharp at any size. Adjust the icon size and color using the controls in the right panel.

Step 4 Arrange Your Layout

Five layout options let you position the icon and text in different configurations. "Icon Top" places the icon above the text, which works well for square logos. "Icon Left" and "Icon Right" create horizontal arrangements suitable for website headers and business cards. "Text Only" removes the icon entirely for clean wordmark logos. "Icon Only" removes the text for app icons, favicons, and social media profile pictures.

Step 5 Set Colors and Background

Use the color pickers to set your text color, icon color, and background color independently. The background supports three modes: solid color, gradient, and transparent. Gradients let you pick two colors and an angle to create directional color blends. Transparent backgrounds are essential when you place your logo on top of photos, colored surfaces, or other design elements.

Step 6 Apply a Style Preset

Six style presets give you quick starting points. Minimal uses thin weights and muted colors. Bold goes heavy with bright accent colors. Elegant pairs serif fonts with gold tones. Playful uses rounded fonts and vibrant hues. Tech applies monospace-style fonts with neon accents. Corporate sets conservative fonts and blue tones. Every preset can be further customized after applying it.

Step 7 Export Your Logo

When your design is ready, click "Export PNG" for a raster image or "Export SVG" for a scalable vector file. PNG files work for websites, social media, and digital use. SVG files are print materials, signage, and situations where you scale the logo without losing quality. Use the size presets to quickly set dimensions for common use cases: favicon (32x32), social media cover (1200x630), square (500x500), or enter custom dimensions.

What Makes a Great Logo

A strong logo communicates your brand identity in a single glance. It should be simple enough to recognize at small sizes yet distinctive enough to stand out among competitors. The most memorable logos in history follow a few consistent principles that apply regardless of industry or budget.

Simplicity matters more than complexity. A logo that uses one or two colors, a clean typeface, and a single icon or shape will reproduce well across business cards, billboards, websites, and embroidered merchandise. Overly complex designs with fine details often lose clarity when scaled down to favicon size or printed on small surfaces.

Color psychology plays a real role in how people perceive your brand. Blue conveys trust and professionalism, which is why financial institutions and technology companies favor it. Green signals growth, health, and sustainability. Red creates urgency and energy. Black communicates luxury and sophistication. Choose colors that align with the emotional response you want from your audience.

Typography carries meaning beyond the words themselves. A rounded, friendly font sends a different message than a sharp, angular one. Serif fonts suggest tradition and reliability. Sans-serif fonts feel modern and accessible. Script fonts imply creativity and personal touch. The weight of your font also matters: lighter weights feel delicate and refined, while heavier weights project strength and confidence.

Common Logo Styles Explained

Understanding the main categories of logo design helps you decide which approach suits your brand.

Wordmark Logos

A wordmark uses only styled text with no icon or symbol. Google, Coca-Cola, and FedEx are well-known wordmarks. This style works when your company name is short, unique, and already carries strong brand recognition. Wordmarks are also the simplest to produce and reproduce across different media. Use the "Text Only" layout in this tool to create a wordmark.

Lettermark Logos

Lettermarks use initials or abbreviations instead of the full company name. IBM, HBO, and NASA all use lettermarks. This approach works for companies with long names that would be difficult to fit into a compact logo. Enter just your initials in the text field and increase the font size for a strong lettermark design.

Icon and Text Combination

Combination logos pair an icon with text. Most brands start with this format because it allows the icon and name to work together while the brand builds recognition. Over time, some brands eventually drop the text and use the icon alone. Use the "Icon Left," "Icon Right," or "Icon Top" layouts for this approach.

Symbol or Icon Only

Pure symbol logos use an icon without any text. Apple, Nike, and Target have reached the point where their symbol alone is instantly recognizable. This works only when your brand has achieved significant awareness. The "Icon Only" layout creates this style, and it pairs well with the favicon and square size presets for app icons.

Tips for Logo Design Without Experience

You do not need a graphic design background to create an effective logo. Following a few practical guidelines will help you produce a result that looks professional and communicates your brand clearly.

Start with black and white. If your logo works in a single color, it will work everywhere. Color adds personality, but the structure needs to hold up without it. Design your layout and typography first, then add color as a final step.

Limit yourself to two fonts maximum. Using one font for the primary text and one for a tagline or secondary element keeps the design cohesive. More than two fonts creates visual confusion and looks unpolished.

Test your logo at multiple sizes. A logo that looks great at 500 pixels wide might become unreadable at 32 pixels. Use the size presets in this tool to check how your design performs at favicon, social media, and full-size dimensions. If small details disappear at small sizes, simplify them.

Leave enough space around your logo. This breathing room, called clear space in design terminology, prevents your logo from feeling cramped when placed next to other elements. A good rule of thumb is to leave a margin equal to the height of one letter in your logo text on all sides.

Look at your competitors before you design. Understanding what colors, fonts, and styles your competitors use helps you choose something that stands out rather than blending in. If every competitor uses blue sans-serif logos, consider a different color or font category to differentiate yourself.

Where to Use Your Logo

Once you export your logo, you can apply it across dozens of touchpoints. Here are the most common places where your logo will appear and the format recommendations for each.

Use a PNG with transparent background. Most website headers display logos at 150 to 300 pixels wide. The SVG format is even better for websites because it scales perfectly on high-resolution displays.

Most platforms require square images between 110 and 400 pixels. Use the square 500x500 preset for a high-quality version that works across Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, and YouTube.

Export as SVG and provide the file to your printer. Vector files ensure your logo prints at any size without pixelation. For digital business card services, PNG at 500x500 or larger works well.

A PNG between 100 and 200 pixels wide fits most email clients. Keep the file size small by using the appropriate dimensions rather than a large image scaled down in HTML.

Use the 32x32 preset to create the small icon that appears in browser tabs. This is also the format used for bookmark icons. At this size, only the simplest version of your logo will be recognizable, so consider using just your icon or first initial.
