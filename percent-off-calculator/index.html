How Percent Off Calculations Work

Calculating a percentage discount is one of the most common math operations in everyday shopping. Here is a breakdown of the key formulas and concepts:

Basic discount formula: Sale Price = Original Price x (1 - Discount / 100). For example, 25% off $80 gives you $80 x 0.75 = $60. The savings amount is simply Original Price x (Discount / 100) = $80 x 0.25 = $20.

Reverse calculation: If you know the sale price and want the original, use Original Price = Sale Price / (1 - Discount / 100). Alternatively, to find the percent off: Discount % = ((Original - Sale) / Original) x 100.

Stacking discounts: When you apply multiple discounts in sequence, each discount applies to the reduced price. The combined effect is: Final = Original x (1 - d1/100) x (1 - d2/100) x ... This always gives less savings than simply adding the percentages together. A 20% off plus 10% off combo yields 28% total savings, not 30%.

Tax after discount: In most US jurisdictions, sales tax is applied to the discounted price. This means your tax savings compound with your discount savings. The formula is: Final = Sale Price x (1 + Tax Rate / 100).

Sources: Discount calculation methods referenced from Investopedia and Khan Academy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate percent off a price?
Multiply the original price by the discount percentage, then divide by 100 to get the discount amount. Subtract that from the original price to get the sale price. For example, 25% off $80: discount = $80 x 25 / 100 = $20, sale price = $80 - $20 = $60.
How do stacked discounts work?
Stacked discounts apply sequentially, not additively. For example, 20% off then 10% off is NOT 30% off. First apply 20%: a $100 item becomes $80. Then apply 10% to $80: the final price is $72. The effective discount is 28%, not 30%.
Is 20% off then 10% off the same as 30% off?
No. Stacking 20% off and 10% off gives you 28% off total, not 30%. This is because the second discount applies to the already-reduced price. The formula: total discount = 1 - (1 - d1) x (1 - d2) = 1 - (0.8 x 0.9) = 0.28 or 28%.
How do I find the original price from a sale price?
Divide the sale price by (1 - discount/100). For example, if an item is $60 after 25% off, the original price is $60 / (1 - 0.25) = $60 / 0.75 = $80.
Should sales tax be applied before or after the discount?
In most US states, sales tax is calculated on the discounted (sale) price, not the original price. This means you save on tax too. Our calculator applies tax after the discount, which matches how most retail transactions work.
