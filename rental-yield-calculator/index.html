Understanding Rental Yield

How to Calculate Rental Yield: An Investor's Guide

Written by Michael Lip, software developer and real estate investor with four rental properties. After comparing spreadsheets and online calculators for years, I built this tool to provide the five metrics I check before making any property purchase decision. The formulas here reflect standard real estate investment analysis methods used by professionals.

Gross Yield vs Net Yield

Gross rental yield is the simplest metric. You take the annual rent and divide it by the property price, then multiply by 100 to get a percentage. If a property costs $350,000 and rents for $2,200 per month ($26,400 per year), the gross yield is 7.5%. It is a quick screening number, but it tells you nothing about profitability because it ignores all expenses.

Net yield factors in your operating costs: maintenance, insurance, property tax, vacancy loss, and management fees. The formula is (Annual Rent minus Annual Expenses) divided by Property Price. This gives you a far more realistic picture. In my experience, the gap between gross and net yield typically runs 2-3 percentage points for well-maintained properties in stable markets.

Cap Rate and Cash-on-Cash Return

The capitalization rate, or cap rate, is Net Operating Income (NOI) divided by the property value. It does not include mortgage payments, making it useful for comparing properties regardless of financing. Cash-on-cash return, on the other hand, measures the annual pre-tax cashflow relative to the total cash you invested (down payment plus closing costs). This is the metric that tells you how hard your actual dollars are working.

Vacancy Rate Assumptions

The default 5% vacancy rate in this calculator assumes roughly 18 vacant days per year. In hot rental markets, actual vacancy might be closer to 2-3%. In slower markets or with student housing that turns over annually, 8-10% is more realistic. I always recommend running the numbers at multiple vacancy rates to stress-test your investment thesis. The comparison table below the calculator does this automatically at different rent levels.

We validated all five financial metrics (gross yield, net yield, cap rate, cash-on-cash return, and monthly cashflow) against manual spreadsheet calculations and two commercial real estate analysis tools (DealCheck and BiggerPockets Calculator).
