Understanding Rental Yield

How to Calculate Rental Yield An Investor's Guide

Written by Michael Lip, software developer and real estate investor with four rental properties. After comparing spreadsheets and online calculators for years, I this tool to provide the five metrics I check before making any property purchase decision. The formulas here reflect standard real estate investment analysis methods used by professionals.

Gross Yield vs Net Yield

Gross rental yield is the simplest metric. You take the annual rent and divide it by the property price, then multiply by 100 to get a percentage. If a property costs $350,000 and rents for $2,200 per month ($26,400 per year), the gross yield is 7.5%. It is a quick screening number, but it tells you nothing about profitability because it ignores all expenses.

Net yield factors in your operating costs: maintenance, insurance, property tax, vacancy loss, and management fees. The formula is (Annual Rent minus Annual Expenses) divided by Property Price. This gives you a far more realistic picture. In my experience, the gap between gross and net yield typically runs 2-3 percentage points for well-maintained properties in stable markets.

Cap Rate and Cash-on-Cash Return

The capitalization rate, or cap rate, is Net Operating Income (NOI) divided by the property value. It does not include mortgage payments, making it useful for comparing properties regardless of financing. Cash-on-cash return, on the other hand, measures the annual pre-tax cashflow relative to the total cash you invested (down payment plus closing costs). This is the metric that tells you how hard your actual dollars are working.

Vacancy Rate Assumptions

The default 5% vacancy rate in this calculator assumes roughly 18 vacant days per year. In hot rental markets, actual vacancy might be closer to 2-3%. In slower markets or with student housing that turns over annually, 8-10% is more realistic. I always recommend running the numbers at multiple vacancy rates to stress-test your investment thesis. Understanding Significant Digits in Results

This tool displays results with appropriate significant figures for the type of calculation being performed. For critical calculations, consider keeping at least two extra significant figures in intermediate steps and rounding only the final result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is rental yield?

Rental yield is the annual rental income expressed as a percentage of the property's value. Gross rental yield is calculated by dividing annual rent by the property price and multiplying by 100. It is a key metric for comparing the income potential of different investment properties.

What is the difference between gross and net rental yield?

Gross rental yield uses total rental income divided by property price. Net rental yield subtracts operating expenses (maintenance, insurance, property management fees, vacancies) from rental income before dividing by the total investment. Net yield gives a more accurate picture of actual returns.

What is a good rental yield?

A good gross rental yield is typically 5% to 8%, while a net yield of 3% to 5% is considered solid in most markets. Higher yields often come with higher risk or less desirable locations. Markets like city centers may have lower yields but stronger capital appreciation.

What is cash-on-cash return?

Cash-on-cash return measures the annual pre-tax cash flow relative to the total cash invested (down payment, closing costs, and renovation costs). It is particularly useful for leveraged purchases where you use a mortgage, as it shows the return on your actual cash outlay.

How does vacancy rate affect rental yield?

Vacancy rate directly reduces your effective rental income. If your property sits empty for one month per year, that is roughly an 8% vacancy rate, reducing your annual income accordingly. This calculator factors in vacancy to give you a realistic net yield estimate.
