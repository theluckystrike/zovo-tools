Daylight Saving Time Info

The United States observes Daylight Saving Time (DST) in most states. During DST, clocks move forward by one hour, and time zone abbreviations change (e.g., PST becomes PDT).

About Time Zones

Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) is the primary time standard by which the world regulates clocks and time. It does not observe daylight saving time and serves as the reference point for all other time zones.

The continental United States spans four time zones: Eastern, Central, Mountain, and Pacific. Each moves one hour further behind UTC as you travel west. During daylight saving time (mid-March through early November), each zone shifts one hour closer to UTC.

This converter handles DST transitions automatically using the IANA time zone database built into your browser, so conversions are accurate for any date past, present, or future.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between PST and PDT?
PST (Pacific Standard Time) is UTC-8, used from the first Sunday of November to the second Sunday of March. PDT (Pacific Daylight Time) is UTC-7, used during the rest of the year. The switch happens automatically, and this converter accounts for it based on the date you select.
How do I convert UTC to PST manually?
Subtract 8 hours from UTC time during standard time (November-March), or subtract 7 hours during daylight saving time (March-November). For example, 20:00 UTC = 12:00 PST or 13:00 PDT. If the subtraction goes below 0, subtract from 24 and go back one day.
When does daylight saving time start and end in 2026?
In 2026, daylight saving time begins on Sunday, March 8 at 2:00 AM local time (clocks spring forward to 3:00 AM) and ends on Sunday, November 1 at 2:00 AM local time (clocks fall back to 1:00 AM). Arizona and Hawaii do not observe DST.
