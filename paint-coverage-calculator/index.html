Gallons vs Quarts Which to Buy

Choosing between gallons and quarts depends on the size of your project and cost efficiency. Here is a practical guide.

Accent wall only1 Gallon or QuartOne accent wall is 80-120 sq ft; 1 quart may suffice for 1 coat, but a gallon ensures full 2-coat coverage

Cost comparison: A gallon typically costs 2-3 times what a quart costs, but contains 4 times the paint. If you need more than 1 quart, buying a gallon is almost always more economical. Many stores do not offer custom color matching for quart sizes.

Paint Sheen Guide

Paint sheen (finish level) affects appearance, durability, washability, and how much it hides wall imperfections. Higher sheens are more durable and washable but highlight surface flaws. Lower sheens hide imperfections better but are less resistant to staining and moisture.

Flat / Matte

Zero sheen with a smooth, velvety finish. Hides surface imperfections and nail holes better than any other finish. Not washable, so fingerprints and scuffs may require repainting rather than wiping. Best for ceilings and low-traffic rooms like formal dining rooms and adult bedrooms.

CeilingsAdult bedroomsDining rooms400 sq ft/gal

Eggshell

A soft, subtle luster resembling the surface of an eggshell. Slightly more washable than flat while still doing a good job hiding minor imperfections. The most popular finish for residential walls. Strikes the best balance between appearance and practicality for most living spaces.

Living roomsBedroomsDining roomsHallways350 sq ft/gal

Satin

A gentle, pearl-like sheen that offers good washability and durability. Resists mildew and moisture better than flat or eggshell, making it suitable for moderate to high traffic areas. Shows some surface imperfections under raking light but cleans up well with a damp cloth.

Family roomsKids roomsHallwaysLaundry rooms350 sq ft/gal

Semi-Gloss

A noticeable sheen that is highly washable and moisture-resistant. for rooms exposed to water, grease, and frequent cleaning. Shows surface imperfections clearly, so wall preparation is important. Used extensively on trim, doors, and casework to walls.

KitchensBathroomsTrimDoorsCabinets300 sq ft/gal

High Gloss

A mirror-like, reflective finish. The most durable and washable of all sheens. Magnifies every surface imperfection, so surfaces must be perfectly smooth. Typically reserved for small areas, furniture, and specialty applications. Creates a dramatic, lacquered look on cabinets and doors.

Front doorsCabinetsFurnitureShutters300 sq ft/gal

Surface Preparation Guide

Proper surface preparation is the most important factor in achieving a professional-quality paint job that lasts. Skipping preparation is the number one cause of peeling, bubbling, and uneven coverage. Follow these steps in order for best results.

1

Clear and Protect the Room

Remove furniture or move it to the center and cover with drop cloths. Remove outlet and switch plate covers. Apply painter's tape along trim, ceiling edges, and any areas you protect. Lay drop cloths or plastic sheeting on the floor along all walls.

2

Clean the Walls

Wash walls with a solution of warm water and a small amount of TSP (trisodium phosphate) or a TSP substitute. This removes grease, dust, cobwebs, and nicotine film that prevent paint adhesion. Rinse with clean water and allow to dry completely (24 hours in humid conditions).

3

Repair Damage

Fill nail holes, dents, and small cracks with lightweight spackling compound. For larger holes (over 1 inch), use a mesh patch kit with joint compound. Allow patches to dry fully, then sand smooth with 120-150 grit sandpaper. Scrape away any loose or peeling old paint down to a solid edge.

4

Sand the Surface

Lightly sand the entire surface with 150-220 grit sandpaper or a sanding sponge. This creates tooth for the new paint to grip, especially on previously glossy or semi-gloss surfaces. Sand all patched areas flush with the surrounding wall. Wipe dust with a damp cloth or tack cloth.

5

Caulk Gaps and Seams

Apply paintable latex caulk to gaps between walls and trim, around window and door frames, and along any visible cracks at wall-ceiling joints. Smooth with a wet finger or caulk tool. This creates clean lines and prevents paint from bridging gaps. Allow caulk to dry per manufacturer instructions (typically 2-4 hours).

6

Prime (When Needed)

Apply primer when painting over bare drywall, bare wood, stains, water damage, smoke damage, or when making a dramatic color change. Use stain-blocking primer (shellac or oil-based) for water stains and smoke damage. Use high-adhesion primer over glossy surfaces. Use PVA drywall primer on new drywall. Allow primer to dry fully before topcoating.

Drying Times by Paint Type and Conditions

Respecting proper drying times between coats is important for adhesion, durability, and a smooth finish. Applying the second coat too early can cause lifting, wrinkling, or poor adhesion. These times assume normal conditions (65-85 degrees F, 40-70% humidity, moderate ventilation).

Factors That Affect Drying Time

  • Temperature: Paint dries faster in warm conditions (65-85 degrees F is ). Below 50 degrees F, most latex paints will not cure properly and may remain tacky or peel.
  • Humidity: High humidity (above 70%) significantly slows drying. In humid conditions, extend all drying times by 50-100%. Use a dehumidifier if necessary.
  • Ventilation: Good airflow speeds evaporation. Open windows, use fans (directing air along walls, not directly at wet paint), and keep interior doors open between rooms.
  • Coat thickness: Thicker coats take longer to dry. Apply thin, even coats rather than heavy, thick ones. Two thin coats always outperform one thick coat.
  • Surface porosity: Porous surfaces (bare drywall, wood) absorb the liquid vehicle faster, so the surface may feel dry sooner, but allow full recoat time for proper inter-coat adhesion.

Full cure vs touch dry: "Touch dry" means the surface is no longer tacky to light contact. "Full cure" means the paint has reached maximum hardness and chemical resistance. Do not scrub, wash, or place objects against newly painted surfaces until fully cured. Latex paint typically needs 2-4 weeks to fully cure even though it feels dry within hours.

Color Psychology How Colors Affect Room Feel

Color selection significantly influences the mood, perceived size, and energy of a room. While personal preference should guide your final choice, understanding color psychology can help narrow your options and avoid choices that work against a room's purpose.

White and Off-White

Creates a sense of openness, cleanliness, and spaciousness. Makes rooms feel larger and brighter by reflecting more light. Warm whites (cream, ivory) feel inviting, while cool whites (blue-white) feel crisp and modern. The most adaptable and safe choice for any room, especially small spaces and rooms with limited natural light.

Gray and Greige

Neutral, complex, and calming. Light grays open up space without the starkness of white. Warm grays (greige, with beige undertones) add coziness. Cool grays pair well with modern and minimalist aesthetics. Gray has been one of the most popular interior paint colors for over a decade due to its versatility.

Blue

Promotes calmness, relaxation, and focus. Light blues make rooms feel serene and airy. Navy and deep blue create drama and sophistication. Excellent for bedrooms, bathrooms, and home offices. Studies consistently associate blue with lower heart rate and reduced anxiety. Avoid very bright blues in dining rooms, as blue is thought to suppress appetite.

Green

Represents nature, balance, and renewal. Creates a calming, refreshing atmosphere. Sage and olive greens feel organic and grounding. Emerald and forest greens add richness and depth. Green works well in almost any room and pairs naturally with wood tones and natural materials.

Yellow

Energizing, cheerful, and optimistic. Pale yellows brighten dark rooms and kitchens. Bold yellows create warmth but can feel overwhelming on large surfaces. Best used as accents or in rooms where you stimulate energy and conversation, such as kitchens and breakfast nooks. Use softer shades in large applications.

Red and Warm Tones

Stimulating, passionate, and appetite-enhancing. Deep reds and terracotta create warmth and intimacy. Bright reds increase energy levels and make spaces feel more active. Traditionally used in dining rooms to encourage conversation and appetite. Use sparingly in bedrooms and spaces meant for relaxation, as red can increase heart rate and restlessness.

Dark and Dramatic Colors

Charcoal, black, deep navy, and dark forest green create a cocoon-like intimacy and make rooms feel luxurious and dramatic. Contrary to common belief, very dark colors can actually make medium-to-large rooms feel more inviting rather than smaller, because the walls visually recede. Best paired with good lighting and lighter furnishings to prevent a cave-like feel in small rooms.

VOC Guide Low-VOC and Zero-VOC Paint

VOC stands for volatile organic compounds, which are chemicals that evaporate into the air as paint dries and cures. VOCs contribute to indoor air pollution and can cause headaches, dizziness, and respiratory irritation. Understanding VOC levels helps you make healthier choices, especially in bedrooms, nurseries, and occupied spaces.

CategoryVOC Level (g/L)Characteristics
Conventional paint150-300+Strong odor, requires good ventilation, cheapest option. Off-gasses for weeks after application.
Low-VOC paintUnder 50Reduced odor, safer for occupied spaces. Available from all major brands. Performance comparable to conventional.
Zero-VOC paintUnder 5Minimal to no odor. Safe for nurseries and bedrooms. May cost 10-20% more. Note: colorants added at the store may increase VOCs.
Natural / organic paintNear 0Made from plant-based ingredients (clay, minerals, plant oils). Lowest environmental impact. Limited color selection and higher cost.

Tip: Even zero-VOC base paint can have VOCs added through tinting. Ask for low-VOC or zero-VOC colorants when having paint mixed. Major brands like Benjamin Moore Natura, Sherwin-Williams Harmony, and Behr Premium Plus are widely available zero-VOC options with full color-matching capability.

When to Hire a Professional

  • Two-story foyers, stairwells, or ceilings above 10 feet (requires scaffolding and safety equipment)
  • Lead paint remediation (homes before 1978 may contain lead paint; professionals are EPA-certified)
  • wall damage, plaster repair, or skim coating required
  • Time constraints or physical limitations
  • Exterior painting (involves ladders, pressure washing, weather coordination)
  • Specialty finishes like faux painting, Venetian plaster, or wallpaper removal

When DIY Makes Sense

  • Single rooms with standard 8-foot ceilings on the ground floor
  • Accent walls and small spaces
  • Trim and door painting (time-consuming but low-risk)
  • Repainting with the same or similar color (less prep needed)
  • You enjoy the work and have a free weekend

Frequently Asked Questions

How much paint do I need for a standard room?▼
A standard 12x12 room with 8-foot ceilings has about 384 sq ft of wall area. After subtracting one door (~20 sq ft) and two windows (~30 sq ft), the paintable area is about 334 sq ft. With 2 coats of eggshell paint (350 sq ft/gal coverage), you need about 1.9 gallons. Rounding up, buy 2 gallons.
How many square feet does one gallon of paint cover?▼
One gallon covers 300-400 sq ft depending on the paint type. Flat/matte provides the best coverage at about 400 sq ft per gallon. Eggshell and satin cover about 350 sq ft. Semi-gloss and high-gloss cover about 300 sq ft. Primer covers about 300 sq ft per gallon. These figures assume smooth, previously painted surfaces.
How many coats of paint do I need?▼
Two coats is the standard for most painting projects, providing even color and proper durability. One coat may work when repainting with the same color over a clean, undamaged surface. Three or more coats are needed when covering dark colors with light ones, applying red or yellow pigments (which have lower opacity), or painting over bare, unprimed surfaces.
Should I buy paint in gallons or quarts?▼
Gallons are more cost-effective for any project larger than a single small accent area. A gallon costs about 2-3 times more than a quart but provides 4 times the paint. If your calculations show 1.3 gallons needed, buy 2 gallons to ensure full coverage and keep leftover paint for future touch-ups. Many paint stores only offer custom tinting in gallon sizes.
What paint finish should I use for each room?▼
Flat/matte for ceilings and low-traffic rooms. Eggshell for living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms. Satin for hallways, family rooms, and kids rooms. Semi-gloss for kitchens, bathrooms, and all trim work. High-gloss for doors, cabinets, and high-moisture areas. Each step up in sheen adds durability and washability but shows more surface imperfections.
Do textured walls need more paint?▼
Yes. Light textures like orange peel require about 15% more paint. Medium textures like knockdown need about 20% more. Heavy textures like popcorn ceilings can require 30-50% more paint than smooth surfaces because of the dramatically increased surface area. The single room calculator above includes a texture adjustment factor.
How long does paint take to dry between coats?▼
Latex (water-based) paint needs 2-4 hours between coats under normal conditions (65-85 degrees F, moderate humidity). Oil-based paint needs 16-24 hours between coats. High humidity, low temperatures, or thick application can extend these times significantly. Always check the manufacturer's recommendations on the paint can.
What is the difference between low-VOC and zero-VOC paint?▼
Low-VOC paint contains less than 50 grams per liter of volatile organic compounds, while zero-VOC contains less than 5 g/L. Both dramatically reduce indoor air quality concerns compared to conventional paint (150-300+ g/L). Zero-VOC is the safest choice for nurseries, bedrooms, and occupied spaces. Performance of modern low-VOC and zero-VOC paints is comparable to conventional formulations.
Should I use primer before painting?▼
Use primer when painting bare drywall, bare wood, over stains or water damage, making dramatic color changes, painting over glossy surfaces, or covering smoke/odor damage. Self-priming (paint-and-primer-in-one) products work adequately for repainting similar colors on clean, previously painted surfaces but are not a substitute for dedicated primer on bare or damaged surfaces.
How much does it cost to paint a room professionally?▼
Professional painters typically charge $2-6 per square foot of wall area, or $400-800+ per average room including labor and materials. DIY painting costs $80-230 per room for paint and supplies. The primary savings in DIY is labor cost. Consider professional painting for high ceilings, prep work, lead paint concerns, or when you value time over budget savings.
