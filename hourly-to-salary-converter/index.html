What Is Hourly to Salary Conversion

According to Wikipedia's definition of wages, a wage is monetary compensation paid by an employer to an employee in exchange for work done. Wages can be structured as hourly rates (payment per hour worked) or as salaries (a fixed annual amount divided into regular payments regardless of specific hours). Converting between the two is a fundamental personal finance calculation that helps you compare job offers, negotiate raises, and understand your true earning rate.

The basic conversion is simple multiplication. But the real value of this tool is in the details. When you factor in overtime, taxes, benefits, and actual hours worked, the comparison between hourly and salaried positions becomes much more nuanced than most people realize.

A salaried employee earning $60,000 per year who works 50 hours per week is effectively earning $23.08 per hour. An hourly worker earning $23 per hour who works 40 hours with 5 hours of overtime per week earns $60,950 per year. Nearly the same gross pay, but the hourly worker gets compensated for every extra hour while the salaried worker does not.

How to Use This Converter

Enter your hourly rate, how many hours you work per week, and how many weeks per year you work. The standard is 40 hours and 52 weeks, but adjust if your situation differs. Part-time workers might enter 25 hours. If you take 2 weeks of unpaid vacation, enter 50 weeks.

The filing status and state tax rate fields allow for more accurate take-home pay estimates. Single filers face higher federal tax rates than married couples filing jointly on the same income. State taxes range from 0% (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming) to over 13% (California's top bracket).

If you regularly work overtime, enter your average weekly overtime hours. The calculator applies the standard 1.5x rate mandated by the Fair Labor Standards Act. This can significantly increase your annual earnings. Ten hours of overtime per week at $20/hour adds $15,600 per year.

The Conversion Formula

The core formula is: Annual Salary = Hourly Rate x Hours/Week x Weeks/Year

For a standard full-time worker: $25/hour x 40 hours x 52 weeks = $52,000/year.

With overtime: Annual = (Regular Hours x Rate x Weeks) + (OT Hours x Rate x 1.5 x Weeks)

Example with 5 overtime hours: ($25 x 40 x 52) + ($25 x 1.5 x 5 x 52) = $52,000 + $9,750 = $61,750/year.

To go the other direction (salary to hourly): Hourly Rate = Annual Salary / (Hours/Week x Weeks/Year)

A $75,000 salary at 40 hours/week: $75,000 / (40 x 52) = $36.06/hour. But if you actually work 50 hours/week, the effective rate is $75,000 / (50 x 52) = $28.85/hour. That is a 20% difference from the "official" rate, and it matters when comparing to an hourly job offer.

Understanding Tax Estimates

Federal income tax in the United States uses a progressive bracket system. You do not pay a flat rate on all your income. Instead, each portion of your income is taxed at increasing rates as you earn more. For 2025 single filers: the first $11,925 is taxed at 10%, $11,926 to $48,475 at 12%, $48,476 to $103,350 at 22%, and higher brackets above that.

This means someone earning $60,000 pays: ($11,925 x 0.10) + (($48,475 - $11,925) x 0.12) + (($60,000 - $48,475) x 0.22) = $1,192.50 + $4,386 + $2,535.50 = $8,114 in federal income tax. The effective tax rate is 13.5%, even though the marginal rate is 22%. Many people confuse these two numbers.

Social Security tax is 6.2% on income up to $176,100 (2025 cap). Your employer matches this, paying another 6.2%. Medicare tax is 1.45% with no income cap, plus an additional 0.9% surtax on income above $200,000 for single filers. These are collectively called FICA taxes.

The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator can give you a more precise picture based on your specific deductions, credits, and filing situation. This converter provides estimates for comparison purposes.

Hourly vs Salary Pros and Cons

Hourly workers get paid for every hour they work. If you stay an extra 2 hours on a Friday, you earn 2 more hours of pay (potentially at overtime rate). The downside is that hours are not guaranteed. If business is slow and your boss cuts your hours from 40 to 32 one week, your paycheck shrinks by 20%. During shutdowns or slow periods, your income can fluctuate significantly.

Salaried employees receive the same paycheck regardless of whether they worked 38 hours or 55 hours that week. This income stability makes budgeting easier. Salaried positions also tend to come with better benefits: employer-subsidized health insurance, 401k matching, paid time off, and professional development budgets. These benefits can add 25 to 40% on top of the base salary in total compensation value.

The FLSA distinguishes between "exempt" and "non-exempt" employees. Exempt employees (typically salaried) are not entitled to overtime pay. Non-exempt employees (typically hourly) must receive 1.5x pay for hours over 40. In 2024, the Department of Labor raised the salary threshold for overtime exemption to $58,656 per year. If you earn a salary below this amount, you may still be entitled to overtime.

From a lifestyle perspective, hourly work often has clearer boundaries. When your shift ends, you leave. Salaried roles frequently include implicit expectations of availability outside normal hours: checking email at night, joining calls early in the morning, or working weekends during crunch periods.

How Overtime Changes the Equation

Overtime pay at 1.5x rate can dramatically increase annual earnings. A $20/hour worker averaging just 5 overtime hours per week earns an extra $7,800 per year ($20 x 1.5 x 5 x 52), pushing their annual from $41,600 to $49,400. At 10 overtime hours per week, the total reaches $57,200, which is equivalent to a $27.50/hour straight-time rate.

Some industries (healthcare, manufacturing, oil and gas, construction) routinely involve significant overtime. Nurses earning $35/hour with 10 hours of weekly overtime earn $91,000 + $27,300 = $118,300 per year. That is substantially more than the $91,000 you would calculate from the base rate alone.

Double time (2x rate) applies in some situations. California law mandates double time for hours beyond 12 in a single day and for all hours worked on a 7th consecutive day in a workweek. Some union contracts also specify double time provisions. This calculator uses the federal standard of 1.5x, so adjust manually if double time applies to your situation.

overtime income is taxed at your marginal rate, which might be higher than your average rate. If your regular income puts you at the top of the 12% bracket and overtime pushes you into the 22% bracket, that overtime income is effectively taxed at 22% federal plus FICA plus state tax. You still come out ahead, but the net impact is less than the gross numbers suggest.

Using This Data to Negotiate Pay

When comparing job offers, convert everything to the same unit. If Offer A is $28/hour and Offer B is $55,000 salary, you compare apples to apples. $28/hour at 40 hours/52 weeks = $58,240. Already $3,240 more than Offer B, and that is before accounting for overtime eligibility.

But do not stop at the base number. Factor in benefits. If Offer B includes health insurance worth $7,200/year (what you would pay on the marketplace), a 4% 401k match worth $2,200, and 15 days of PTO worth $3,173 (15 days x daily rate), the total compensation is $67,573. Offer A at $58,240 with no benefits is suddenly the worse deal.

Know your market rate before negotiating. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics with median hourly and annual wages for every occupation by region. Glassdoor, Payscale, and Levels.fyi provide additional salary data. Walking into a negotiation knowing that the median rate for your role in your city is $32/hour gives you a factual anchor point.

If an employer offers a salary and you counter, converting it to an hourly rate can be a reframing tool. "This $50,000 salary works out to $24.04 per hour, and the market rate for this role in Austin is $28 to $32. I'd like to discuss closing that gap."

Cost of Living Considerations

$50,000 in Omaha, Nebraska provides a very different standard of living than $50,000 in Boston, Massachusetts. The cost of living index (with 100 being the national average) ranges from about 82 in Mississippi to 187 in Hawaii. New York City is around 187, San Francisco 179, Denver 128, and Dallas 103.

Housing is the biggest variable. Median rent for a one-bedroom apartment ranges from about $700 in many Midwestern and Southern cities to $3,500+ in San Francisco and Manhattan. The common guideline is keeping housing costs below 30% of gross income, but in expensive cities, many people spend 40% or more.

To make meaningful comparisons, search for cost of living calculators that adjust salary between cities. A $60,000 salary in Atlanta has roughly the same purchasing power as $95,000 in San Francisco. Keep this in mind when evaluating remote job offers or considering relocation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you convert hourly wage to annual salary?

Multiply your hourly rate by the number of hours you work per week, then multiply by 52 weeks. Annual Salary = Hourly Rate x Hours Per Week x 52. For a standard full-time schedule at $25 per hour: $25 x 40 x 52 = $52,000 per year before taxes. If you work fewer than 52 weeks (say you take 2 weeks unpaid vacation), replace 52 with 50 and the result becomes $50,000. For part-time workers doing 25 hours per week at $18/hour: $18 x 25 x 52 = $23,400. This basic formula gives you the gross (pre-tax) annual equivalent. The converter above also handles overtime and provides estimated after-tax figures.

Is $20 an hour a good wage?

At 40 hours per week and 52 weeks per year, $20 per hour equals $41,600 annually before taxes. Whether that qualifies as "good" depends almost entirely on where you live. In a city like Des Moines, Iowa (cost of living index 89), $41,600 goes further than the national average. In Boston (index 148), you would struggle to cover rent and basic expenses on that salary. The 2024 median household income in the US was about $80,600, so $41,600 is roughly half the median household level. For a single person without dependents in a moderate-cost area, $20/hour covers the basics with modest room for savings. For a household with children in an expensive metro, it would be very tight without a second income.

How much is $15 an hour annually?

$15 per hour at full-time (40 hours, 52 weeks) equals $31,200 per year gross. After federal income tax for a single filer (using 2025 brackets: 10% on the first $11,925, then 12% on the remainder), plus 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare, and assuming 5% state income tax, your take-home would be approximately $25,000 to $26,000 per year. That breaks down to roughly $2,083 per month gross or about $2,080 to $2,170 per month net. Biweekly gross paycheck: $1,200. $600 gross. This is the rate that many states have adopted as their minimum wage, and several major cities now require $15+ as the local minimum. The federal minimum remains $7.25 as of 2025.

Do salaried employees actually work 40 hours?

Most do not. Gallup's annual work survey consistently shows that full-time salaried employees in the US average 47 hours per week, with significant variation by industry. Roughly 25% of salaried workers report working 60+ hours per week. This means the "effective hourly rate" for many salaried workers is considerably lower than the number you get by dividing salary by 2,080 (40 hours x 52 weeks). A $70,000 salary at 40 hours/week gives an hourly rate of $33.65. At 47 hours/week, that drops to $28.66. At 55 hours, it is $24.48. Before accepting a salaried role, ask about expected hours, on-call requirements, and the team's typical schedule. Compare the effective hourly rate to what you would earn in an equivalent hourly position with overtime.

What taxes are taken out of hourly pay?

Several types of tax are withheld from each paycheck. Federal income tax is based on your W-4 form, which indicates your filing status and allowances. The 2025 brackets for single filers are 10% up to $11,925, 12% to $48,475, 22% to $103,350, 24% to $197,300, and higher brackets above. Social Security (OASDI) takes 6.2% of gross wages up to $176,100. Medicare takes 1.45% on all wages with no cap, plus an additional 0.9% on wages above $200,000 for single filers. State income tax varies wildly: seven states have no income tax at all (Texas, Florida, Washington, Nevada, Wyoming, South Dakota, Alaska), while California tops out at 13.3%. Some cities (New York City, Philadelphia, Detroit) add local income taxes on top. Total withholdings typically range from 20% to 35% of gross pay.

How do I calculate my take-home pay?

Start with your gross annual salary. First, subtract pre-tax deductions (401k contributions, health insurance premiums, HSA contributions, etc.) since these reduce your taxable income. Then calculate federal income tax using marginal brackets on the remaining taxable income. Subtract Social Security (6.2% up to the cap), Medicare (1.45%), and your state income tax rate. The result is your approximate net annual pay. Divide by 12 for monthly, by 26 for biweekly, or by 52 for weekly. As a rough estimate, most people take home between 65% and 80% of their gross salary. look at your most recent pay stub. The ratio of net pay to gross pay on that stub applies to your entire year if your situation is stable.

Is it better to be paid hourly or salary?

It depends on your role, industry, and personal priorities. Hourly pay is better when you regularly work overtime (you get paid 1.5x for those hours), when you value clear boundaries between work and personal time, and when you want direct compensation proportional to effort. Salaried pay is better when the role comes with substantial benefits (health insurance, retirement matching, equity), when income stability matters to you (same paycheck regardless of business conditions), and when career advancement depends on the role title and responsibilities more than hours logged. always calculate the total compensation of both options. A $55,000 salary with $12,000 in benefits and 3 weeks PTO is actually $67,000+ in value, which might beat a $30/hour job without benefits ($62,400 gross at 40 hrs).

How does overtime affect the hourly to salary conversion?

Overtime can add a surprising amount to annual income. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt workers must receive 1.5 times their regular rate for hours beyond 40 per week. Here is the math with a real example: at $22/hour with an average of 8 overtime hours per week, your calculation is: Regular pay = $22 x 40 x 52 = $45,760. Overtime pay = $22 x 1.5 x 8 x 52 = $13,728. Total annual = $59,488. That extra $13,728 represents a 30% income boost from overtime alone. Some workers in overtime-heavy fields (oil rigs, hospitals, warehouses) can earn 40-60% above their base rate equivalent. Always factor in overtime when comparing an hourly position to a salaried offer, because the salary would be significantly higher to match the hourly total.
