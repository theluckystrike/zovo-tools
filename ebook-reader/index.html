    How the EPUB Reader Works

    This EPUB reader processes ebook files entirely in your browser using pure JavaScript with no external libraries. When you upload an EPUB file, the reader parses the raw binary data of the ZIP archive, extracts the internal file structure, locates the OPF manifest, determines the correct reading order from the spine element, and renders each chapter as clean HTML in the reading pane.

    The ZIP parsing is implemented from scratch by reading ZIP local file headers and decompressing DEFLATE-compressed entries using a built-in inflate implementation. This means the reader has zero external dependencies while still being able to handle standard EPUB files produced by Calibre, Sigil, Adobe InDesign, Apple Books, and other publishing tools.

    Images embedded in the EPUB are extracted and converted to data URIs so they display inline within the chapter content. CSS stylesheets from the EPUB are stripped to ensure consistent rendering with the reader's own typography settings, though basic formatting like bold, italic, and headings is preserved.

    Understanding the EPUB Format

    An EPUB file is technically a ZIP archive with a .epub extension. Inside this archive you will find a specific structure defined by the EPUB specification (currently maintained by the W3C):

    • META-INF/container.xml — Points to the root OPF file that describes the book's structure.
    • content.opf (or similar) — The Open Packaging Format manifest that lists every file in the book and defines the spine (reading order).
    • toc.ncx or nav.xhtml — The table of contents file that maps human-readable chapter titles to content files.
    • XHTML files — The actual chapter content, written in XHTML (a stricter variant of HTML).
    • CSS, images, fonts — Supporting assets referenced by the XHTML content files.

    The OPF manifest's <spine> element is particularly important because it defines the linear reading order. Each <itemref> in the spine points to an item in the manifest by its ID, and the reader follows this sequence to present chapters in the correct order.

    Reading Features and Settings

    The reader provides several customization options to make your reading experience comfortable:

    • Font size: Adjustable from 12px to 32px. The default of 18px works well on most screens, but you can increase it for high-DPI displays or decrease it to fit more text on screen.
    • Font family: Choose between serif (Georgia, best for long-form reading), sans-serif (system default, clean and modern), or monospace (Courier New, for technical books).
    • Line height: Controls the vertical spacing between lines. Wider line spacing (1.8-2.0) reduces eye strain during extended reading sessions.
    • Themes: Three background options. White is traditional daylight reading. Sepia reduces blue light and feels warmer. Dark mode uses a dark background with light text, ideal for reading at night or in low-light environments.

    All settings are saved to localStorage and persist across sessions. Your reading preferences are restored automatically when you return to the reader.

    Chapter Navigation and Progress

    Navigation through the book is available in multiple ways. The sidebar table of contents shows all chapters extracted from the EPUB's NCX or navigation document. Click any chapter title to jump directly to it. The Previous and Next buttons at the bottom of each chapter let you move linearly through the book.

    The progress bar at the top of the reader and the percentage indicator show how far through the book you have read, calculated from your current chapter position relative to the total number of chapters. This gives you a quick sense of your progress through the entire book.

    Your reading position (current chapter) is saved to localStorage using the book's title as a key. When you reopen the same EPUB file, the reader jumps back to the chapter where you left off. This works reliably as long as you don't clear your browser's local storage.

    Keyboard Shortcuts

    The reader supports keyboard shortcuts for quick navigation and adjustments:

    • Left Arrow or Page Up — Go to the previous chapter
    • Right Arrow or Page Down — Go to the next chapter
    • + (Plus) or = (Equals) — Increase font size
    • - (Minus) — Decrease font size
    • Escape — Close the reader and return to the page

    These shortcuts work when the reader view is open. They are designed to let you navigate and adjust settings without taking your hands off the keyboard, which is especially convenient when reading on a laptop.

    Tips for the Best Reading Experience

    To get the most out of this browser-based ebook reader, consider these practical suggestions:

    • Use full screen (F11): Entering your browser's full screen mode removes the address bar and tabs, giving you a distraction-free reading environment similar to a dedicated ebook reader.
    • Match theme to lighting: Use the white theme in bright daylight, sepia in warm indoor lighting, and dark mode at night. Matching the screen brightness to your environment reduces eye fatigue.
    • Set comfortable font size: If you find yourself leaning toward the screen, increase the font size. For extended reading, slightly larger text (20-22px) is less tiring than the default.
    • Use a serif font for fiction: Research consistently shows that serif fonts like Georgia improve readability in long-form text. The serifs create a visual flow that guides the eye along each line.
    • DRM-free books only: This reader works with standard, unencrypted EPUB files. Books purchased from stores with DRM (like Adobe DRM) cannot be read. Many free ebooks from Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks work perfectly.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is this EPUB reader free?

    Yes, it is completely free with no usage limits, no accounts required, and no subscriptions. The reader runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript, so there are no server costs and nothing to pay for.

    Are my ebook files uploaded to a server?

    No. Your EPUB files are parsed and rendered entirely within your browser. The file data never leaves your device. No information about your reading habits or book contents is transmitted anywhere.

    What EPUB versions are supported?

    This reader supports both EPUB 2 and EPUB 3 files. It parses the OPF manifest, reads the spine for chapter ordering, and renders XHTML content documents. Most standard EPUB files from Calibre, Sigil, and public domain sources work correctly. DRM-protected files are not supported.

    Does it remember my reading position?

    Yes. The current chapter index is saved to your browser's localStorage whenever you navigate between chapters. When you reopen the same EPUB file (identified by its title), the reader returns to the last chapter you were reading.

    Can I read in dark mode?

    Yes. The reader includes three themes: white, sepia, and dark. The dark theme uses a comfortable dark background (#0f0f18) with light text that reduces eye strain during nighttime reading. Your theme preference is saved and restored automatically.
