How Credit Card Consolidation Works

Credit card consolidation involves taking out a single personal loan to pay off multiple high-interest credit card balances. The goal is to secure a lower overall interest rate, which reduces both your monthly payment and total interest paid over the life of the debt.

Step 1: Add up your current credit card balances and note each card's APR and minimum payment.

Step 2: Shop for a personal loan or balance transfer card with a lower interest rate than your weighted average APR.

Step 3: Use this calculator to compare your current total payments against the consolidation loan to see your potential savings.

Step 4: Factor in any origination fees or balance transfer fees, which this tool automatically includes in the comparison.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is credit card consolidation?
Credit card consolidation combines multiple credit card balances into a single loan, typically at a lower interest rate. This can reduce your monthly payments and total interest paid over time. Common consolidation options include personal loans, balance transfer credit cards, and home equity loans.
How much can I save by consolidating credit card debt?
Savings depend on your current interest rates, balances, and the consolidation loan terms. Most people save between 5-15% on total interest by consolidating high-rate credit cards (typically 18-25% APR) into a lower-rate personal loan (typically 6-12% APR). Use this calculator to see your specific savings estimate.
Does consolidating credit cards hurt my credit score?
Initially, applying for a consolidation loan may cause a small, temporary dip in your credit score due to the hard inquiry. However, consolidation often improves your score over time by reducing your credit utilization ratio and establishing a consistent payment history on the new loan.
What interest rate can I expect on a consolidation loan?
Consolidation loan rates typically range from 6% to 20% APR, depending on your credit score, income, and debt-to-income ratio. Borrowers with excellent credit (750+) often qualify for rates between 6-9%, while those with good credit (700-749) may see rates between 9-14%.
