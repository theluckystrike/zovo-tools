How Kelvin to Celsius Works

The Kelvin and Celsius scales share the same degree increment. A one-degree change in Celsius equals exactly a one-degree change in Kelvin. The only difference is their zero point. The Celsius scale anchors 0°C at the freezing point of water, while the Kelvin scale anchors 0 K at absolute zero, the lowest temperature physically possible.

This makes the conversion formula remarkably simple. To convert from Kelvin to Celsius, subtract 273.15. To go the other direction, add 273.15. There is no multiplication or division involved, unlike conversions between Fahrenheit and the other scales.

The number 273.15 is the precise offset between the two zero points. Absolute zero was experimentally determined to be -273.15°C through gas thermometry and thermodynamic calculations. When Lord Kelvin proposed his absolute temperature scale in 1848, he anchored it at this point while preserving the Celsius degree size.

The Kelvin scale is the SI base unit of temperature and is used across all scientific disciplines. It is essential in physics for calculations involving the ideal gas law (PV = nRT), blackbody radiation (Planck's law), and the Boltzmann distribution. In these equations, temperature must be absolute rather than relative, which is why Kelvin rather than Celsius is required.

For Fahrenheit equivalents, the conversion from Kelvin is °F = (K - 273.15) × 9/5 + 32, or equivalently °F = K × 9/5 - 459.67. This tool calculates Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Rankine simultaneously so you never have to chain conversions manually.

A practical note on scientific notation: when working with extremely high temperatures such as stellar interiors (millions of Kelvin) or plasma physics, this converter supports scientific notation input. Type values like "1.5e7" or "5.778e3" and the tool handles them correctly, displaying results in scientific notation when the magnitude exceeds six digits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Subtract 273.15 from the Kelvin value. The formula is °C = K - 273.15. For example, 373.15 K equals 373.15 - 273.15 = 100°C, which is the boiling point of water at standard atmospheric pressure.

Add 273.15 to the Celsius value. The formula is K = °C + 273.15. For example, 25°C equals 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K. This works because both scales use the same degree size, differing only in their zero point.

Absolute zero is 0 K, which equals -273.15°C or -459.67°F. It represents the lowest possible temperature, where all thermal molecular motion ceases. No object can ever reach absolute zero, though scientists have cooled atoms to within billionths of a degree above it.

Scientists use Kelvin because it is an absolute scale starting at absolute zero. This eliminates negative values in most thermodynamic calculations, simplifying equations like the ideal gas law (PV = nRT) and Planck's radiation law. Kelvin is also the SI base unit of temperature, making it the international standard for scientific measurement.
