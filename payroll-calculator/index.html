Understanding Payroll Calculations

Payroll processing involves multiple layers of calculations that transform gross compensation into the net pay an employee actually receives. Each paycheck undergoes federal income tax withholding, state income tax withholding (in most states), FICA contributions for Social Security and Medicare, and various voluntary deductions. Understanding these components helps both employers and employees verify that paychecks are accurate and plan their finances effectively.

This payroll calculator handles the full calculation pipeline: converting between annual salary and per-period pay, applying pre-tax deductions to reduce taxable income, computing federal and state income tax withholdings using current bracket rates, calculating FICA contributions with proper caps and thresholds, subtracting post-tax deductions, and producing a detailed pay stub breakdown. All processing runs entirely in your browser with no data transmitted to any external server.

Federal Income Tax Brackets (2026)

The United States uses a progressive tax system where different portions of your income are taxed at different rates. For the 2026 tax year, the brackets for single filers range from 10% on the first $11,925 of taxable income up to 37% on income exceeding $626,350. Married filing jointly filers benefit from wider brackets, with the 10% bracket extending to $23,850 and the top 37% bracket starting at $751,600. Head of household filers fall between these ranges.

A common misconception is that moving into a higher tax bracket means all your income is taxed at the higher rate. In reality, only the income within each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. For example, a single filer earning $50,000 pays 10% on the first $11,925, 12% on income from $11,925 to $48,475, and 22% only on the remaining $1,525. This marginal system ensures that earning more always results in higher take-home pay.

FICA Social Security and Medicare

The Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) requires both employees and employers to contribute to Social Security and Medicare. Employees pay 6.2% of their wages toward Social Security, but only on wages up to the annual wage base limit of $168,600 for 2026. Once your year-to-date earnings exceed this cap, no additional Social Security tax is withheld for the remainder of the year.

Medicare tax is calculated at 1.45% of all wages with no cap., the Affordable Care Act introduced an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% on wages exceeding $200,000 for single filers ($250,000 for married filing jointly). This additional tax is only paid by employees and is not matched by the employer. Combined, FICA taxes represent 7.65% of most workers' gross pay, making it the second-largest payroll deduction after federal income tax for many earners.

State Income Tax Considerations

State income tax systems vary significantly across the United States. Nine states impose no state income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Among the states that do tax income, approaches differ widely. Some states use a flat tax rate applied uniformly to all income, while others use progressive bracket systems similar to the federal model.

This calculator uses simplified effective rates for each state to provide a reasonable estimate. Actual state tax liability may vary based on specific state deductions, credits, local taxes, and individual circumstances. For precise state tax calculations, consult your state's department of revenue or a qualified tax professional. The calculator is give employees a useful approximation for budgeting and financial planning purposes.

Pre-Tax vs. Post-Tax Deductions

Pre-tax deductions reduce your taxable income before federal and state income taxes are calculated. Common pre-tax deductions include traditional 401(k) contributions, health savings account (HSA) contributions, and employer-sponsored health insurance premiums. By reducing your taxable income, pre-tax deductions effectively lower your tax burden in the current year, though the funds may be taxed when eventually withdrawn (as with 401(k) distributions in retirement).

Post-tax deductions are taken from your pay after all taxes have been calculated and withheld. Roth 401(k) contributions, Roth IRA contributions, and certain insurance premiums fall into this category. While post-tax deductions do not reduce your current tax liability, they offer the advantage of tax-free growth and withdrawals in the case of Roth retirement accounts. The decision between pre-tax and post-tax savings depends on your current tax rate, expected future tax rate, and overall financial strategy.

Pay Frequency and Its Impact

The frequency at which you receive paychecks affects both the per-period amount and how tax brackets are applied. Weekly pay (52 periods per year) results in the smallest individual paychecks but the most frequent payments. Biweekly pay (26 periods) is the most common schedule in the United States. Semi-monthly pay (24 periods) pays on fixed dates such as the 1st and 15th. Monthly pay (12 periods) produces the largest individual paychecks.

Tax withholding calculations annualize your per-period income to determine the appropriate tax bracket, then divide the annual tax back into per-period amounts. This means the per-period withholding rate is the same regardless of frequency, but rounding differences may cause slight variations in annual totals. For hourly workers, pay frequency also affects how overtime is tracked and calculated within each pay period.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is federal income tax calculated?▾

Federal income tax uses 2026 marginal tax brackets. Your taxable income per pay period (gross pay minus pre-tax deductions) is annualized, then each portion of income falling within a bracket range is taxed at that bracket's rate. The rates range from 10% on the lowest bracket up to 37% on income exceeding the highest threshold. The total annual tax is then divided back into your pay period amount.

What is FICA and how much is withheld?▾

FICA stands for the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. It includes Social Security tax at 6.2% of wages up to $168,600 per year and Medicare tax at 1.45% with no wage cap. An additional 0.9% Medicare surtax applies to wages exceeding $200,000 for single filers. Combined, most employees pay 7.65% of their gross wages in FICA taxes.

Does this calculator support all 50 states?▾

Yes. The calculator includes tax rates for all 50 US states plus the District of Columbia. States without income tax (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming) show zero state withholding. States with income tax use simplified effective rates for estimation purposes.

How are pre-tax deductions handled?▾

Pre-tax deductions such as traditional 401(k) contributions, HSA contributions, and health insurance premiums are subtracted from gross pay before federal and state income taxes are calculated. This reduces your taxable income and lowers your overall tax withholding for the pay period. FICA taxes are still calculated on gross pay before most pre-tax deductions.

What pay frequencies are supported?▾

Four pay frequencies are supported: weekly (52 pay periods per year), biweekly (26 pay periods), semi-monthly (24 pay periods), and monthly (12 pay periods). The calculator annualizes your per-period income for tax bracket calculations, then divides back to per-period amounts, ensuring accurate withholding regardless of frequency.

How is overtime calculated?▾

The overtime calculator follows Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) guidelines. Regular hours are the first 40 hours worked in a week at your standard hourly rate. Overtime hours (anything beyond 40) are compensated at 1.5 times your regular hourly rate. The calculator shows regular pay, overtime pay, and total gross pay separately.

Is my salary data sent to a server?▾

No. All payroll calculations run entirely in your web browser using JavaScript. No salary figures, tax information, deduction amounts, or any other data are transmitted to any server. You can verify this by disconnecting from the internet and confirming the calculator still works. Your financial information stays completely private on your device.

Can I export my pay stub?▾

Yes. After running a payroll calculation, switch to the Pay Stub tab to see a formatted breakdown of your paycheck. You can export this as a plain text file or copy it to your clipboard. The pay stub includes gross pay, each pre-tax deduction, federal tax, state tax, FICA breakdown, post-tax deductions, and final net pay.
