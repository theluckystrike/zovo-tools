How to Use This Plagiarism Checker

This plagiarism checker helps you compare two pieces of text to identify overlapping or duplicate content. Unlike web-based plagiarism detectors that search the internet, this tool performs all analysis directly in your browser using sentence fingerprinting and n-gram matching algorithms. This means your text is never uploaded, stored, or seen by anyone else.

The tool is designed for writers, students, educators, and content creators who want to verify that two documents are sufficiently different from each other. Common use cases include checking a rewritten article against the original, comparing student submissions with each other, and verifying that translated content has not been copied from existing translations.

Understanding the Comparison Mode

In the default comparison mode, you paste two texts side by side. The "Source" text is treated as the original or reference, while the "Text to Check" is analyzed against it. After clicking "Check Similarity," the tool breaks both texts into sentences and compares them using multiple similarity metrics.

The tool calculates a similarity score based on how many sentences in the checked text closely match sentences in the source. Each sentence is converted into a set of n-grams (sequences of consecutive words), and the Jaccard similarity coefficient is computed between every pair of sentences. When the similarity between two sentences exceeds a threshold, they are flagged as potential matches.

What the Scores Mean

The results include several key metrics:

  • Similarity: the percentage of the checked text that overlaps with the source. Higher values indicate more shared content.
  • Originality: the inverse of similarity, showing the percentage of content that appears to be unique.
  • Matching Sentences: the raw count of sentences in the checked text that have a high similarity match in the source.
  • Word Counts: the total number of words in each text, useful for comparing document lengths.

Understanding the Single Text Mode

The single text analysis mode examines one text for internal patterns. It counts words, sentences, and paragraphs, calculates the average sentence length, and detects repeated phrases within the document. While this mode cannot detect plagiarism from external sources, it is useful for identifying self-repetition and assessing text structure.

Repeated phrases of three or more words that appear multiple times are listed with their frequency. This can help you identify unintentional repetition or template-like writing patterns that reduce content quality.

How Sentence Fingerprinting Works

Sentence fingerprinting is a technique used to create a compact representation of a sentence that can be quickly compared with other sentence fingerprints. The process involves several steps:

  1. Normalize the text by converting to lowercase and removing punctuation
  2. Split the sentence into individual words (tokens)
  3. Generate n-grams from the token sequence (typically 3-grams and 4-grams)
  4. Create a hash or set representation of these n-grams
  5. Compare the n-gram sets between sentences using set intersection metrics

This approach is robust against minor word changes and reordering while still detecting when substantial portions of a sentence have been copied. The Jaccard coefficient, defined as the size of the intersection divided by the size of the union of two sets, provides a normalized similarity score between 0 and 1.

N-Gram Matching Explained

An n-gram is a contiguous sequence of n items from a text. For plagiarism detection, word-level n-grams are most useful. For example, the sentence "The quick brown fox" contains the following 3-grams: "the quick brown" and "quick brown fox." By comparing the n-gram sets of two texts, you can identify shared phrases even when surrounding content differs.

This tool uses a combination of 3-grams and 4-grams, which provides a good balance between sensitivity (detecting short matching phrases) and specificity (avoiding false positives from common word combinations). Very short n-grams like bigrams produce too many coincidental matches, while very long n-grams miss partially modified passages.

Limitations of Client-Side Plagiarism Detection

It is important to understand what this tool can and cannot do. Since all processing happens in your browser, the tool can only compare texts you provide. It does not crawl the web, search academic databases, or access any external content repositories. This means:

  • It can find matching content between two specific documents you provide
  • It cannot determine if a text was copied from an online source
  • It cannot access paywalled academic papers or proprietary content
  • It works best for direct comparison scenarios like checking rewrites or comparing submissions

For comprehensive plagiarism screening against published content, professional services with web crawling capabilities are necessary. However, for quick side-by-side comparisons, self-checking, and educational purposes, this local tool is fast, free, and completely private.

Tips for Reducing Text Similarity

If you find that your text has high similarity with a source, here are strategies for creating more original content:

  • Rephrase sentences using your own words and sentence structures
  • Change the order in which ideas are presented
  • Add your own analysis, examples, and perspectives
  • Use different vocabulary while preserving the meaning
  • Combine information from multiple sources rather than relying on one
  • Always cite your sources properly when using someone else's ideas

The goal is not to trick plagiarism detectors but to genuinely understand and restate information in your own voice. Good writing demonstrates comprehension, not just rearrangement of words.

Use Cases for Text Comparison

Beyond academic integrity, text comparison has many practical applications. Content marketers use it to ensure their articles are distinct from competitor content. Legal professionals compare contracts and documents for similarities and differences. Publishers check submitted manuscripts against existing works. Translators verify that their work is original rather than copied from existing translations. SEO professionals check for duplicate content that could harm search rankings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this plagiarism checker work?
This tool uses client-side text analysis including sentence fingerprinting, n-gram matching, and cosine similarity to compare texts. It does NOT search the web. Instead, it compares two texts you provide to find duplicate or similar passages between them.
Does it search the internet for plagiarism?
No. This is a local text comparison tool. It compares two texts you paste in and identifies similar or identical passages between them. For web-based plagiarism detection, you would need a service with web crawling capabilities.
Is my text data private?
Yes, completely private. All analysis runs in your browser using JavaScript. Your text is never sent to any server, stored anywhere, or shared with anyone. Close the tab and your data is gone.
What does the originality score mean?
The originality score represents the percentage of text that appears to be unique when compared to the reference text. A 100% score means no similar passages were found. Lower scores indicate more shared content between the two texts.
Can I check a single text for plagiarism?
The single text mode analyzes your text for internal consistency, repeated phrases, and provides readability statistics. For meaningful plagiarism detection, use the comparison mode with two texts.
How accurate is the similarity detection?
The tool uses multiple algorithms including n-gram analysis and sentence fingerprinting. It reliably detects exact matches and close paraphrases. However, it cannot detect conceptual similarity or heavily rewritten content the way a human reviewer could.
Is there a word limit?
There is no hard limit since all processing happens in your browser. However, very long texts (over 50,000 words) may cause slower processing depending on your device. For best performance, check texts in segments.
Can I use this for academic work?
This tool is useful for self-checking before submission. However, since it only compares two texts you provide (not the entire internet), it should be used alongside other verification methods for academic integrity purposes.
