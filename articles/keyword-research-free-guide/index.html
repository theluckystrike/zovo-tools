How to Do Keyword Research for Free in 2026

Keyword research is the foundation of every successful content strategy, SEO campaign, and paid search effort. The problem is that most keyword research guides push expensive tools as requirements. Ahrefs costs $99 per month. Semrush starts at $129.95 per month. Moz Pro is $99 per month. For individuals, small businesses, and anyone just starting out, spending over $1,000 per year on keyword tools is not realistic.

The good news is that free keyword research in 2026 is more powerful than paid tools were just five years ago. Google itself gives away enormous amounts of keyword data through Autocomplete, People Also Ask, Search Console, and Keyword Planner. Combined with systematic methods and free tools like the Google Keyword Planner tool, you can build a keyword strategy that competes with anyone.

This guide walks through every free method available, explains the theory behind keyword selection, and gives you a repeatable process for finding keywords that drive traffic.

Understanding Search Intent Before Keywords

Before looking at any keyword data, you need to understand search intent. Search intent is the reason behind a search query. Google has gotten extremely good at identifying intent, and it ranks pages that match intent above pages that merely contain the keyword.

There are four primary types of search intent.

Informational intent means the searcher wants to learn something. Examples include "how to change a tire," "what causes inflation," and "symptoms of vitamin D deficiency." The best content format for informational queries is detailed articles, guides, tutorials, and explainers. Roughly 80% of all search queries are informational.

Navigational intent means the searcher wants to find a specific website or page. Examples include "Gmail login," "YouTube," and "Amazon customer service number." These queries have a clear destination in mind, and unless your site is that destination, you will not rank for them.

Transactional intent means the searcher wants to complete an action, usually a purchase. Examples include "buy running shoes online," "order pizza delivery," and "subscribe to Netflix." Product pages, pricing pages, and checkout flows match transactional intent.

Commercial investigation intent means the searcher is comparing options before making a decision. Examples include "best laptops under $1000," "iPhone vs Samsung 2026," and "top CRM software for small business." Comparison articles, review roundups, and buying guides match this intent.

To identify the intent behind any keyword, simply search for it on Google and look at what ranks on page one. If the top results are all blog posts, the intent is informational. If they are all product pages, the intent is transactional. Google has already done the intent classification for you through its ranking algorithm.

Mining Google Autocomplete for Keywords

Google Autocomplete is a goldmine of keyword ideas because it shows you actual queries that real people type into Google. The suggestions are based on search frequency, freshness, and your location. Every suggestion represents genuine search demand.

The basic technique is to type your seed keyword followed by each letter of the alphabet. For example, start with "keyword research" and then try "keyword research a," "keyword research b," "keyword research c," and so on. Each letter reveals different completions that people actually search for.

"keyword research"

"keyword research a" reveals: keyword research api, keyword research analysis, keyword research automation

"keyword research b" reveals: keyword research beginners, keyword research blog, keyword research best practices

"keyword research f" reveals: keyword research free, keyword research for youtube, keyword research for etsy

"keyword research t" reveals: keyword research template, keyword research tools free, keyword research tutorial

You can extend this by adding modifiers before your seed keyword. Try "how to [keyword]," "best [keyword]," "[keyword] for beginners," "[keyword] vs," and "[keyword] 2026." Each combination produces a different set of autocomplete suggestions.

For local businesses, add location modifiers. "Plumber [city name]," "best restaurants in [neighborhood]," and "[service] near [landmark]" all reveal locally relevant keywords with clear commercial intent.

Record every relevant suggestion in a spreadsheet. After mining autocomplete for 20 to 30 seed keywords with the alphabet technique and modifier variations, you will have hundreds of keyword ideas. This process takes 1 to 2 hours and costs nothing.

Using People Also Ask to Find Questions

People Also Ask (PAA) boxes appear in roughly 85% of Google search results. Each PAA box contains 4 to 6 questions related to the search query, and clicking any question expands to show more questions. This creates an effectively infinite source of question-based keywords.

The strategic value of PAA questions is threefold. First, they reveal the exact questions your audience is asking, which tells you what to cover in your content. Second, ranking in a PAA box gives your page a prominent position on page one even if your organic ranking is lower. Third, PAA questions often represent long-tail keywords with low competition that are perfect for new or small sites.

To systematically mine PAA data, search for your main keyword and note all the PAA questions. Click each question to expand more questions. Continue clicking until you start seeing repeated questions, which usually happens after 15 to 20 expansions. Each question you find is a potential subheading, FAQ entry, or standalone article topic.

Each of those questions has search volume, has confirmed user intent, and can be answered in your content. If you create a complete article that directly answers all of them with clear, specific information, you have a strong chance of ranking for the parent keyword and appearing in PAA boxes for the related questions.

Google Search Console as a Keyword Intelligence Tool

If you have an existing website, Google Search Console is the most underrated free keyword research tool available. It shows you the exact queries that triggered impressions and clicks for your site, along with average position, click-through rate, and impression count.

The most valuable data in Search Console is the queries where your site appears on page 2 or page 3 (positions 11 to 30). These are keywords where Google already considers your site relevant enough to rank, but you are not yet on page one. These represent your lowest-effort, highest-reward opportunities because small improvements in content quality, on-page optimization, or internal linking can push them to page one where the majority of clicks happen.

To find these opportunities, open Search Console, go to Performance, filter by position (average position greater than 10 and less than 30), and sort by impressions descending. The queries at the top of this list have the most search volume and you are already almost ranking for them.

Another valuable Search Console analysis is finding your "money keywords" by filtering for queries with high impressions but low click-through rate. These are keywords where you rank on page one but are not getting clicks. The fix is usually improving your title tag and meta description to be more compelling, which is a 5-minute change that can double your traffic from that keyword.

Google Keyword Planner Without Running Ads

Google Keyword Planner is free to use with any Google Ads account. You do not need to run any ads or spend any money. Simply create a Google Ads account, skip the campaign setup, and navigate to Tools and Settings then Keyword Planner.

The tool has two main functions. "Discover new keywords" takes a seed keyword or URL and returns a list of related keywords with search volume ranges, competition level, and suggested bid prices. "Get search volume and forecasts" takes a list of keywords you already have and returns volume data for each one.

The limitation of the free version is that search volumes are shown as ranges (like 1K-10K) rather than exact numbers. This is sufficient for most keyword research because you are comparing relative volume between keywords, not making decisions based on the difference between 3,200 and 3,800 monthly searches.

The competition metric in Keyword Planner refers to advertising competition, not organic search competition. High competition means many advertisers bid on that keyword, which correlates with commercial value. A keyword with low search volume but high competition and high suggested bid is likely very profitable for the businesses ranking for it.

Long-Tail vs Short-Tail Keyword Strategy

The terms "long-tail" and "short-tail" describe where keywords fall on the search demand curve. Short-tail keywords (also called head terms) are broad, 1 to 2 word phrases with very high search volume and very high competition. Long-tail keywords are specific, 3+ word phrases with lower individual volume but much less competition.

The search demand curve follows a power law distribution. The top 100 most-searched keywords in any niche account for roughly 20% of total search volume. The remaining 80% is distributed across thousands of long-tail variations. This is why long-tail strategy works: there are so many more opportunities with less competition.

For new websites or sites with low domain authority, long-tail keywords are the only realistic path to organic traffic. Trying to rank for "running shoes" against Nike, Amazon, and major retailers is futile. But "best running shoes for wide feet and high arches" has specific intent, lower competition, and converts at a higher rate because the searcher knows exactly what they want.

The ideal approach is to build a content hub. Create a complete pillar page targeting the broader keyword, then create supporting articles targeting long-tail variations that all link back to the pillar. Over time, the accumulated authority from the long-tail articles strengthens the pillar page's ability to compete for the head term.

Finding Zero-Competition Keywords

Zero-competition keywords are queries where no high-quality, dedicated content exists. They represent the easiest wins in SEO because you can rank on page one simply by creating the first good page on the topic.

The process for finding zero-competition keywords involves searching Google for your candidate keyword and evaluating the search results page. Look for these signals that a keyword is underserved:

The top results are forum posts, Quora answers, or Reddit threads. This means no website has created a dedicated page for this query, so Google is serving user-generated content as the best available option.

The top results do not have the keyword in their title tags. This means no one has specifically improved for this query. A page with the exact query in its title will likely outrank these results.

The top results are from low-authority sites (no recognizable brands). This means you do not need high domain authority to compete.

Google shows a featured snippet that pulls from a tangentially related page rather than a directly relevant one. This means Google wants to show a direct answer but cannot find one.

Competitor Keyword Analysis for Free

You do not need paid tools to analyze competitor keywords. The simplest method is manual SERP analysis. Identify your top 3 to 5 competitors by searching for your main keywords and noting which sites appear most frequently. Then visit each competitor's site and study their content structure.

Look at their blog or resource section. What topics do they cover? What are their article titles? How do they organize content into categories? Each title represents a keyword they are targeting. Each category represents a topic cluster they have identified as important.

Check their sitemap (usually at domain.com/sitemap.xml) for a complete list of their pages. Many sitemaps include last-modified dates, which tells you what content they have recently created or updated.

Use Google's site search operator to find all of a competitor's pages indexed for a specific topic. Search for "site:competitor.com keyword" to see every page on their site that Google associates with that keyword. This reveals their content depth on any topic.

For a more systematic approach, take each of your candidate keywords and note which competitors rank in the top 10. If three out of five competitors rank for a keyword, it is clearly a valuable term in your niche. If none of them rank, either the keyword is not relevant to your industry or it is an untapped opportunity.

Keyword Clustering and Content Planning

Keyword clustering is the process of grouping related keywords that should be targeted by a single page rather than separate pages. Without clustering, you risk keyword cannibalization, where multiple pages on your own site compete against each other for the same query.

The test for whether two keywords belong in the same cluster is simple: search for both on Google and compare the top 10 results. If the search results share 5 or more URLs in common, the two keywords should be on the same page. Google is telling you it considers them the same topic. If the results are completely different, the keywords need separate pages.

Keywords to cluster test:

"how to meal prep" and "meal prep for beginners"

SERP overlap: 7 of 10 results are the same URLs

Same cluster. One page targets both.

"meal prep recipes" and "meal prep containers"

SERP overlap: 0 of 10 results are the same

Different clusters. Separate pages needed.

To build clusters at scale, start with your full list of keywords from autocomplete, PAA, Search Console, and competitor analysis. Group them by topic area first, then refine by checking SERP overlap for keywords that seem similar. The end result is a set of content briefs, each targeting a cluster of related keywords with one primary keyword (the one with the highest volume) and several secondary keywords.

Content Gap Analysis Without Paid Tools

Content gap analysis identifies topics that your competitors cover but you do not. These are proven opportunities because someone else has already validated the demand.

The free method involves three steps. First, list every topic or page your top 3 competitors have published. You can do this by browsing their blog archives, checking their sitemap, or using Google's "site:" operator with broad topic keywords. Second, list every topic you have covered on your own site. Third, compare the two lists. Topics that appear in the competitor list but not yours are your content gaps.

Prioritize gaps by estimated search volume and relevance to your business. A gap in a high-volume, high-intent topic area is more valuable than a gap in a tangential, low-volume topic.

Google Search Console provides another angle on content gaps. Filter for queries where your site has impressions but no clicks and an average position of 20 or higher. These are topics where Google sees some relevance in your site but you do not have dedicated content. Creating targeted content for these queries can quickly move you from page 3 to page 1.

Estimating Search Volume Without Premium Tools

Exact search volume numbers are less important than relative volume comparisons. You do not need to know that a keyword gets exactly 4,200 searches per month. You need to know whether it gets significantly more or less traffic than alternative keywords you could target.

Google Keyword Planner provides volume ranges (like 100-1K, 1K-10K, 10K-100K) for free. These ranges are sufficient for prioritization. A keyword in the 10K-100K range is clearly worth more effort than one in the 100-1K range.

Google Trends lets you compare the relative popularity of up to five keywords simultaneously. While it does not show absolute numbers, it shows you which keyword has more search demand and whether demand is growing, stable, or declining. A keyword trending upward is more valuable than a higher-volume keyword that is declining.

Another free estimation method is using the suggested bid price from Keyword Planner as a proxy for value. A keyword with a $15 suggested bid is driving significant revenue for someone, even if the search volume seems modest. Keywords with high bids and moderate volume often represent the best opportunities because the traffic they deliver is highly valuable.

Building a Keyword Research Workflow

A repeatable workflow ensures consistent results every time you do keyword research. Here is a step-by-step process you can follow for any topic or niche.

Step 1. Start with 5 to 10 seed keywords that describe your core topics. These should be the broadest terms related to your business or content area.

Step 2. Run each seed keyword through Google Autocomplete using the alphabet technique. Record every relevant suggestion. Estimated time: 45 minutes for 10 seed keywords.

Step 3. Search each seed keyword on Google and mine the People Also Ask boxes. Click to expand and record all relevant questions. Estimated time: 30 minutes.

Step 4. Check Google Search Console for queries you are already ranking for (positions 11 to 30 and positions 1 to 10 with low CTR). Estimated time: 20 minutes.

Step 5. Enter your collected keywords into Google Keyword Planner to get volume ranges and discover additional related keywords. Estimated time: 20 minutes.

Step 6. Cluster your keywords by checking SERP overlap. Group keywords that share similar search results into clusters. Estimated time: 45 minutes.

Step 7. Evaluate competition for each cluster by analyzing the page-one results. Prioritize clusters where you can realistically rank. Estimated time: 30 minutes.

Step 8. Create content briefs from your prioritized clusters, specifying the primary keyword, secondary keywords, search intent, target word count, and subheadings to include. Estimated time: 30 minutes.

The total time for this workflow is approximately 3.5 hours. The result is a prioritized content plan with dozens of validated keywords organized into clusters with clear intent mapping. This plan can guide content creation for months.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is keyword research and why does it matter?

Keyword research is the process of finding the words and phrases that people type into search engines when looking for information, products, or services. It matters because targeting the right keywords means your content appears in front of people who are actively searching for what you offer. Without keyword research, you are guessing what people want. With it, you are building content around proven demand. The difference between a page that gets 10 visits per month and one that gets 10,000 often comes down to whether the right keywords were targeted.

What is the difference between long-tail and short-tail keywords?

Short-tail keywords are broad terms with 1 to 2 words, like "running shoes" or "email marketing." They have high search volume but also high competition and vague intent. Long-tail keywords are more specific phrases with 3 or more words, like "best running shoes for flat feet 2026" or "email marketing for small restaurants." They have lower search volume individually but much less competition and clearer intent, which means higher conversion rates. About 70% of all search queries are long-tail keywords.

Can I do keyword research without paying for tools?

Yes. Google itself is the most powerful free keyword research tool. Google Autocomplete, People Also Ask boxes, Related Searches at the bottom of results pages, and Google Trends all provide keyword data directly from Google. Google Search Console shows you keywords your site already ranks for. Google Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account (you do not need to run ads). Combining these sources with free tools gives you more keyword data than most paid tools provided five years ago.

What are the four types of search intent?

The four types are informational (the searcher wants to learn something, like "how to tie a tie"), navigational (the searcher wants to find a specific website, like "Facebook login"), transactional (the searcher wants to buy something, like "buy iPhone 16 Pro Max"), and commercial investigation (the searcher is comparing options before buying, like "best wireless earbuds under $100"). Matching your content type to search intent is critical. Google ranks pages that match intent, so a product page will not rank for an informational query no matter how well improved.

How do I find zero-competition keywords?

Look for queries where the top results are forum posts, Quora answers, Reddit threads, or thin content from low-authority sites. These indicate that no one has created a dedicated, high-quality page for that query. Use Google Autocomplete with specific modifiers like "how to," "best," years, and location names to find long-tail variations. Check Google's search results page directly. If you do not see any exact-match titles in the top 10, the query is underserved. Niche forums, customer support tickets, and comment sections are also sources of questions that no one has answered well.

What is keyword clustering?

Keyword clustering is the process of grouping related keywords that can be targeted by a single page. Instead of creating separate pages for "how to meal prep," "meal prep for beginners," and "weekly meal prep guide," you identify that Google shows similar results for all three queries and create one complete page targeting the entire cluster. Clustering prevents keyword cannibalization (where multiple pages on your site compete for the same queries) and helps you build more complete content that satisfies a broader range of related searches.

How do I estimate search volume without paid tools?

Google Keyword Planner provides search volume ranges (like 1K-10K) for free with a Google Ads account. Google Trends shows relative popularity over time and comparative volume between keywords. You can also infer volume from the number of Google Autocomplete suggestions (more suggestions generally means more search activity around that topic), the specificity of People Also Ask questions, and the number of ads showing for commercial queries (more ads typically means higher volume and commercial value).

How often should I do keyword research?

For established sites, a complete keyword research review every quarter keeps your content strategy aligned with changing search behavior. Monthly checks of Google Search Console data help you spot emerging keywords your site is starting to rank for. For new sites or new content verticals, do thorough keyword research before creating any content. Seasonal businesses should research 2 to 3 months before their peak season. Any time you notice traffic changes, revisit your keyword data to see if search behavior has shifted.

What is content gap analysis?

Content gap analysis identifies keywords that your competitors rank for but you do not. It reveals opportunities where demand exists (proven by competitor traffic) but you have not yet created content. To do it for free, search for your main topics and note which subtopics your competitors cover that you have not addressed. Check their site structure, navigation, and blog archives for topics missing from your site. Google Search Console also shows queries where you appear on page 2 or 3, which represents gaps where you have relevance but need better content to compete.
