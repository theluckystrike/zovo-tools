What Is ACS Citation Style

ACS citation style is the referencing format established and maintained by the American Chemical Society for use in chemistry journals, research papers, and academic publications. As the world's largest scientific society devoted to chemistry, the ACS publishes definitive guidelines in The ACS Style Guide that dictate how researchers should format citations, references, and bibliographies in their manuscripts.

The ACS style is distinguished by several key characteristics that set it apart from other academic citation formats. It uses a numbered reference system where sources are cited in the text using superscript numbers that correspond to entries in a numbered reference list. Journal titles are abbreviated according to the Chemical Abstracts Service Source Index (CASSI), author names are formatted with last name followed by initials without periods between the initials, and the year of publication appears after the journal abbreviation rather than immediately after the author names.

Understanding ACS citation style matters for anyone writing chemistry papers, lab reports, review articles, or dissertations in the chemical sciences. Proper citation formatting is not merely a stylistic preference but a professional requirement. Journals will reject manuscripts that do not follow their citation guidelines, and professors routinely deduct points for incorrect reference formatting in student papers. This generator handles the formatting rules automatically so you can focus on the substance of your research rather than the mechanics of citation.

The ACS Style Guide also covers broader aspects of scientific writing including grammar, nomenclature, and manuscript preparation. However, the citation and reference formatting rules are what most writers interact with on a daily basis, and they are the focus of this tool and guide.

Journal Article Format in ACS Style

Journal articles are the most common source type in chemistry writing, and formatting them correctly in ACS style requires attention to several specific conventions. The standard format for a journal article reference is:

ACS Journal Article Format:
Author 1; Author 2; Author 3. Article Title. J. Abbrev. Year, Volume, Pages. DOI.

Each component of this format follows specific rules. Author names are listed with the last name first, followed by a comma and the first and middle initials. No periods appear between initials, and each author is separated by a semicolon. The article title is written in sentence case (only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized) with a period at the end. The journal abbreviation is italicized and follows CASSI conventions. The year is bolded, followed by a comma and the italicized volume number, another comma, and the page range. The DOI, when available, appears at the end.

Example Journal Citations

A single-author journal article would appear as:

Smith, J A. Catalytic Asymmetric Synthesis of Chiral Amines. J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024, 146, 4521-4530.

A multi-author article with a DOI:

Chen, W; Park, S J; Lee, H K; Rodriguez, M A. Nanostructured Materials for Energy Storage Applications. Angew. Chem., Int. Ed. 2023, 62, e202301456. https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202301456.

Volume, Issue, and Page Numbers

In ACS style, the volume number is required and italicized. The issue number is generally omitted because the volume and page combination uniquely identifies the article. However, if the journal uses article numbers instead of page numbers (as many online-only journals do), include the article number in place of the page range. Some journals use both continuous pagination and article numbers; in these cases, either format is acceptable as long as you are consistent throughout your reference list.

Book and Book Chapter Format

Books and book chapters follow somewhat different conventions than journal articles in ACS style. The key distinction is between citing an entire book and citing a specific chapter within an edited volume.

Entire Book

ACS Book Format:
Author 1; Author 2. Book Title, Edition; Publisher: City, Year.

For books, the title is italicized (unlike journal article titles). The edition is included only for editions beyond the first. The publisher name and city are separated by a colon, and the year appears at the end. If the book has editors rather than authors, the editors' names are followed by a comma and "Eds." (or "Ed." for a single editor).

Book Chapter in an Edited Volume

ACS Book Chapter Format:
Chapter Author 1; Chapter Author 2. Chapter Title. In Book Title; Editor 1, Editor 2, Eds.; Publisher: City, Year; pp Page-Range.

When citing a specific chapter, the chapter author and title appear first, followed by "In" and the book title in italics. The editors are listed after the book title with "Eds." designation. Page numbers are preceded by "pp" (or "p" for a single page) and use an en dash between the start and end pages.

Examples

An entire book:

Clayden, J; Greeves, N; Warren, S. Organic Chemistry, 2nd ed.; Oxford University Press: Oxford, 2012.

A chapter in an edited volume:

Thompson, R B. Fluorescence Sensing. In Topics in Fluorescence Spectroscopy; Lakowicz, J R, Ed.; Springer: New York, 2005; pp 1-42.

Website and Patent Format

As digital resources become increasingly important in scientific research, knowing how to cite websites, databases, and patents in ACS format is essential. Each of these source types has distinct formatting requirements.

Website Citations

ACS Website Format:
Author or Organization. Title of Page. Site Name. URL (accessed Month Day, Year).

Website citations in ACS style include the author or sponsoring organization, the page title, the name of the website, the complete URL, and the access date in parentheses. The access date is important because web content can change or be removed at any time. If no author is available, begin with the page title or organization name. If no date of publication is available, the access date serves as the temporal reference for the source.

Patent Citations

ACS Patent Format:
Inventor 1; Inventor 2. Title of Patent. Patent Number, Date.

Patent citations follow a format similar to journal articles but replace the journal information with the patent number and date. The inventors are listed as authors, the patent title appears in sentence case, and the patent number includes the country designation (e.g., U.S. Patent, EP Patent, WO Patent). The date can be formatted as Month Day, Year or simply the year, depending on the level of detail available.

Database and Software Citations

For databases and software, ACS recommends citing the specific version or access point used. Include the developer or organization, the database or software name (in italics), the version number, the URL or publisher, and the access date. This ensures that readers can locate the same version of the resource you used in your research.

Author Name Formatting Rules

Correct author name formatting is one of the most frequent challenges in ACS citations. The rules are specific and differ from other citation styles in important ways.

Basic Name Format

In ACS style, author names are written with the last name first, followed by a comma and the first and middle initials. Unlike some other styles, ACS does not place periods between initials. For example, "John Andrew Smith" becomes "Smith, J A" rather than "Smith, J.A." or "Smith, J. A." This is a common source of errors for writers accustomed to other citation formats.

Multiple Authors

Authors are separated by semicolons, not commas (commas are used within each author's name to separate the last name from the initials). For references with up to 10 authors, list all of them. For works with more than 10 authors, list the first 10 followed by "et al." This rule exists to balance completeness with readability in references that have dozens of contributors, as is common in large collaborative research projects.

Special Cases

For authors with compound last names (e.g., "de la Cruz" or "van der Berg"), maintain the full last name as a unit. For authors with suffixes (Jr., III), place the suffix after the initials: "Smith, J A, Jr." For organizational authors, use the full organization name in place of individual author names.

Finding Correct Abbreviations

The CAS website provides a free CASSI search tool where you can look up the official abbreviation for any journal. When the full title consists of a single word (like "Biochemistry" or "Science"), the abbreviation is the same as the full title. Multi-word titles are abbreviated according to standard rules: common words like "Journal" become "J.", "Chemistry" becomes "Chem.", "International" becomes "Int.", and so on. Always verify abbreviations through CASSI rather than guessing, as incorrect abbreviations can make it difficult for readers to locate your sources.

In-Text Citation Methods

ACS style offers three acceptable methods for citing sources within the body of your text. The choice between them typically depends on journal requirements or instructor preferences, and you must use the same method consistently throughout your entire paper.

Superscript Numbers

The most common method uses superscript numbers placed after the relevant text and outside any punctuation marks. References are numbered in the order they first appear in the text. For example: "Recent studies have demonstrated improved catalytic efficiency.1 This finding contradicts earlier work by Thompson et al.2,3" When citing multiple sources at one point, separate the numbers with commas or use an en dash for a range: "Several groups have reported similar results.4-7"

Italic Numbers in Parentheses

The second method places reference numbers in parentheses with italicized numbers: "Recent studies have demonstrated improved catalytic efficiency (1). This approach is less common but is preferred by some ACS journals, particularly in fields where superscript numbers might be confused with exponents or other notations.

Author Name and Year

The third option uses the author's last name and year of publication in parentheses: "(Smith, 2024)." This method resembles APA style and is used by some ACS journals, though it is the least common of the three approaches. When using this method, the reference list is arranged alphabetically by first author rather than in order of appearance. For works with three or more authors, use the first author's name followed by "et al."

Common ACS Citation Mistakes

Even experienced researchers frequently make citation formatting errors. Recognizing and avoiding these common mistakes will help ensure your references are correct and consistent.

Periods Between Author Initials

Perhaps the most common error is placing periods between author initials. ACS style uses "Smith, J A" not "Smith, J.A." or "Smith, J. A." This differs from many other citation styles and catches many writers off guard. When this generator formats author names, it automatically removes periods from initials to comply with ACS conventions.

Using Full Journal Titles

Another frequent mistake is writing out full journal names instead of using CASSI abbreviations. "Journal of the American Chemical Society" should always appear as "J. Am. Chem. Soc." in ACS references. Similarly, do not invent your own abbreviations; always use the official CASSI abbreviation.

Incorrect Title Capitalization

Article titles in ACS references use sentence case, meaning only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized. Many writers mistakenly use title case (capitalizing all major words), which is incorrect for ACS style. Book titles, however, are italicized and use title case. This inconsistency between article and book title formatting is a common source of confusion.

Missing or Malformed DOIs

DOIs should be formatted as complete URLs (https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx) and placed at the end of the reference. Common errors include using "doi:" prefix instead of the URL format, omitting the DOI entirely when one is available, or including a DOI that does not resolve to the correct publication. Always verify that your DOI links to the intended source.

Inconsistent Formatting

Mixing citation formats within a single reference list is a serious error. If you begin using superscript numbers, maintain that system throughout. Similarly, ensure that all journal articles, all books, and all other source types follow consistent formatting patterns. Reviewers and instructors notice inconsistencies quickly, and they suggest a lack of attention to detail.

Reference List Formatting Rules

The reference list (also called the bibliography or works cited list) in ACS style follows specific organizational and formatting rules that differ from other citation styles.

Ordering References

When using the superscript or italic number citation methods, references in the list are numbered sequentially in the order they first appear in the text. This means the first source you cite is reference 1, the second new source is reference 2, and so on. If you cite reference 1 again later in the paper, it retains its original number. When using the author-date method, references are listed alphabetically by first author's last name.

Formatting the List

Each reference entry begins with its number followed by a period. The number may be superscript or normal text, depending on the journal's preferences. Entries should use a hanging indent format where the first line is flush with the left margin and subsequent lines are indented. Single-spacing is used within each entry, with a blank line or extra space between entries.

Handling Repeated Authors

Unlike some citation styles that use em dashes or "ibid." for repeated authors, ACS style requires the full author list for every reference entry. Even if the same author appears in consecutive references, spell out the complete name each time. This ensures clarity and prevents confusion when references are renumbered during the editing process.

Electronic-Only Sources

For articles published only in electronic format without traditional page numbers, use the article number, DOI, or other unique identifier in place of page numbers. Many modern journals assign article numbers (e.g., e202301456) that function like page numbers for citation purposes. Always include the DOI when available, as it provides the most reliable way for readers to locate electronic publications.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ACS citation style?
ACS citation style is the referencing format established by the American Chemical Society for use in chemistry and related scientific publications. It uses a numbered superscript system for in-text citations and formats references with specific rules for author names (last name, initials without periods), abbreviated journal titles (per CASSI), and a distinctive ordering of year, volume, and pages. It is the standard format for all ACS-published journals and widely used in chemistry education.
How do I format an ACS journal article citation?
An ACS journal article citation follows this format: Author 1; Author 2; Author 3. Article Title. J. Abbrev. Year, Volume, Pages. DOI. Author names use last name followed by initials without periods. Authors are separated by semicolons. The journal title is abbreviated per CASSI and italicized. The year is bolded, and the volume is italicized. Example: Smith, J A; Jones, B C. Synthesis of Novel Compounds. J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024, 146, 1234-1245.
How many authors should I list in an ACS citation?
In ACS style, list all authors for references with up to 10 authors, separated by semicolons. For works with more than 10 authors, list the first 10 authors followed by "et al." This rule applies to all source types including journal articles, books, and patents. Each author name is formatted as Last Name, First Initial Middle Initial (without periods between initials).
Do I italicize journal names in ACS format?
Yes, journal names in ACS format are italicized and must be abbreviated according to the Chemical Abstracts Service Source Index (CASSI). Common abbreviations include J. Am. Chem. Soc. for Journal of the American Chemical Society, J. Org. Chem. for Journal of Organic Chemistry, and Angew. Chem., Int. Ed. for Angewandte Chemie International Edition. Single-word journal titles like Science or Nature are not abbreviated but are still italicized.
How do I cite a website in ACS format?
To cite a website in ACS format: Author or Organization. Title of Page. Site Name. URL (accessed Month Day, Year). The access date is important because web content can change. If no author is listed, begin with the title or organization name. Example: National Institutes of Health. Drug Interaction Checker. NIH.gov. https://example.com (accessed Jan 15, 2024). Always verify the URL is correct and accessible before submitting your paper.
What is the difference between ACS and APA citation styles?
ACS uses numbered superscript citations in text and lists references in order of appearance, while APA uses author-date parenthetical citations and lists references alphabetically. ACS abbreviates journal titles using CASSI and separates authors with semicolons, while APA uses full journal titles and ampersands. ACS places the year after the journal abbreviation, while APA places it immediately after the authors. ACS is standard in chemistry; APA is standard in psychology and social sciences.
How do I cite a book in ACS format?
An ACS book citation follows this format: Author 1; Author 2. Book Title, Edition; Publisher: City, Year. For example: March, J; Smith, M B. Advanced Organic Chemistry, 7th ed.; Wiley: New York, 2020. The book title is italicized (unlike article titles). Include the edition only for editions beyond the first. For edited volumes, add "Ed." or "Eds." after the editor names.
Should I include DOIs in ACS citations?
Yes, including DOIs (Digital Object Identifiers) in ACS citations is strongly recommended and increasingly required by journals. The DOI provides a permanent, stable link to the publication. Format the DOI as a full URL: https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx. Place it at the end of the reference entry, after the page numbers. Always verify that the DOI resolves to the correct publication before including it in your reference list.
How do I cite a patent in ACS format?
To cite a patent in ACS format: Inventor 1; Inventor 2. Title of Patent. Patent Number, Date. Include the country designation with the patent number (e.g., U.S. Patent 9,876,543). The date can be formatted as the full date or just the year. Example: Smith, J A; Jones, B C. Method for Synthesizing Compound X. U.S. Patent 9,876,543, March 15, 2023.
What is the CASSI abbreviation system?
CASSI (Chemical Abstracts Service Source Index) is the standard system for abbreviating journal titles in chemistry citations. Maintained by the American Chemical Society, it provides official abbreviations for thousands of scientific journals. Common abbreviation rules include: "Journal" becomes "J.", "Chemistry" becomes "Chem.", "International" becomes "Int.", and single-word titles remain unabbreviated. The CAS website offers a free CASSI lookup tool for finding correct abbreviations. Using proper CASSI abbreviations is mandatory in ACS-formatted references.
