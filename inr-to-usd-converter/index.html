RBI Regulations on Forex Transactions

The Reserve Bank of India tightly regulates foreign exchange under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999. Understanding these rules is critical if you're converting large amounts or making regular transfers. I've summarized the key regulations that affect most individuals.

Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)

The LRS is the primary framework for Indian residents to send money abroad. Key points:

  • $250,000 per financial year (April to March) per individual
  • Education, travel, medical treatment, gifts/donations, investments in shares/property abroad, maintenance of relatives, emigration expenses
  • Forex trading, margin trading, lottery tickets, banned items
  • TCS (Tax Collected at Source): 20% on remittances exceeding ₹7 lakh per year (except 5% for education loans from specified institutions, and 0% for medical/education up to ₹7 lakh). TCS is adjustable against your income tax liability.
  • Your Permanent Account Number is mandatory for all LRS transactions
  • Form 15CA/15Required for remittances above ₹5 lakh; a CA certificate (Form 15CB) is needed for amounts above ₹5 lakh that are not covered by the exemption list
Important compliance note: Non-compliance with FEMA regulations can result in penalties up to three times the amount involved. All outward remittances under LRS are reported to the RBI and linked to your PAN. Ensure you have proper documentation, especially for amounts above ₹7 lakh where TCS applies.

NRI Tax Implications Overview

The intersection of Indian and US tax law creates unique challenges for NRIs. The India-US Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) provides relief, but navigating it requires care. This section covers the essentials, but I strongly recommend consulting a cross-border tax professional for your specific situation. These details come from our original research into FEMA compliance.

Key Tax Considerations for NRIs

  • Residential status matters: India taxes based on residential status (determined by days spent in India), not citizenship. If you're in India for less than 182 days in a financial year, you're generally non-resident for tax purposes.
  • Indian income is taxable in Salary earned in India, rental income from Indian property, capital gains on Indian assets, and interest on NRO accounts are all taxable in India regardless of your residential status.
  • The India-US treaty prevents double taxation. If you pay tax on Indian income in India, you can claim a Foreign Tax Credit on your US return (Form 1116). The credit is limited to the lower of Indian tax paid or US tax on the same income.
  • US residents (including green card holders) must report foreign accounts exceeding $10,000 aggregate on FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR). This includes NRE, NRO, and FCNR accounts.
  • Indian financial institutions report NRI account details to the US IRS under FATCA. US residents must also report specified foreign financial assets on Form 8938 if they exceed the threshold ($50,000 on the last day of the year or $75,000 at any point).
  • Capital gains on property: If an NRI sells Indian property, the buyer must deduct 20% TDS (plus surcharge and cess) on long-term capital gains, or 30% on short-term gains. The NRI can claim a lower deduction with a certificate from the Assessing Officer.
Don't forget to file Form 67 in India when claiming DTAA relief. This form must be filed before the due date of your Indian return. Many NRIs miss this requirement and end up paying tax in both countries on the same income.

Factors Affecting the INR/USD Exchange Rate

India's currency dynamics are unique among emerging markets because of the RBI's heavy hand in managing the Rupee. The RBI holds one of the world's largest forex reserve stockpiles (over $600 billion) and uses it to prevent excessive volatility.

Key Drivers

  • Unlike free-floating currencies, the Rupee is a "managed float." The RBI buys and sells dollars to keep the Rupee in a desired range. This is why the Rupee rarely moves more than 0.5% in a single day.
  • India imports about 85% of its crude oil. Higher oil prices mean higher dollar demand to pay for imports, which weakens the Rupee. A $10/barrel increase in oil roughly translates to $15 billion in additional annual import costs.
  • Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) flows: When foreign investors buy Indian stocks and bonds, they bring dollars in (strengthening INR). When they sell and repatriate, it weakens the Rupee. India's stock market has attracted massive FPI flows due to strong GDP growth.
  • India's $250+ billion IT export industry is a major source of dollar inflows. Companies like TCS, Infosys, and Wipro earn in USD and convert to INR for operations.
  • India receives over $125 billion annually in remittances, making it the world's largest recipient. This steady dollar inflow supports the Rupee.
  • Inflation differential: India's inflation (typically 4-6%) is higher than the US (2-3%), which structurally weakens the Rupee over time. This is why the Rupee has moved from 45/$ in 2010 to 84/$ in 2026.
  • Current account deficit: India typically imports more than it exports, creating a structural need for dollars. The deficit was around 1-2% of GDP in 2025.
  • US Federal When the Fed raises rates, capital flows from emerging markets to US treasury bonds, putting pressure on the Rupee.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current INR to USD exchange rate? +
As of March 2026, the approximate mid-market rate is 1 USD = 84 INR (or ₹1 = $0.0119). The RBI publishes a daily reference rate at 1:30 PM IST. Actual rates from banks and transfer services include a markup of 0.5-3% depending on the provider and method.
How much is 1 Lakh in USD? +
1 Lakh (₹1,00,000) is approximately $1,190 USD at the current rate of 84 INR/USD. In the Indian numbering system, 1 Lakh = 100,000. This amount is commonly referenced for monthly salaries of mid-level professionals in India's IT sector.
How much is 1 Crore in USD? +
1 Crore (₹1,00,00,000 or 10,000,000) is approximately $119,048 USD. In the Indian numbering system, 1 Crore = 100 Lakhs = 10 Million. This is a common benchmark for property prices and business valuations. "Crorepati" (someone worth 1 Crore or more) is a cultural milestone in India, equivalent to being a "millionaire" in the West.
What is the LRS limit for sending money abroad from India? +
The Liberalised Remittance Scheme allows Indian residents to remit up to $250,000 per financial year (April to March). This covers education, travel, investments, property abroad, gifts, and maintenance of relatives. Since October 2023, a 20% TCS applies on remittances exceeding ₹7 lakh per year (with exceptions for education and medical purposes). The TCS is not an additional tax, it's an advance tax that can be adjusted against your income tax liability when filing returns.
Can NRIs repatriate money from India freely? +
It depends on the account type. NRE (Non-Resident External) account funds are freely repatriable, both principal and interest, with no limit. NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account funds can be repatriated up to $1 million per financial year after paying applicable taxes and obtaining a CA certificate. FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) deposits are fully repatriable as they're already in foreign currency.
Why doesn't the Rupee fluctuate as much as other EM currencies? +
The RBI actively manages the Rupee through forex market intervention. With over $600 billion in reserves, the RBI can buy or sell billions of dollars daily to smooth volatility. This "managed float" approach means the Rupee depreciates gradually (2-3% per year against USD) rather than in sharp moves. The tradeoff is that the RBI's reserves fluctuate, and the gradual depreciation still adds up over time.
Is UPI accepted for international payments? +
UPI is primarily domestic, but cross-border UPI is expanding rapidly. As of 2025, UPI works in Singapore, UAE, France, Mauritius, Nepal, Bhutan, and Sri Lanka. It doesn't yet work directly in the US, but the NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) is negotiating with multiple countries. For now, NRIs in the US can use UPI through select NRE-linked bank accounts.
