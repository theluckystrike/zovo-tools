For example, a single filer with $60,000 in taxable income after the standard deduction would owe approximately $60 + $60 + $600 + ($43,000 x 5.75%) = $3,192.50 in Virginia state tax. The effective state rate in that scenario works out to about 5.32%, which is below the top marginal rate of 5.75%.

Virginia conforms to many federal tax provisions but maintains its own standard deduction and personal exemption amounts. The state does not index its brackets to inflation, which means these thresholds have stayed the same for a long period.

FICA: Social Security and Medicare

FICA taxes fund Social Security and Medicare. Every worker in Virginia pays these regardless of state tax rules:

  • Social Security: 6.2% on wages up to $176,100 (2025 wage base). Your employer matches this amount.
  • Medicare: 1.45% on all wages with no cap. An additional 0.9% surtax applies to wages exceeding $200,000 for single filers ($250,000 for married filing jointly).
  • Total FICA: 7.65% for most workers, combining both Social Security and Medicare contributions.

FICA is calculated on gross wages before the federal or Virginia standard deduction but after certain pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions. These taxes are not reducible through filing status or exemptions.

Virginia Deductions and Exemptions

Virginia offers its own set of deductions and exemptions separate from the federal system:

  • Standard Deduction: $8,000 for single filers, $16,000 for married filing jointly
  • Personal Exemption: $930 per taxpayer and each qualifying dependent
  • Age Deduction: Up to $12,000 for taxpayers age 65 and older (income-limited)
  • Itemized Deductions: Virginia allows itemization using most of the same categories as federal itemized deductions

The combination of Virginia's standard deduction and personal exemptions means that a married couple with two children has $19,720 removed from taxable income before state brackets apply ($16,000 standard deduction + $3,720 in exemptions). This is a meaningful reduction that lowers the effective state tax rate for families.

Check your latest Virginia tax forms or visit the Virginia Department of Taxation for the most current figures.

Self-Employment Taxes in Virginia

If you work for yourself in Virginia, your tax situation differs from a W-2 employee in several ways:

  • Self-Employment Tax: You pay both the employee and employer share of FICA, totaling 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on net self-employment income. The 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies above $200,000.
  • Deductible Half: You can deduct 50% of the self-employment tax from your adjusted gross income, which reduces both your federal and Virginia taxable income.
  • Quarterly Estimated Taxes: Virginia requires quarterly estimated tax payments if you expect to owe $150 or more in state taxes. Federal quarterly payments are also required.
  • Virginia State Tax: The same four-bracket progressive system applies to self-employment income after deductions.
  • Business Deductions: Home office, equipment, mileage, health insurance premiums, and retirement contributions (SEP-IRA, Solo 401k) reduce your taxable income.

Self-employed Virginia workers should plan for a combined effective tax rate of 30-40% depending on income level. Setting aside at least 30% of net income for taxes is a common recommendation. Consult the IRS Self-Employed resource page for federal guidance.

Virginia Cost of Living

Take-home pay only tells part of the story. Virginia's cost of living varies dramatically by region:

  • Northern Virginia (NoVA): Among the most expensive areas in the U.S. Median home prices exceed $600,000 in Fairfax County. However, salaries are 30-50% higher than the state average.
  • Richmond Metro: Moderate cost of living with median home prices around $340,000. Strong job market in finance, healthcare, and government.
  • Hampton Roads: Below-average costs with median homes around $290,000. Military and port-related industries dominate.
  • Charlottesville: University-driven economy with rising housing costs but a high quality of life.
  • Southwest Virginia: Lowest cost of living in the state, with correspondingly lower average salaries.

When evaluating a job offer in Virginia, always consider the regional cost of living alongside the salary. A $90,000 salary in Richmond may provide more purchasing power than $120,000 in Arlington.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Virginia state tax calculated on my paycheck?

Virginia uses a progressive system. Your employer subtracts allowances and exemptions from your gross pay, then applies the four-bracket rates (2%, 3%, 5%, 5.75%) to the resulting taxable amount. The tax is withheld proportionally each pay period based on your VA-4 withholding form.

Do I need to file a Virginia return if I work remotely for an out-of-state employer?

If you live in Virginia and work remotely, you owe Virginia state tax on your income regardless of where your employer is located. Virginia taxes residents on worldwide income. You may receive credits for taxes paid to other states if applicable.

What is the Virginia VA-4 form?

The VA-4 is Virginia's equivalent of the federal W-4. You submit it to your employer to determine how much Virginia state tax to withhold from each paycheck. You can claim exemptions for yourself, your spouse, and dependents to reduce withholding.

Are there any Virginia tax credits I should know about?

Virginia offers several credits including the Earned Income Tax Credit (matching 20% of the federal EITC), the Land Preservation Tax Credit, and credits for taxes paid to other states. Low-income taxpayers benefit most from the state EITC.

When are Virginia state taxes due?

Virginia individual income tax returns are due May 1, which is different from the federal April 15 deadline. Estimated quarterly payments for self-employed workers follow a separate schedule aligned with federal estimated payment dates.
