How It Works

This headshot editor runs entirely in your web browser using the HTML5 Canvas API. When you upload a photo, it is loaded into memory on your device. No data is sent to any external server at any point.

The editing pipeline works as follows:

  1. Upload and decode. Your image file is read using the FileReader API and decoded into a bitmap that the Canvas API can manipulate.
  2. Crop and position. You select an output size (LinkedIn, resume, passport, or custom). The tool calculates the crop region based on your zoom and pan settings, maintaining the correct aspect ratio.
  3. Pixel-level adjustments. Brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, and sharpness are applied by reading and modifying individual pixel values in the ImageData buffer. This is the same approach used by desktop photo editors.
  4. Filter presets. Each filter preset applies a specific combination of adjustments. For example, the "Professional" preset adds subtle contrast, slight desaturation, and mild sharpening.
  5. Export. The final image is rendered at the target resolution and encoded as PNG or JPEG for download.

Tips for Professional Headshots

A professional headshot conveys competence and approachability. According to portrait photography best practices, several factors determine whether a headshot looks professional:

Lighting

Natural window light produces the most flattering results. Position yourself facing a large window with the light source at a 30-45 degree angle. Avoid overhead fluorescent lighting, which creates unflattering shadows under the eyes.

Background

A plain, uncluttered background keeps the focus on your face. White, light gray, or muted blue backgrounds are standard for corporate headshots. Avoid busy patterns or distracting elements.

Framing

Crop from mid-chest up. Your face should fill 60-70% of the vertical frame. Eyes should sit at roughly the upper third of the image. Leave a small margin of space above the head.

Expression

A genuine, relaxed smile with a slight squint of the eyes (known as a "Duchenne smile") reads as authentic and approachable. Practice in a mirror before the shoot. Avoid forced grins.

Clothing

Wear solid, neutral colors. Dark blues, grays, and blacks are safe choices. Avoid loud patterns, logos, and bright whites, which can cause overexposure in photos.

Camera Settings

If using a phone, hold it at eye level and use the rear camera for better quality. Use portrait mode if available. If using a DSLR, an 85mm lens at f/2.8 produces flattering compression and background blur.

Lighting and Adjustments

Good lighting is the single most important factor in headshot photography. Here is what each adjustment in the editor does and when to use it:

  • Brightness: Increases or decreases the overall luminance of the image. Use it when the original photo is too dark (underexposed) or too bright (overexposed). Keep adjustments moderate; values beyond +/- 30 often look unnatural.
  • Contrast: Controls the difference between light and dark areas. Increasing contrast makes shadows deeper and highlights brighter, which adds visual punch. A small boost (+10 to +20) is common for professional headshots.
  • Saturation: Adjusts color intensity. Corporate headshots typically look best with neutral to slightly reduced saturation (-10 to 0). Avoid oversaturating, which makes skin tones look unnatural.
  • Warmth: Shifts the color temperature. Positive values add warm (golden/amber) tones; negative values add cool (blue) tones. A slight warm shift (+5 to +15) can make skin look healthier.
  • Sharpness: Applies an unsharp mask to enhance edge detail. Use moderately (20-40) to make the image crisper without creating visible halos around edges.

Common Use Cases

Professional headshots are used across many contexts. Here are the most common scenarios where this tool helps:

  • LinkedIn and professional networking: A high-quality profile photo increases profile views by up to 21 times and connection requests by 36 times, according to LinkedIn's own data.
  • Job applications and resumes: In regions where resume photos are standard (most of Europe, Asia, and Latin America), a polished headshot sets a strong first impression.
  • Company websites and directories: Consistent, well-lit team headshots with uniform backgrounds create a cohesive and trustworthy brand image.
  • Conference speaker profiles: Event organizers typically require a square headshot at minimum 400x400 px resolution.
  • Real estate and consulting: Agents and consultants use professional headshots on business cards, yard signs, and marketing materials.
