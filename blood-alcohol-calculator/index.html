How to Use This Blood Alcohol Calculator

This blood alcohol calculator uses the Widmark formula to estimate your blood alcohol content (BAC) based on several key factors. Here is a step-by-step guide to using the tool and understanding your results.

Step 1 Select Your Gender

Choose Male or Female. Gender matters in BAC calculations because males and females have different average body water percentages. Males typically have about 68% body water content while females average about 55%. Since alcohol distributes through body water, this difference significantly affects BAC. The Widmark formula accounts for this by using a distribution ratio of 0.68 for males and 0.55 for females.

Step 2 Enter Your Body Weight

Type your weight and select either pounds (lbs) or kilograms (kg). A heavier person generally has a lower BAC than a lighter person after consuming the same amount of alcohol, because the alcohol is distributed across a larger volume of body water. Make sure to enter your actual weight for the most accurate estimate.

Step 3 Choose Your Drink Type

Select from one of the preset drink types or choose Custom to enter your own ABV percentage and serving size. The presets represent standard drink sizes: Beer at 12 oz and 5% ABV, Wine at 5 oz and 12% ABV, and Spirits at 1.5 oz and 40% ABV. Each of these standard drinks contains approximately 14 grams of pure alcohol. If you are drinking a craft beer with higher ABV or a cocktail with multiple shots, the Custom option lets you enter the exact alcohol percentage and serving size.

Step 4 Enter Number of Drinks and Time Period

Input the total number of drinks you have consumed and the number of hours over which you have been drinking. The time period is important because your body metabolizes alcohol continuously. The average metabolism rate is approximately 0.015% BAC per hour. So if you consumed 3 beers over 3 hours, your BAC would be lower than if you drank the same 3 beers in 1 hour.

Step 5 Review Your Results

After clicking Calculate, you will see several pieces of information. The main BAC number shows your estimated blood alcohol content as a percentage. The color coding gives you a quick visual indicator: green means below the standard legal limit, yellow means approaching the limit, and red means you are likely over the legal limit. You will also see the estimated number of standard drinks equivalent to your intake, the total grams of alcohol consumed, the amount your body has metabolized so far, and the estimated time until your BAC returns to zero.

Understanding the BAC Timeline Chart

The timeline chart shows how your BAC changes over the coming hours. The curve peaks at your current estimated BAC and then gradually decreases as your body metabolizes the alcohol. The red dashed line indicates the common legal driving limit of 0.08%. This visual representation helps you understand that even after you stop drinking, it takes considerable time for alcohol to leave your system.

The Widmark Formula Explained

The Widmark formula, developed by Swedish scientist Erik Widmark in the 1930s, remains the standard method for estimating BAC. The formula is:

BAC = (A / (r x W)) - (beta x t)

Where A is the mass of alcohol consumed in grams, r is the Widmark factor (0.68 for males, 0.55 for females), W is body weight in grams, beta is the metabolism rate (approximately 0.015% per hour), and t is the time in hours since drinking began.

The mass of alcohol is calculated from the volume of each drink, its ABV percentage, and the density of ethanol (0.789 grams per milliliter). For a standard 12-oz beer at 5% ABV, the calculation would be: 12 oz x 29.5735 ml/oz x 0.05 x 0.789 g/ml = approximately 14 grams of alcohol.

Practical Examples

Social Dinner

A 170-pound male drinks 2 glasses of wine (5 oz each, 12% ABV) over 2 hours with dinner. Using the the total alcohol is about 28 grams. His estimated BAC would be approximately 0.05% before metabolism, minus 0.03% for 2 hours of metabolism, resulting in a BAC of about 0.02%. He is likely below the legal limit but should still exercise caution.

Weekend Outing

A 130-pound female drinks 3 craft beers (12 oz each, 7% ABV) over 3 hours. The total alcohol is approximately 59 grams. Her estimated peak BAC would be around 0.15% before metabolism, minus 0.045% for 3 hours, resulting in approximately 0.11%. This is well above the legal limit in all US states, and she would need roughly 7 additional hours to reach 0.00%.

Single Cocktail

A 200-pound male has one cocktail containing 2 oz of spirits (40% ABV) 1 hour ago. The alcohol mass is about 18.7 grams. His BAC would be approximately 0.03% minus 0.015% for 1 hour of metabolism, giving an estimated 0.015%. He is well below the legal limit, though even small amounts of alcohol can impair judgment and reaction time.

Factors That Affect Your BAC

While the Widmark formula provides a useful estimate, many factors can cause your actual BAC to differ from the calculated value.

Food Intake

Eating before or while drinking slows the absorption of alcohol from the stomach into the bloodstream. A full meal can reduce peak BAC by up to 20% compared to drinking on an empty stomach. High-protein and high-fat foods are particularly effective at slowing absorption., food does not prevent intoxication; it only delays and slightly reduces the peak.

Metabolism and Genetics

Individuals metabolize alcohol at different rates. The average is 0.015% per hour, but this can range from 0.010% to 0.020% or more. People with higher body fat percentages tend to reach higher BAC levels because fat tissue does not absorb alcohol as effectively as lean tissue. Genetic factors, including variations in the enzymes alcohol dehydrogenase and aldehyde dehydrogenase, also play a significant role.

Medications

Many medications interact with alcohol and can affect how your body processes it. Some medications slow alcohol metabolism, leading to higher BAC levels. Others intensify the effects of alcohol even at lower BAC levels. Always check medication labels and consult your doctor about alcohol interactions.

Hydration and Health

Dehydration can increase your BAC because there is less body water for the alcohol to distribute into. Your overall health, liver function, and even recent illness can affect alcohol metabolism. Chronic heavy drinking can lead to increased tolerance (the liver processes alcohol faster) but also liver damage that eventually reduces metabolism.

Legal BAC Limits Around the World

Legal BAC limits for driving vary significantly between countries. In the United States, most states set the limit at 0.08%, with Utah being the exception at 0.05%. Commercial drivers in the US are held to a stricter 0.04% limit. Many European countries have adopted 0.05% as their standard, including France, Germany, and Australia. Some countries, including Brazil, Saudi Arabia, and several Eastern European nations, enforce a zero-tolerance policy with a 0.00% limit. In Japan, the limit is 0.03%. Regardless of the legal limit in your jurisdiction, any amount of alcohol impairs driving ability to some degree.

Tips for Responsible Drinking

  • Eat a substantial meal before drinking. Food slows alcohol absorption and reduces peak BAC.
  • Pace yourself to one standard drink per hour, which roughly matches your body's metabolism rate.
  • Alternate alcoholic drinks with water or non-alcoholic beverages to stay hydrated and slow consumption.
  • Always arrange for a designated driver, rideshare, or taxi before you start drinking.
  • Know the alcohol content of what you are drinking. Craft beers and cocktails often contain significantly more alcohol than standard drinks.
  • Be aware that tiredness, stress, and medications can amplify the effects of alcohol.
  • Never rely on a calculator to decide whether you are safe to drive. When in doubt, wait or find another way home.

How accurate is this BAC calculator? ▶
This calculator uses the Widmark formula, which is the standard method for estimating BAC., actual BAC varies based on many individual factors including metabolism, food intake, medications, and overall health. This tool provides an estimate and should not be used to determine whether you are safe to drive.
What is the Widmark formula? ▶
The Widmark formula calculates BAC = (Alcohol consumed in grams / (Body weight in grams x Widmark factor)) - (Metabolism rate x Hours). The Widmark factor is 0.68 for males and 0.55 for females, and the average metabolism rate is 0.015% per hour.
What is a standard drink? ▶
A standard drink in the United States contains about 14 grams (0.6 oz) of pure alcohol. This is roughly equivalent to 12 oz of regular beer (5% ABV), 5 oz of wine (12% ABV), or 1.5 oz of distilled spirits (40% ABV).
What BAC level is legally drunk? ▶
In most US states, the legal BAC limit for drivers 21 and over is 0.08%. For commercial drivers, the limit is typically 0.04%. Utah has a lower limit of 0.05%. For drivers under 21, most states enforce a zero tolerance policy with limits between 0.00% and 0.02%.
How long does it take for alcohol to leave your system? ▶
The body metabolizes alcohol at an average rate of about 0.015% BAC per hour. This means if your BAC is 0.08%, it would take roughly 5.3 hours to reach 0.00%., metabolism rates vary between individuals.
Does food affect BAC? ▶
Yes, eating food before or while drinking slows the absorption of alcohol into the bloodstream, which can result in a lower peak BAC., food does not prevent intoxication. It only delays and slightly reduces the peak BAC level.
Can coffee or water lower my BAC? ▶
No. Coffee, water, cold showers, and exercise do not lower your BAC or speed up alcohol metabolism. Only time reduces BAC. Coffee may make you feel more alert, but it does not make you less impaired.
Why does gender affect BAC? ▶
Males and females have different body water content percentages. Males average about 68% body water while females average about 55%. Since alcohol distributes through body water, the same amount of alcohol results in a higher BAC for someone with lower body water content.
