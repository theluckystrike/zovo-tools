How to Use the MP3 Cutter

  1. Upload your file. Drag and drop an audio file onto the upload area, or click to browse. The tool accepts MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A, and other browser-supported formats.
  2. View the waveform. Once loaded, you'll see a visual representation of your audio with file details like duration, sample rate, and estimated bitrate.
  3. Set start and end points. Drag the green start marker and red end marker to select the region you keep.
  4. Preview your selection. Click "Play Selection" to hear only the trimmed portion before exporting.
  5. Export. Click "Export Trimmed Audio" to download the selected region as a high-quality WAV file.

Why Cut Audio Files?

Cutting audio is one of the most common tasks in audio production, content creation, and everyday file management. Here are real-world situations where trimming makes a difference:

  • Ringtone creation. Most ringtones be under 30 seconds. I've used this tool to cut hundreds of ringtones from songs without installing any software.
  • Podcast editing. Removing silence at the beginning and end, or extracting a segment for a social media teaser, becomes straightforward with a waveform view.
  • Music sampling. Musicians often isolate a drum break, vocal phrase, or melodic hook from a longer recording.
  • Presentation audio. Embedding audio into slides usually requires trimming to exact length.
  • Voice memo cleanup. Quick recordings on your phone often have dead air that should be removed before sharing.

How Browser-Based Audio Cutting Works

This tool uses the Web Audio API, a browser-native feature set for audio processing. When you upload a file, the browser decodes the compressed audio into raw PCM sample data. The waveform is rendered by sampling peak amplitudes from the decoded buffer and drawing them on an HTML Canvas element.

When you export a trimmed selection, the tool copies the relevant portion of the decoded audio buffer into a new WAV file. WAV is used because it can be created entirely in the browser without external encoding libraries, ensuring zero dependencies and maximum compatibility. The encoding follows the standard RIFF WAV specification documented on Wikipedia.

Because the entire process uses -in browser APIs, your audio data never touches a remote server. This is fundamentally different from tools like mp3cut.net or clideo.com, which upload your files for server-side processing. Discussions on Hacker News about web audio tools frequently highlight the privacy advantages of client-side processing.

Tips for Clean Audio Cuts

  • Cut at zero-crossings. Audio waveforms oscillate above and below a center line. Cutting at a point where the waveform crosses zero reduces clicks and pops at the edit point.
  • Leave a tiny margin. When trimming speech, leave about 50-100ms of silence before the first word and after the last word. This prevents abrupt clipping.
  • Preview before exporting. Always use "Play Selection" to hear the trimmed region. What looks right on the waveform doesn't always sound right.
  • Use the time display. The start, end, and selection duration readouts give precise timings for matching required durations.
  • Consider format conversion. The export is WAV for maximum quality. If you need smaller files, use an audio converter afterward.
