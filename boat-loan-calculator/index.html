How Boat Loans Work

Boat financing operates similarly to auto or mortgage loans, but with some key differences. Marine lenders evaluate the vessel type, age, and condition in addition to your credit profile. Here is what you need to know:

Loan terms: Boat loans typically range from 2 to 20 years. Boats priced above $50,000 often qualify for longer terms (12-20 years) secured by the vessel, similar to a marine mortgage. Smaller boats under $25,000 are frequently financed with shorter unsecured personal loans.

Interest rates: Rates depend on your credit score, the boat's age, loan amount, and term length. New boats from established manufacturers generally qualify for the best rates. Expect rates between 5.99% and 10% for most borrowers in 2026.

Down payment: Most lenders require 10-20% down. A larger down payment lowers your monthly payment, reduces total interest, and helps avoid negative equity as boats depreciate. Boats typically lose 15-20% of value in the first year and about 10% per year after that.

Sales tax: Most states charge sales tax on boat purchases. Some states exempt boats or offer reduced rates. This calculator lets you factor sales tax into the loan amount, which is common practice.

Sources: Boat financing terms and rate ranges referenced from Bankrate, NADA Guides, and BoatUS.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a boat loan monthly payment calculated?
The monthly payment uses the standard amortization formula: M = P[r(1+r)^n]/[(1+r)^n-1], where P is the loan principal (boat price minus down payment and trade-in, plus sales tax), r is the monthly interest rate (APR divided by 12), and n is the total number of monthly payments. This calculator handles all the math instantly.
What is a good interest rate for a boat loan in 2026?
Competitive boat loan rates in 2026 range from 6% to 9% APR for borrowers with strong credit (720+). New boats from established manufacturers typically qualify for the lowest rates. Used boats and longer terms generally carry higher rates.
What is the true cost of owning a boat?
Beyond the loan payment, budget for annual insurance (1-2% of value), maintenance and repairs (roughly 10% of value per year), marina or storage fees ($1,200-$6,000+ annually), fuel, winterization, and registration. Many owners find carrying costs run 10-15% of the purchase price each year.
How much should I put down on a boat loan?
Most marine lenders require 10-20% down. Putting at least 20% down gets you better rates, lower monthly payments, and helps avoid being underwater as the boat depreciates. For boats under $25,000, some lenders offer unsecured personal loans with no down payment, but at higher interest rates.
Can I finance a used boat?
Yes, many lenders finance used boats. Rates on used boat loans are typically 0.5-2% higher than new boat loans. Most lenders require the boat to be less than 20 years old and in good condition, and you may face shorter maximum loan terms for older vessels.
