The Conversion Formula

The formula to convert an hourly wage to an annual salary is simple, but it can get detailed depending on your work schedule.

Annual Salary = Hourly Rate x Hours Per Week x Weeks Per Year

For a standard full-time employee working 40 hours per week for all 52 weeks, that means.

$25/hr x 40 hours x 52 weeks = $52,000/year

But most people don't work exactly 52 weeks. If you take 2 weeks of unpaid vacation, you would use 50 weeks. If you have paid time off, you still use 52 because you are being paid for those weeks. This distinction trips people up. If your employer offers paid vacation, sick days, and holidays, your annual salary calculation should still use 52 weeks because those are paid weeks.

For the reverse calculation (salary to hourly), just divide.

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary / (Hours Per Week x Weeks Per Year)

So $52,000 per year divided by (40 x 52) = $25.00 per hour. Simple enough, but the overtime calculation adds a layer of complexity that I'll cover next.

Understanding Overtime Pay

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt employees must receive overtime pay at 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a single workweek. This is federal law, though some states have additional overtime rules.

Here is how overtime changes the math. If you earn $25/hr and work 45 hours per week.

ComponentCalculationWeekly Pay
Regular hours (40)40 x $25.00$1,000.00
Overtime hours (5)5 x $37.50 (1.5x)$187.50
Total weekly$1,187.50
Annual (52 weeks)$1,187.50 x 52$61,750.00

That is $9,750 more per year than the base $52,000. Overtime makes a massive difference, and it is something I don't see enough calculators account for. If you regularly work 45 or 50 hours per week, the overtime component can represent 15-25% of your total annual earnings.

I should note that this applies to non-exempt (hourly) employees. Salaried exempt employees generally don't receive overtime pay regardless of hours worked. The distinction between exempt and non-exempt status is based on job duties and salary thresholds set by the Department of Labor. The 2025 salary threshold for overtime exemption is $58,656 per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert my hourly wage to an annual salary?

Multiply your hourly rate by the number of hours you work per week, then multiply by the number of weeks you work per year. For a standard full-time schedule, that is hourly rate times 40 times 52. So $20/hr becomes $20 x 40 x 52 = $41,600/year. If you get paid vacation, use 52 weeks. If your vacation is unpaid, subtract those weeks.

Does this calculator include overtime?

Yes. If you enter more than 40 hours per week, the calculator automatically applies the overtime multiplier (default 1.5x) to the hours above 40. You can also change the overtime rate to 2x (double time) or 1x (no premium) depending on your situation. This is one of the features I found missing from most other hourly to salary converters.

Should I use 50 or 52 weeks per year?

Use 52 weeks if you receive paid time off (PTO), paid vacation, or paid holidays. Your employer is paying you for those weeks even though you aren't working. Use 50 weeks (or whatever the actual number is) if your time off is unpaid. Most full-time salaried positions and many hourly positions include paid time off, so 52 is the correct number for most people.

What is the federal minimum wage in 2025?

The federal minimum wage remains $7.25/hr as of 2025. However, many states and cities have set higher minimums. California is at $16.00/hr, Washington at $16.66/hr, and New York at $15.00/hr (higher in NYC). At $7.25/hr for 40 hours and 52 weeks, the annual gross salary is $15,080. Many states have set their minimum at $15/hr or are phasing toward it.

How do I compare an hourly job to a salaried position?

First, convert both to the same unit (either annual or hourly). Then factor in the full compensation package. Salaried positions typically include health insurance ($5,000-$15,000 value), retirement matching (3-6% of salary), paid time off, and other benefits. A rough rule of thumb is to add 25% to the base salary to estimate total compensation. If the salaried position pays $50,000, the total compensation is roughly $62,500, which equals about $30/hr in total value.

What is the overtime threshold for 2025?

Under the FLSA, non-exempt employees must be paid overtime (1.5x regular rate) for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek. The salary threshold for overtime exemption in 2025 is $58,656/year ($1,128/week). If you are salaried below this amount and your job duties don't meet the executive, administrative, or professional exemption criteria, you are entitled to overtime pay.

Using This Data in Salary Negotiations

One practical use of an hourly-to-salary converter is preparing for salary negotiations. Here are a few ways to use the numbers.

If you are moving from hourly to salaried work, calculate your current total annual compensation including overtime. Many people accept salary offers that are lower than their current hourly earnings because they don't account for overtime. If you earn $25/hr and regularly work 45 hours per week, your current annual compensation is $61,750, not $52,000. Don't accept a salary offer below that number unless the benefits package makes up the difference.

If you are negotiating a raise, knowing your hourly rate helps frame the conversation. A $3/hr raise sounds modest, but it translates to $6,240 more per year ($3 x 40 x 52). Presenting it in annual terms can make the request feel more justified to both you and your manager.

For freelancers setting rates, work backward from your desired annual income. If you earn $80,000/year and expect to work 48 billable weeks at 35 hours per week (accounting for admin time and finding clients), your hourly rate needs to be at least $47.62. Add 25% for self-employment taxes and benefits you purchase yourself, and the minimum viable rate is about $60/hr. This is why experienced freelancers typically charge $75-$150/hr or more, even though their "salary equivalent" might be more modest.
