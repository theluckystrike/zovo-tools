How to Use This Grade Calculator

This free grade calculator helps students calculate their current grade, plan for upcoming exams, and track academic progress across multiple courses. It supports weighted grading categories, customizable letter grade thresholds, grade distribution visualization, and data import/export.

Step 1: Set Up Your Categories

Start by creating grade categories that match your course syllabus. Common categories include Homework, Quizzes, Midterm Exams, Final Exam, Projects, Labs, and Participation. Each category needs a name and a weight percentage. The weights should add up to 100%, though the calculator will warn you if they do not.

If you are unsure about your categories, click "Load preset categories" to start with a common configuration (Homework 20%, Quizzes 10%, Exams 40%, Projects 20%, Participation 10%). You can modify these at any time.

Step 2: Enter Your Assignments

Within each category, add individual assignments by clicking the "+ Add assignment" button. For each assignment, enter three pieces of information:

  • Assignment name (for your reference)
  • Points earned (your score)
  • Total points possible (what the assignment was out of)

The calculator computes each category's average automatically, then applies the category weights to produce your overall weighted grade.

Step 3: Read Your Results

The summary bar at the top updates in real time as you enter grades. It shows your overall weighted percentage, the corresponding letter grade, and how many assignments and categories are active. The grade distribution chart below gives a visual breakdown of how each category contributes to your final grade.

Step 4: Plan Ahead with the Final Exam Calculator

The "What Do I Need on the Final?" section lets you figure out the minimum score needed on your final exam (or any remaining assignment) to achieve a target grade. Enter your current overall grade, the weight of the final, and the grade you are aiming for. The calculator uses the formula:

Formula Required Score = (Target Grade - (1 - Final Weight) x Current Grade) / Final Weight

Example Current grade: 85%, Final weight: 25%, Target: 90%
Required = (90 - 0.75 x 85) / 0.25 = (90 - 63.75) / 0.25 = 105%
You would need 105% on the final to achieve a 90% overall.

Multiple Courses

Use the course tabs at the top of the calculator to manage grades for different classes. Click "+ Add Course" to create a new course, and click any course tab to switch to it. Each course maintains its own independent set of categories, assignments, and calculations. Right-click or use the X button to remove a course.

Understanding Weighted Grades

Weighted grading means that different types of assignments contribute differently to your final grade. This is the standard approach in most college courses and many high school classes.

How Weighted Grades Work

In a weighted system, each category of assignments has a designated weight, expressed as a percentage. The sum of all weights should equal 100%. Your grade in each category is calculated as the total points earned divided by the total points possible, converted to a percentage. Then, each category percentage is multiplied by its weight, and the results are summed to produce your overall grade.

Weighted Grade Example Homework (20% weight): scored 180/200 = 90%
Quizzes (15% weight): scored 42/50 = 84%
Midterm (25% weight): scored 78/100 = 78%
Project (25% weight): scored 92/100 = 92%
Participation (15% weight): scored 28/30 = 93.3%

Weighted grade = (0.20 x 90) + (0.15 x 84) + (0.25 x 78) + (0.25 x 92) + (0.15 x 93.3)
= 18 + 12.6 + 19.5 + 23 + 14 = 87.1%

Why Weighted Grades Matter

Without weights, a homework assignment worth 10 points would have the same impact on your grade as a midterm exam worth 10 points. That rarely reflects the actual importance of each assignment. Weighting ensures that high-stakes assessments like exams and projects have a proportionally larger effect on your final grade, which better represents your mastery of the material.

Letter Grade Scales and GPA

Letter grades provide a standardized way to communicate academic performance. While the most common scale in the United States assigns A through F based on percentage ranges, schools vary in their exact cutoff points.

Standard Grading Scale

The default thresholds in this calculator follow the most widely used scale:

  • A: 90% and above
  • B: 80% to 89%
  • C: 70% to 79%
  • D: 60% to 69%
  • F: below 60%

You can customize these thresholds in the "Letter Grade Thresholds" section to match your school's grading policy. Some institutions use a plus/minus system (A+, A, A-, B+, etc.) with narrower ranges. Others may have different cutoff points entirely.

GPA Conversion

Each letter grade corresponds to a GPA point value on a 4.0 scale. A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.0. For plus/minus grades, increments of 0.3 are typical (A- = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, etc.). Your cumulative GPA is the weighted average of all course GPAs, where the weights are the credit hours for each course.

Strategies for Improving Your Grades

A grade calculator is not just a reporting tool. It is a planning tool. Here are ways to use it strategically:

Identify High-Impact Categories

Focus your study time on categories with the highest weight. Improving your exam score by 5% in a category weighted at 40% has twice the impact of the same improvement in a 20% category. Use the grade distribution chart to visualize where your grade is coming from and where the biggest opportunities lie.

Set Realistic Targets

Use the "What Do I Need on the Final?" calculator early in the semester, not just before the final. By entering hypothetical current grades and checking what final scores you would need, you can gauge whether your target grade is realistically achievable and adjust your study plan accordingly.

Track Trends Over Time

Enter grades as you receive them throughout the semester. Watching your running average change over time helps you identify problems early. If your grade starts dropping in a particular category, you have time to adjust before it becomes critical.

Plan for the Worst Case

Enter estimated low scores for upcoming assignments to see the worst-case scenario for your final grade. This helps you understand how much room you have and whether you can afford to prioritize one course over another during a busy exam period.

Exporting and Backing Up Your Data

All grade data is stored in your browser's localStorage, which means it persists between visits but is tied to your specific browser on your specific device. To protect against data loss or to transfer your grades to another device, use the Export feature. It downloads a JSON file containing all courses, categories, assignments, and custom thresholds. Import the file on any device to restore your complete grade setup.
