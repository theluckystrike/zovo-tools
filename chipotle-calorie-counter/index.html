Complete Guide to Chipotle Nutrition

I eat at Chipotle regularly and have spent considerable time analyzing their nutritional data to understand exactly how each ingredient affects the total calorie count. This calorie counter uses data from Chipotle's official nutrition information, updated to reflect their current 2026 menu offerings.

Chipotle Mexican Grill has positioned itself as a fast-casual restaurant that offers customizable meals made with real ingredients. Unlike many fast food chains, Chipotle prepares food in-house daily and allows customers to build their meals from scratch. This customization is what makes calorie counting both important and challenging, because two bowls ordered at the same restaurant can differ by more than 1,000 calories depending on ingredient choices.

The calorie range for a Chipotle meal can span from under 300 calories for a lean salad to well over 1,500 calories for a loaded burrito with all the extras. Understanding where those calories come from gives you control over your nutrition without sacrificing the foods you enjoy.

How Chipotle Calories Break Down by Category

Every Chipotle meal starts with a base, adds a protein, and then layers on rice, beans, and toppings. Each category contributes differently to the overall nutritional profile.

The base itself is the first major calorie decision. A burrito bowl (the container itself) adds zero calories. A flour tortilla for a burrito adds 320 calories before you put anything inside it. This single choice is one of the biggest calorie swings on the entire menu. Taco shells add roughly 200 calories for a set of three. A salad base with romaine lettuce adds only about 15 calories.

Protein choices range from 150 to 210 calories per serving, but the macronutrient profiles differ significantly. Chicken delivers the best protein-per-calorie ratio at 32 grams of protein for 180 calories. Carnitas, while delicious, bring 210 calories with more fat and less protein per calorie. Sofritas (tofu-based) offer 150 calories with 8 grams of protein, making them the lowest-calorie protein but also the lowest in protein content.

Smart Ordering Strategies for Different Goals

Weight Loss Orders (Under 550 Calories)

If your primary goal is fat loss, Chipotle can be one of the best fast-casual options available. The key is understanding which ingredients deliver the most satisfaction per calorie.

My go-to lean order is a burrito bowl with chicken, fajita veggies, pico de gallo, green tomatillo salsa, and romaine lettuce. This combination comes in at roughly 245 calories with 34 grams of protein. Add half a serving of black beans for an additional 65 calories and 3.5 grams of protein, bringing the total to 310 calories with excellent satiety from the protein and fiber combination.

For those who find a bowl without rice unsatisfying, adding brown rice brings the total to roughly 520 calories. This is still a complete, filling meal that fits comfortably within most calorie deficit targets.

Muscle Building Orders (High Protein)

Chipotle is arguably the best fast-casual restaurant for high-protein meals. A double chicken bowl with black beans, brown rice, and tomato salsa delivers approximately 75 grams of protein at around 700 calories. That protein-to-calorie ratio is exceptional for a restaurant meal.

Steak offers a slightly different macro profile with less total protein per serving (21g) but a lower calorie count (150). Combining chicken and steak in a single bowl (some locations accommodate this) creates a varied protein source meal with excellent amino acid diversity.

For post-workout meals when you need both protein and carbohydrates for recovery, a bowl with double chicken, white rice, black beans, and corn salsa hits approximately 760 calories with 71 grams of protein and 77 grams of carbohydrates. The carb-to-protein ratio makes it an effective recovery meal.

Balanced Everyday Orders (600 to 800 Calories)

A well-balanced Chipotle meal that works for general health and maintenance typically includes a protein, one grain component, one bean choice, and two to three vegetable-based toppings. This pattern provides a good distribution of macronutrients without excessive calories from high-fat additions.

An example balanced order would be a burrito bowl with chicken, brown rice, pinto beans, fajita veggies, pico de gallo, and a half portion of cheese. This comes to approximately 665 calories with 47 grams of protein, 65 grams of carbohydrates, and 20 grams of fat. The macronutrient split is roughly 28% protein, 39% carbs, and 27% fat, which aligns well with general dietary guidelines.

Where the Hidden Calories Live

Several Chipotle ingredients carry more caloric weight than most customers expect. Understanding these hidden calorie sources prevents unintentional overeating.

The flour tortilla is the single biggest calorie addition at 320 calories. Many customers do not realize that choosing a bowl over a burrito saves them more calories than skipping guacamole. If you love the tortilla experience, ask for a tortilla on the side and use half of it to scoop bites, which cuts the calorie contribution roughly in half.

Guacamole adds 230 calories, mostly from healthy monounsaturated fats. While nutritionally valuable, it represents a significant calorie investment. One strategy is to add guac at home where you can control the portion size more precisely.

Sour cream and cheese each add approximately 110 calories. Together, they contribute 220 calories of mostly saturated fat. For the calorie-conscious, these are often the first toppings to eliminate because they add calories without proportional flavor impact compared to salsa-based toppings.

Chips are not included in the bowl builder above because they are ordered as a side item, but they deserve mention. A serving of Chipotle tortilla chips adds approximately 540 calories. Combined with guacamole, that side order totals 770 calories, which is more than many complete entrees.

Understanding Macronutrients at Chipotle

Macronutrients fall into three categories, and each plays a different role in your nutrition. Chipotle's customizable format lets you adjust all three with precision.

Protein at Chipotle

Protein supports muscle maintenance, satiety, and metabolic health. At Chipotle, protein content comes primarily from the meat or sofritas selection, with smaller contributions from beans, cheese, and queso. A single serving of chicken provides 32 grams of protein, making it the most protein-dense option per calorie.

For customers following high-protein diets (1g per pound of body weight or higher), Chipotle is one of the few fast-casual chains where you can hit 50+ grams of protein in a single meal without excessive calories. The double protein option (available at most locations for an extra charge) effectively doubles the protein contribution of your main selection.

Carbohydrates at Chipotle

Rice and beans are the primary carbohydrate sources. Each serving of rice (white or brown) provides approximately 40 grams of carbohydrates. Beans add another 22 grams per serving. The tortilla, at 50 grams of carbohydrates, is the single largest carb contributor on the menu.

For low-carb or ketogenic diets, a burrito bowl without rice, beans, or tortilla keeps total carbohydrates under 20 grams when paired with salsa-based toppings. Add protein and fats from guacamole, cheese, and sour cream for a keto-friendly meal.

Fat Sources at Chipotle

Dietary fat at Chipotle comes from several sources with varying nutritional quality. Guacamole provides healthy monounsaturated fats from avocado. Cheese and sour cream contribute saturated fats. The cooking oils used for rice and proteins add a baseline of fat across most items.

Carnitas has the highest fat content among proteins at 12 grams per serving. Sofritas, despite being plant-based, contains 10 grams of fat. Chicken and steak are leaner options at 7 and 6 grams respectively.

Chipotle vs. Other Fast Casual Chains

When I compare Chipotle to other fast-casual restaurants for nutritional value, several factors set it apart. The customization options allow for precise macro control that most chains cannot match. A Chipotle bowl can be built to fit almost any dietary framework, from keto to vegan to high-protein.

Compared to Qdoba, Chipotle's calorie counts tend to be slightly lower for equivalent menu items. Qdoba's queso is complimentary (included at no extra charge), which can lead to unintentional calorie creep when customers add it reflexively. Chipotle's portion sizes are also more standardized, making calorie estimation more dependable.

Compared to Moe's Southwest Grill, Chipotle uses fewer processed ingredients and generally higher-quality proteins. Moe's includes chips and salsa with every order, adding approximately 540 calories that many customers eat without tracking.

Using Chipotle for Meal Prep

Chipotle bowls transport well and can function as meal prep for busy schedules. Order two or three bowls at once, store them in the refrigerator, and reheat throughout the week. Rice and beans maintain texture well when reheated. Proteins stay moist if you keep the salsa mixed in.

The key to successful Chipotle meal prep is keeping wet and dry components separate. Request dressings, guacamole, and salsas on the side in small containers. Add them just before eating to prevent the bowl from becoming soggy during storage.

From a cost-per-protein-gram perspective, Chipotle competes favorably with grocery store chicken. A double chicken bowl provides roughly 64 grams of protein for approximately $12 to $14 (prices vary by location), which works out to roughly $0.19 to $0.22 per gram of protein. While more expensive than bulk grocery protein, the convenience and flavor make it a viable option for those who struggle with home cooking consistency.

Chipotle for Specific Dietary Patterns

Keto and Low-Carb at Chipotle

The ketogenic diet requires keeping daily carbohydrates below 20 to 50 grams to maintain ketosis. Chipotle is one of the most keto-friendly fast-casual restaurants because you can build a bowl that eliminates all high-carb ingredients while still creating a satisfying meal.

A keto-optimized Chipotle order starts with a bowl base (zero carbs from the container). Skip the rice and beans entirely, as a single serving of either adds 22 to 40 grams of carbs, which would exhaust most of a daily keto carb budget in one component. Choose any protein, then load up on fajita veggies (4g carbs), cheese (1g), sour cream (2g), and guacamole (8g). Top with green tomatillo salsa (4g carbs) or pico de gallo (4g).

This combination totals approximately 15 to 20 grams of net carbs with 35 to 50 grams of fat and adequate protein. The high fat content from guacamole, cheese, and sour cream aligns perfectly with keto macros while keeping carbs well within ketogenic range.

One practical tip for keto Chipotle orders is requesting extra lettuce as a base layer. The generous bed of romaine creates volume and crunch that partially compensates for the missing rice, adding only 1 gram of carbs in the process.

Vegan and Plant-Based at Chipotle

Chipotle offers more vegan-friendly options than most fast-casual chains. The sofritas (seasoned tofu) protein option is both vegan and flavorful, providing 150 calories with 8 grams of protein per serving. For additional protein, add both black and pinto beans to the same bowl.

The complete list of vegan items includes sofritas, fajita veggies, white rice, brown rice, black beans, pinto beans, pico de gallo, corn salsa, green tomatillo salsa, and romaine lettuce. Guacamole is also vegan. The items to avoid include all meat proteins, cheese, sour cream, queso, and the flour tortilla (which contains L-cysteine, a dough conditioner that may be animal-derived).

A well-constructed vegan bowl with sofritas, both beans, brown rice, guacamole, corn salsa, and lettuce delivers approximately 735 calories with 25 grams of protein. While the protein content is lower than meat-based options, the combination of soy (sofritas) and legumes (beans) provides a complete amino acid profile.

Paleo at Chipotle

Paleo dieters avoid grains, legumes, dairy, and processed foods. At Chipotle, this means skipping rice, beans, cheese, sour cream, queso, and the flour tortilla. What remains is surprisingly satisfying if you focus on the high-quality protein and produce.

A paleo Chipotle bowl typically includes a meat protein (chicken, steak, barbacoa, or carnitas), fajita veggies, guacamole, pico de gallo, green salsa, and romaine lettuce. This combination runs approximately 430 to 480 calories depending on the protein choice, with 25 to 35 grams of protein and healthy fats from the guacamole.

Carnitas is an interesting paleo choice because its higher fat content provides satiety without the dairy-based additions. A bowl with carnitas, double fajita veggies, guacamole, and all salsas creates a filling meal at approximately 510 calories.

High-Fiber at Chipotle

Fiber is chronically underconsumed in the American diet, with most adults eating half the recommended 25 to 38 grams per day. A Chipotle bowl can be an excellent fiber source when built intentionally.

The highest fiber ingredients are black beans (12g per serving), pinto beans (11g), brown rice (2g), fajita veggies (2g), and corn salsa (2g). A bowl with both bean types, brown rice, and vegetable-based toppings delivers approximately 29 grams of fiber in a single meal, meeting or exceeding most daily targets.

Sodium Considerations at Chipotle

While this calculator focuses on calories and macronutrients, sodium deserves attention for anyone monitoring cardiovascular health or blood pressure. A typical Chipotle bowl contains between 1,200 and 2,500 mg of sodium depending on ingredient selection. The American Heart Association recommends no more than 2,300 mg per day, with an ideal limit of 1,500 mg for most adults.

The highest sodium items on the Chipotle menu include the flour tortilla (680 mg), chicken (310 mg), barbacoa (390 mg), and queso (410 mg). Rice and beans contribute moderate amounts (310 to 370 mg each). Salsas are relatively low in sodium compared to their flavor contribution.

If you are watching sodium intake, the most effective strategy is choosing a bowl over a burrito (eliminating the tortilla saves 680 mg alone), selecting steak over barbacoa (120 mg less), and skipping queso. These three changes can reduce total sodium by over 1,000 mg while maintaining a complete, satisfying meal.

Understanding Portion Variability

One important caveat about calorie counting at any restaurant is portion variability. The calorie values used in this calculator represent standard serving sizes as published by Chipotle. In practice, the actual amount of each ingredient placed in your bowl or burrito varies depending on the employee preparing your order, the time of day, and even the specific location.

Studies analyzing fast-casual restaurant portions have found that actual serving sizes can vary by 15% to 30% from the published standard. At Chipotle specifically, the protein portion (often the most calorie-dense component per gram) can range from slightly under to significantly over the stated serving size.

This variability means that the calorie count generated by this tool should be treated as an estimate rather than an exact figure. For most nutrition goals, this level of precision is sufficient. If you are tracking calories with extreme precision for a bodybuilding competition or medical reason, consider weighing your food at home on a kitchen scale after ordering.

Several factors influence portion generosity. Ordering during off-peak hours tends to result in larger portions because employees are less rushed. Being friendly and making eye contact (rather than staring at your phone) sometimes leads to more generous servings. Ordering a bowl and asking for a tortilla on the side effectively gets you more food since employees tend to load bowls more heavily than burritos, where overfilling makes wrapping difficult.

Chipotle Beverage Calories

Beverages are an often-overlooked source of calories. A fountain drink from the Chipotle beverage station can add 150 to 250 calories depending on size and choice. A regular Coca-Cola adds approximately 140 calories per 12 ounces, and most fountain cups hold 20 to 32 ounces.

Chipotle's bottled water, sparkling water, and unsweetened iced tea add zero calories. Diet soda and zero-sugar options also contribute negligible calories. If you are tracking total meal calories, switching from a regular soda to water or unsweetened tea eliminates 200+ calories without changing your food order at all.

Chipotle also offers margaritas and beer at some locations. A Patron margarita adds approximately 250 to 300 calories. Beer ranges from 100 to 200 calories per serving depending on the variety. These liquid calories add up quickly and are particularly easy to underestimate because they do not produce the same satiety signals as solid food.

Chipotle Kids Meal Nutrition

Chipotle offers a kids meal that includes a smaller entree (build-your-own taco, quesadilla, or bowl), a side, and a drink. The portions are roughly half the adult serving size for the entree component.

A kids burrito bowl with chicken, rice, and beans runs approximately 300 to 350 calories, making it a reasonable option for children and also for adults who want a lighter meal without ordering full portions of each component. The kids meal provides a way to enjoy Chipotle with better portion control at a lower price point.

Allergen Information at Chipotle

Food allergies require careful attention when ordering at any restaurant, and Chipotle is no exception. Understanding which ingredients contain common allergens helps individuals with food sensitivities make safe choices.

Gluten-containing items include the flour tortilla, the crispy taco shells (corn-based but processed on shared equipment), and the flour used in some preparation processes. The bowl base, rice, beans, proteins, and most toppings are naturally gluten-free, making Chipotle one of the more accessible fast-casual options for individuals with celiac disease or gluten sensitivity. However, cross-contamination is possible since gluten-containing and gluten-free items are prepared in close proximity.

Dairy is present in cheese, sour cream, and queso. All other menu items are dairy-free. The proteins, rice, beans, salsas, guacamole, and lettuce contain no dairy ingredients. Individuals with severe dairy allergies should alert staff to their allergy, as utensils used for cheese and sour cream may contact other ingredients during busy periods.

Soy is present in sofritas (tofu-based) and in the soybean oil used for cooking some items. Individuals with soy allergies should avoid sofritas entirely and inquire about cooking oils used at their specific location.

Tree nut and peanut allergies are less of a concern at Chipotle than at many other restaurants, as no menu items contain tree nuts or peanuts as primary ingredients. However, Chipotle's allergen statement notes that items may come into contact with allergens during preparation and supply chain handling. Individuals with severe nut allergies should exercise the same caution they would at any restaurant.

Experienced Chipotle customers have developed several ordering strategies that increase nutritional value, portion sizes, or flavor combinations beyond what the standard menu suggests.

Ordering a bowl with a tortilla on the side effectively gives you both a burrito and a bowl for the price of a bowl. You get more food total, and you can use the tortilla selectively for some bites while eating the rest as a bowl.

Half-and-half protein combinations are available at most locations. The calorie count for a half-and-half combination falls between the two individual protein counts.

Requesting light portions of rice or beans is an option when you want the flavor contribution without the full calorie load. A half serving of white rice adds approximately 105 calories instead of 210, and a half serving of beans adds roughly 65 calories. This level of customization is not always available during peak hours but is generally accommodated during slower periods.

The vinaigrette dressing served with the Chipotle salad adds approximately 220 calories. Requesting it on the side allows you to control the amount used. Using half the vinaigrette saves 110 calories while still providing plenty of flavor. Alternatively, using salsa verde as a dressing adds only 15 calories for a tangy, lower-calorie alternative.

Chipotle Ingredient Quality and Sourcing

Chipotle has built its brand around the "Food with Integrity" philosophy, which emphasizes responsibly sourced ingredients. While this does not change the calorie content of the food, it does affect the nutritional quality in ways that calorie counting alone does not capture.

Chipotle's chicken comes from suppliers that raise birds without the use of antibiotics. Their beef and pork sources follow similar standards when supply allows, though the company has acknowledged occasional gaps in availability of responsibly raised proteins. This commitment to antibiotic-free meat does not alter macronutrient content but may influence the broader nutritional and environmental impact of your meal.

The rice is cooked with cilantro and lime juice (white rice) or just lime juice (brown rice). Both preparations use rice bran oil. The beans are seasoned with onion, garlic, cumin, and oregano. These preparation methods add minimal calories but significantly improve flavor, which is one reason Chipotle's base ingredients taste more complex than plain rice and beans prepared at home.

Chipotle's guacamole is made fresh daily from Hass avocados, lime juice, cilantro, red onion, jalapeno, and salt. There are no preservatives, artificial colors, or flavor enhancers. The calorie content (230 per serving) comes entirely from whole food sources, primarily the healthy monounsaturated fats in the avocados.

General Principles for Calorie Counting at Restaurants

Chipotle is one of the easiest restaurants for calorie tracking because of its transparent preparation and published nutritional data. However, the principles that make Chipotle trackable apply to eating out in general.

The foundation of restaurant calorie estimation is understanding the components of a dish. Every restaurant meal can be broken down into its base components, just like a Chipotle bowl. A pasta dish consists of pasta (carbs), sauce (variable), protein, and finishing ingredients (cheese, oil, garnishes). Estimating each component separately and summing them produces a more precise total than guessing the calorie count of the entire dish as a single unit.

Cooking methods significantly affect calorie content. Grilled, baked, steamed, and broiled preparations add fewer calories than fried, sauteed, or cream-based preparations. A grilled chicken breast at 165 calories becomes a 350+ calorie item when battered and fried. At Chipotle, most proteins are grilled, making them relatively lean and predictable in calorie content.

Hidden calories in restaurant food come from sources customers rarely see. Butter and oil used in cooking, sugar in dressings and marinades, and cream in sauces can add 200 to 400 invisible calories to an otherwise healthy-looking dish. Chipotle's relatively simple preparation methods reduce these hidden sources, which is one reason its nutritional data is more dependable than that of many full-service restaurants.

When nutritional information is not available (at non-chain restaurants, for example), use these estimation guidelines. A tablespoon of cooking oil adds 120 calories. A tablespoon of butter adds 100 calories. Restaurant portion sizes for proteins typically range from 6 to 8 ounces, not the 3 to 4 ounces listed on nutritional labels. Side dishes like mashed potatoes, rice, or fries at a restaurant typically contain 250 to 400 calories per serving.

The best strategy for maintaining calorie awareness when eating out frequently is to rotate among a small number of restaurants where you have already mapped the nutritional content of your preferred orders. Building a mental database of 5 to 10 dependable restaurant meals with known calorie counts provides flexibility without constant recalculation.

The Nutritional Evolution of the Chipotle Menu

Chipotle's menu has remained remarkably consistent since the company's founding in 1993, which is one reason their nutritional data is well-established and dependable. Unlike many fast-food chains that frequently introduce and retire limited-time offerings, Chipotle's core menu has changed very little over three decades.

The most significant nutritional changes have come from recipe modifications rather than new menu items. In 2020, Chipotle adjusted the seasoning on their chicken to a new recipe that slightly changed the macro profile. The introduction of queso blanco in 2017 added a new calorie-dense topping option. The cauliflower rice option, introduced in some markets, provided a lower-calorie alternative to traditional rice at approximately 40 calories per serving compared to 210 for white or brown rice.

Chipotle's commitment to customization has been consistent throughout its history. This customization model was major when introduced and remains the company's greatest nutritional advantage. By allowing customers to build meals from individual components, Chipotle enables a level of dietary control that most restaurants cannot match. Whether your goal is 250 calories or 1,500 calories, the menu accommodates both extremes and everything between them.

Mindful Eating at Fast-Casual Restaurants

Calorie counting is one approach to managing nutrition, but mindful eating principles can complement the numbers-based approach for a more complete relationship with food. Mindful eating at Chipotle means paying attention to hunger cues, eating slowly, and noticing the flavors and textures of each ingredient rather than rushing through the meal while distracted.

One practical application of mindful eating at Chipotle is the practice of eating half the bowl, pausing for 10 minutes, and then deciding whether you need the rest. A full Chipotle bowl is often 600 to 1,000 calories, and many people are comfortably satisfied after eating half. Saving the remainder for a second meal effectively halves the calorie cost of eating at Chipotle while also reducing your per-meal spending.

The texture variety in a well-built Chipotle bowl supports mindful eating naturally. The contrast between warm protein, cool salsa, creamy guacamole, and crunchy lettuce creates sensory engagement that slows eating pace. Research has consistently shown that slowing eating speed improves satiety signals, reducing total consumption by 10% to 15% compared to fast eating without any calorie counting or portion control.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories are in a Chipotle burrito bowl?

A standard burrito bowl ranges from 500 to 1100+ calories depending on your selections. A typical bowl with chicken, white rice, black beans, and mild salsa comes to approximately 545 calories. Adding guacamole, cheese, and sour cream pushes it over 900 calories.

What is the lowest calorie meal at Chipotle?

A salad base with chicken, fajita veggies, green tomatillo salsa, and romaine lettuce totals approximately 220 calories with 33 grams of protein. This is the leanest complete meal you can build at Chipotle.

How many calories is Chipotle guacamole?

A standard serving of guacamole contains approximately 230 calories, with 22 grams of fat from avocado. While calorie-dense, these are primarily heart-healthy monounsaturated fats. If you are counting calories, consider using half a portion.

What is the highest protein option at Chipotle?

Chicken provides the highest protein per serving at 32 grams for 180 calories. For maximum protein, order double chicken in a bowl with black beans for 71+ grams of protein total.

How many calories are in a Chipotle tortilla?

The flour burrito tortilla adds 320 calories, 50 grams of carbs, and 10 grams of fat. Switching from a burrito to a bowl eliminates these calories entirely while keeping all other ingredients identical.

Is Chipotle good for weight loss?

Yes, when ordered strategically. A bowl with chicken, fajita veggies, salsa, and lettuce provides a filling, high-protein meal under 300 calories. Adding rice and beans brings it to a satisfying 500-calorie range that fits most deficit plans.

How many calories are in Chipotle chips and guac?

Chips contain approximately 540 calories. Combined with guacamole at 230 calories, the total is roughly 770 calories. This single side item can exceed the calorie count of many complete entree bowls.

What is the macro breakdown of Chipotle chicken?

A serving of Chipotle chicken contains 180 calories, 32g protein, 1g carbs, and 7g fat. It has the best protein-to-calorie ratio of any protein on the Chipotle menu.

Does brown rice have fewer calories than white at Chipotle?

No. Both white and brown rice at Chipotle contain approximately 210 calories per serving. The nutritional difference is in fiber content (brown rice has 2g more) and glycemic index, not total calories.

How do I order a high-protein Chipotle meal?

Order a bowl with double chicken (64g protein), black beans (7g), and skip high-fat toppings like cheese, sour cream, and guac. Add rice for energy. This combination provides 75+ grams of protein at approximately 700 calories.

How many calories are in Chipotle queso?

Queso blanco contains approximately 120 calories per serving with 8g fat, 5g carbs, and 5g protein. It is a moderate calorie addition compared to guacamole (230 cal) but higher than salsa options (15-80 cal).

Can I eat Chipotle on keto?

Yes. Order a bowl with no rice, no beans, and no tortilla. Add your protein of choice, fajita veggies, cheese, sour cream, and guacamole. This combination keeps total carbs under 15 grams while providing plenty of fat and protein for ketosis.

How many calories are in a Chipotle burrito with everything?

A fully loaded burrito with white rice, black beans, chicken, queso, sour cream, cheese, and guacamole contains approximately 1,300-1,500 calories. The tortilla alone adds 320 calories. Switching to a bowl eliminates that 320-calorie tortilla.

Q

Is Chipotle guacamole worth the extra calories?

Chipotle guacamole adds 230 calories and 22g of fat per serving. However, it is primarily heart-healthy monounsaturated fat from avocados, plus 4g fiber and potassium. If you are counting calories, use half a portion. If you are tracking macros for healthy fats, guacamole is one of the better calorie sources on the menu.
