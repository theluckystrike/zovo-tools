How Recipe Scaling Works

Recipe scaling is more nuanced than simple multiplication. According to Wikipedia's article on recipe scaling, the relationship between ingredients isn't always linear - especially in baking where chemical reactions are involved. I've this scaler to handle the practical side: you don't see "0.0625 cups" when "1 tablespoon" communicates the same quantity far more clearly.

The smart conversion system in this tool follows a hierarchy: it won't show awkward decimal cups when tablespoons or teaspoons are more practical. It can't solve every edge case in cooking, but for the vast majority of scaling tasks, it provides measurements that actually make sense in a real kitchen. I don't think most recipe scalers handle this well - they just multiply and dump a decimal on you.
