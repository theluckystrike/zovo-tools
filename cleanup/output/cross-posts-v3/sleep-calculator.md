I used to set my alarm for a random time and wonder why some mornings I felt great after 6 hours and other mornings I was groggy after 8. Turns out, sleep cycles matter more than total hours. So I built a calculator to figure out the right wake-up times.

## What It Does

Tell it when you want to wake up, and it calculates the best times to fall asleep based on 90-minute sleep cycles. Or tell it when you're going to sleep, and it tells you the optimal times to set your alarm. The goal is to wake up at the end of a sleep cycle rather than in the middle of one, which is what causes that awful groggy feeling.

It accounts for the average 14 minutes it takes to fall asleep, and shows you options for 3, 4, 5, or 6 full cycles (4.5 to 9 hours).

## The Science Behind Sleep Cycles

Sleep happens in roughly 90-minute cycles, each moving through stages: light sleep, deep sleep, and REM. Waking up during deep sleep (stage 3) is when you feel terrible, even if you've slept "enough" hours. Waking at the end of a REM period — when the cycle completes — feels dramatically better.

This isn't complicated math, but having a tool that does it instantly is nicer than doing mental arithmetic when you're already tired and about to go to bed.

## No Tracking, Because Obviously

A sleep tool shouldn't track your sleep schedule. Your bedtime habits are personal data. This calculator runs in your browser with zero server communication. No cookies, no analytics, no account. Enter your time, get your answer, done.
