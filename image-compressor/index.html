Large image files slow down websites, eat through storage quotas, and make email attachments unwieldy. Our free online image compressor solves all of these problems in seconds, directly inside your web browser. There is nothing to download, no account to create, and absolutely no data is ever transmitted to a server. Every pixel of every image stays on your device from start to finish.

Whether you are a web developer optimizing page-load performance, a photographer preparing a client gallery, a blogger formatting posts, or simply someone trying to free up storage space on a phone, this tool gives you precise control over the trade-off between visual quality and file size. Why Image Compression Matters

Modern cameras and smartphones produce images that range from 3 MB to well over 20 MB each. While that level of detail is wonderful for printing, it is wildly excessive for most digital uses. A hero image on a landing page only needs to look crisp at screen resolution, and an email thumbnail certainly does not need to weigh 8 MB.

Page speed is a confirmed ranking signal for Google, and images are almost always the heaviest elements on a page. Shaving even 40% off your image payload can meaningfully improve your Core Web Vitals scores, especially Largest Contentful Paint (LCP). In e-commerce, faster pages directly correlate with higher conversion rates — studies consistently show that every additional second of load time can reduce conversions by 7% or more.

How the Compressor Works

When you drop an image into the tool, the browser decodes it into an in-memory bitmap using the native Image API. That raw bitmap is then drawn onto an HTML5 Canvas element at either the original resolution or a resized resolution, depending on your settings. Finally, the Canvas toBlob method re-encodes the bitmap into your chosen output format (JPEG, PNG, or WebP) at the quality level you specify.

The quality slider maps to the compression parameter of the encoder. For JPEG and WebP, a value of 80 typically produces a file that is 60–75% smaller than the original with virtually no perceptible loss of detail. Dropping to 60 saves even more space and is usually acceptable for thumbnails and social-media previews. PNG compression is lossless, so the quality slider has a smaller effect, but converting a heavy PNG to WebP at quality 80 can reduce its size by 80% or more.

Choosing the Right Format

JPEG is the universal standard. Every browser, email client, and social platform supports it. Choose JPEG when compatibility is your top priority and your images are photographs or complex scenes with smooth gradients.

WebP delivers the best compression-to-quality ratio of the three. It is supported by all modern browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari 14+, Edge). If your audience is on reasonably current software, WebP is almost always the optimal choice for web delivery.

PNG is best when you need lossless quality or transparency. Icons, logos, screenshots with sharp text, and UI elements are ideal candidates for PNG. Keep in mind that PNG files are inherently larger than lossy formats at equivalent visual quality.

Resize Options Explained

Resizing and compressing at the same time is one of the most effective ways to reduce file size. A 4000×3000 photograph rescaled to 1920×1440 before JPEG encoding will typically be 70–85% smaller than the original file, even at quality 90. This tool offers two resize modes.

Max Dimensions lets you set a maximum width and height in pixels. Any image larger than those bounds is scaled down proportionally (when "Maintain aspect ratio" is checked). Images already smaller than the limits are left at their original resolution.

Percentage scales every image by a fixed factor. Setting 50% halves both width and height, which reduces total pixel count to 25% of the original — a dramatic file-size reduction even before compression is applied.
