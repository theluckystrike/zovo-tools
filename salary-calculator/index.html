How to Use This Salary Calculator

This salary calculator helps you convert between different pay frequencies and estimate your take-home pay after taxes. Whether you are evaluating a new job offer, negotiating a raise, or just trying to understand your paycheck, this tool gives you a clear picture of your earnings.

Start by selecting your pay type. If you know your hourly rate, select "Hourly" and enter your rate. If you have an annual salary, select "Annual" and enter the full amount. The calculator converts between all frequencies automatically.

Understanding Salary Conversions

Converting between different pay periods is straightforward once you know the math. An hourly rate multiplied by hours per week and then by 52 gives you the annual salary. Dividing the annual figure by 12 gives monthly, by 26 gives bi-weekly, and by 52 gives weekly.

For example, if you earn $30 per hour and work 40 hours per week, your annual gross salary is $62,400. That breaks down to $5,200 per month, $2,400 per bi-weekly paycheck, and $1,200 per week. These figures represent gross pay before any taxes or deductions.

How Federal Income Tax Works

The United States uses a progressive tax system, meaning different portions of your income are taxed at different rates. For 2024, the federal tax brackets for single filers are: 10% on income up to $11,600, 12% on income from $11,601 to $47,150, 22% on income from $47,151 to $100,525, 24% on income from $100,526 to $191,950, 32% on income from $191,951 to $243,725, 35% on income from $243,726 to $609,350, and 37% on income above $609,350.

Many people misunderstand how tax brackets work. Moving into a higher bracket does not mean all of your income gets taxed at that higher rate. Only the portion of income within each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. This means a raise will never result in less take-home pay due to higher taxes.

Social Security and Medicare

Every worker in the United States pays FICA taxes, which fund Social Security and Medicare. The Social Security tax rate is 6.2% on wages up to $168,600 (for 2024). Your employer also pays 6.2%, making the total 12.4%. The Medicare tax rate is 1.45% with no wage cap. High earners pay an additional 0.9% Medicare surtax on wages above $200,000 for single filers.

These taxes are automatically deducted from every paycheck. They are separate from income tax and apply regardless of your filing status or deductions. Self-employed individuals pay both the employee and employer portions, totaling 15.3%.

State Income Taxes

State income tax varies significantly across the country. Nine states have no income tax at all: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. On the other end, states like California (up to 13.3%), Hawaii (up to 11%), and New Jersey (up to 10.75%) have some of the highest state income tax rates.

Where you live has a major impact on your take-home pay. Someone earning $100,000 in Texas takes home significantly more than someone earning the same amount in California, simply due to the state tax difference. This is worth considering when comparing job offers in different locations.

Pre-Tax Deductions and Their Impact

Pre-tax deductions reduce your taxable income, which means you pay less in taxes. Common pre-tax deductions include 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums, HSA contributions, and FSA contributions. For example, contributing $6,000 per year to a traditional 401(k) on a $60,000 salary means you only pay income tax on $54,000.

This calculator includes a field for pre-tax deductions so you can see how contributions to retirement accounts or health plans affect your take-home pay. The reduction in taxes partially offsets the cost of the deduction, making pre-tax benefits an efficient way to save.

Overtime Pay Calculations

The Fair Labor Standards Act requires that non-exempt employees receive overtime pay of at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek. This calculator includes an overtime hours field that applies the standard 1.5x multiplier.

If you regularly work overtime, this can significantly increase your earnings. For instance, working 5 hours of overtime per week at a $25/hour base rate adds an additional $9,750 per year ($25 x 1.5 x 5 x 52)., overtime income is also subject to all applicable taxes.

Tips for Salary Negotiations

When evaluating a job offer, always look at the total compensation package rather than just the base salary. Benefits like health insurance, retirement matching, paid time off, and stock options can add 20% to 40% to the value of your base salary.

Research salary ranges for your role and location using sites like Glassdoor, PayScale, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Having data to back up your negotiation makes a stronger case than just asking for more money.

Consider the cost of living in the area. A $70,000 salary in a low-cost city may provide a better quality of life than $100,000 in a high-cost city. Factor in housing, transportation, food, and state taxes when comparing offers in different locations.

Understanding Your Paycheck

Your paycheck stub contains several important numbers. Gross pay is your total earnings for the pay period. Deductions include federal income tax withholding, state income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and any voluntary deductions. Net pay is the amount actually deposited into your bank account.

Your W-4 form determines how much federal tax is withheld from each paycheck. If you consistently receive large tax refunds, you may adjust your W-4 to reduce withholding and increase your take-home pay throughout the year. Conversely, if you owe taxes at filing time, increasing your withholding can prevent a surprise bill.

Which is Better?

Both salaried and hourly positions have advantages. Salaried employees typically receive consistent paychecks, access to benefits, and may have more career advancement opportunities. Hourly employees benefit from overtime pay, more flexibility in scheduling, and direct compensation for every hour worked.

The best option depends on your industry, career goals, and personal preferences. Some high-paying professions like consulting or contracting pay hourly rates that exceed equivalent salaries because they compensate for the lack of benefits and job security.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert hourly wage to annual salary?
Multiply your hourly rate by the number of hours you work per week, then multiply that by 52 weeks. For example, $25/hour at 40 hours/week equals $52,000 per year.
What is gross pay vs net pay?
Gross pay is your total earnings before any deductions. Net pay (take-home pay) is what you actually receive after federal taxes, state taxes, Social Security, Medicare, and other deductions are subtracted.
How much of my salary goes to taxes?
The percentage varies based on your income, filing status, and state. Federal income tax ranges from 10% to 37%. Social Security is 6.2% and Medicare is 1.45%. State taxes vary from 0% to over 13% depending on where you live.
Is this salary calculator accurate?
This calculator provides estimates based on 2024 federal tax brackets and standard deductions. Actual take-home pay may differ based on your specific deductions, credits, pre-tax contributions, and local taxes.
Does this calculator include state taxes?
Yes, you can select your state to include estimated state income tax. States with no income tax (like Texas, Florida, and Nevada) will show 0% state tax.
How do I calculate overtime pay?
Overtime is typically paid at 1.5 times your regular hourly rate for hours worked beyond 40 per week. This calculator includes an optional overtime hours field that applies the 1.5x multiplier.
What is the FICA tax?
FICA stands for the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. It includes Social Security tax (6.2% up to a wage base of $168,600 in 2024) and Medicare tax (1.45% with no wage limit, plus an additional 0.9% on earnings above $200,000).
Can I save my calculations?
Your most recent calculation is automatically saved in your browser. You can also copy the results summary to your clipboard or bookmark the page for quick access.
