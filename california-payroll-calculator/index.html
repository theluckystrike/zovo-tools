How to Use This Tool

Getting your California paycheck estimate is quick. Here is how:

  1. Enter your annual gross salary - Total salary before any deductions. For hourly workers, multiply your rate by 2,080 hours.
  2. Select your filing status - Single, Married Filing Jointly, or Head of Household. This affects both federal and California state tax brackets and standard deductions.
  3. Choose your pay frequency - Weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly. This determines your per-paycheck amount.
  4. Add pre-tax deductions - Include 401(k), HSA, or other pre-tax benefits. These reduce both federal and state taxable income.
  5. Click Calculate - The tool shows federal tax, California state tax, SDI, FICA, and your take-home pay across all periods.

This calculator uses 2024 tax brackets for both federal and California state taxes. Actual paychecks may vary based on W-4/DE 4 elections, employer benefits, and local taxes (some CA cities have additional payroll taxes).

California SDI Tax

California State Disability Insurance (SDI) is a mandatory payroll tax that funds short-term disability benefits and Paid Family Leave (PFL). Here are the key details:

Important changes for 2024: California removed the SDI wage ceiling. Previously, SDI only applied to the first ~$153,000 of wages. Now it applies to all wages with no upper limit. For high earners, this means a significant increase in SDI withholding.

SDI benefits include:

  • Short-Term Disability - Up to 52 weeks of partial wage replacement (60-70% of wages) if you cannot work due to a non-work-related illness, injury, or pregnancy.
  • Paid Family Leave (PFL) - Up to 8 weeks of partial wage replacement to bond with a new child, care for a seriously ill family member, or handle certain military-related events.

SDI is paid entirely by employees. Your employer does not contribute to SDI. If you are self-employed, you can opt into the SDI program through the EDD elective coverage program.

Common Deductions Guide

Understanding your deductions helps you take control of your California paycheck:

Pre-Tax Deductions (Reduce Federal AND State Tax)

  • 401(k) / 403(b) Contributions - Up to $23,000 (2024). Reduces both federal and California taxable income. At a combined 31% marginal rate, $23,000 in 401(k) saves you $7,130 in taxes.
  • HSA Contributions - $4,150 individual / $8,300 family. Important note: California does NOT give HSA contributions a state tax deduction. HSAs reduce federal tax only. You still owe CA tax on HSA contributions.
  • Traditional IRA - Up to $7,000 ($8,000 if over 50). Deductible for both federal and state if you meet income limits.
  • FSA - $3,200 healthcare / $5,000 dependent care. Reduces both federal and state taxable income.

California-Specific Considerations

  • HSA State Tax Trap - California is one of only two states (along with New Jersey) that does not recognize HSA tax benefits. You owe CA income tax on HSA contributions and investment gains. Keep records for your state return.
  • State Itemized Deductions - California allows itemized deductions but with different limits than federal. Mortgage interest, property taxes, and charitable donations are deductible on your CA return.
  • Renter's Credit - If you rent in California and your AGI is under $50,746 (single) or $101,492 (married), you get a $60 (single) or $120 (married) nonrefundable credit.

Franchise Tax Board Information

The California Franchise Tax Board (FTB) is the state agency that administers California's income tax program. Here is what you know about dealing with the FTB:

  • Filing Deadline - California follows the federal April 15 deadline for personal income tax returns (Form 540). Extensions to October 15 are available.
  • Estimated Taxes - If you expect to owe $500+ in CA taxes, you must make quarterly estimated payments. Due dates: April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15.
  • Residency Rules - California taxes residents on worldwide income. Part-year residents and nonresidents are taxed only on California-source income. The FTB is known for aggressively auditing people who claim to have left California.
  • Penalties - Late filing penalty is 5% per month (up to 25%). Late payment penalty is 5% of the balance. Interest accrues from the original due date.
  • FTB Website - ftb.ca.gov - File returns, make payments, check refund status, and access tax forms online.

California has one of the most aggressive state tax enforcement programs in the country. The FTB actively tracks people who leave the state and may challenge your residency status if you continue to have California ties (property, bank accounts, professional licenses).

Tax Saving Tips for CA Workers

California's high tax rates make tax planning especially valuable. Here are practical ways to keep more of your income:

  1. Max out your 401(k) - At a 31%+ combined marginal rate (federal 22% + CA 9.3%), a full $23,000 contribution saves $7,130+ in taxes. That is more impact than in lower-tax states.
  2. Use FSA instead of HSA for state tax savings - Since California taxes HSA contributions but not FSA contributions, an FSA may be more tax-efficient for predictable medical expenses in California.
  3. Claim the CalEITC - California has its own Earned Income Tax Credit for workers earning up to $30,950. Combined with the federal EITC, low-income workers can receive substantial credits.
  4. Track work-from-home expenses - If you are self-employed, California allows deductions for home office expenses, internet, and equipment on your state return.
  5. Consider municipal bonds - Interest from California municipal bonds is exempt from both federal and California state tax. For high earners, the after-tax yield can exceed taxable alternatives.
  6. Bunch charitable deductions - If you are near the standard deduction threshold, consider "bunching" two years of donations into one year to itemize, then taking the standard deduction the next year.
  7. Review your DE 4 - California's withholding form (DE 4) is separate from the federal W-4. Make sure both are optimized to avoid over-withholding or owing at tax time.

Retirement in California

California taxes most retirement income at the regular state tax rates, with one notable exception:

  • Social Security - Not taxed by California. This is one of the few tax benefits for CA retirees.
  • 401(k) and IRA Distributions - Fully taxable at California income tax rates. A $60,000 annual withdrawal from a traditional 401(k) would face roughly $2,100 in CA state tax.
  • Pension Income - Fully taxable. CalPERS, CalSTRS, and private pensions are all subject to California income tax.
  • Capital Gains - California taxes capital gains as ordinary income. There is no special capital gains rate like at the federal level. This means long-term gains face up to 13.3% state tax.

Many Californians choose to retire in no-income-tax states like Nevada, Arizona, or Texas to avoid state taxes on their retirement income. However, California's FTB is known to pursue people who claim to have moved but maintain California ties. If you plan to leave, make a clean break: sell property, change your driver's license, register to vote in your new state, and update all financial accounts.

CalSavers

California's CalSavers program is a state-mandated retirement savings plan for workers whose employers do not offer a 401(k) or similar plan. Employers with 5+ employees must either offer their own retirement plan or enroll workers in CalSavers. Contributions are to a Roth IRA (after-tax), starting at 5% of pay with annual auto-escalation. Workers can opt out at any time.

Major California Industries and Pay

California has the largest state economy in the US and would rank as the fifth largest economy in the world if it were a country. Here is a breakdown of the key sectors:

Technology (Silicon Valley and Beyond)

California is the global center of the tech industry. Silicon Valley companies like Apple, Google, Meta, and Nvidia are headquartered here. Software engineers in San Francisco/San Jose earn $150,000-$250,000+ in total compensation. However, the high cost of living and state taxes mean your purchasing power may not match the raw salary number. A $200,000 salary in San Francisco leaves you with roughly $131,584 after all taxes - before Bay Area rent takes another $38,400+ for a one-bedroom.

Entertainment and Media

Los Angeles is the entertainment capital of the world. The industry spans film, television, music, gaming, and streaming. Salaries vary wildly - from $40,000 for production assistants to millions for established talent. The average entertainment industry salary in LA is about $75,000-$90,000 for non-talent roles.

Agriculture

California produces over one-third of US vegetables and two-thirds of US fruits and nuts. The Central Valley is the agricultural heartland. Farm workers earn $15-$20/hour, while farm managers earn $55,000-$85,000. Agricultural engineers and food scientists command $75,000-$120,000.

Healthcare and Biotech

California has a massive healthcare sector, with major hospital systems and biotech hubs in San Diego, San Francisco, and Los Angeles. Registered nurses earn $80,200 median (among the highest in the US), pharmacists $130,000-$150,000, and biotech researchers $90,000-$140,000. The high wages partly reflect the high cost of living in California metro areas.

Defense and Aerospace

Southern California is home to major defense contractors including Northrop Grumman, Raytheon, and L3Harris. Edwards Air Force Base and Vandenberg Space Force Base are also in California. Aerospace engineers earn $110,000-$160,000, and the security clearance premium adds 10-20% to base salaries.

California Quarterly Estimated Payments

Self-employed California workers must make quarterly estimated payments to both the IRS and the California Franchise Tax Board. The FTB has its own payment schedule and expects 30% of the annual liability by April 15, 40% by June 15, 0% by September 15, and 30% by January 15. Missing these deadlines triggers estimated tax penalties.

LLC Fees in California

If you operate through an LLC in California, you owe an annual minimum franchise tax of $800 regardless of income. LLCs with gross revenue over $250,000 owe additional fees: $900 for revenue of $250K-$500K, $2,500 for $500K-$1M, $6,000 for $1M-$5M, and $11,790 for over $5M. This is a significant cost that does not exist in Texas or Florida.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the California state income tax brackets for 2024?

California has 9 brackets from 1% to 12.3%, plus a 1% Mental Health Services Tax on income over $1 million (total top rate: 13.3%). For single filers, the 1% bracket covers income up to $10,412. The 9.3% bracket applies from $68,351 to $349,137, which is where most middle-class earners fall.

How much is California SDI tax?

SDI is 1.1% of all wages in 2024. There is no longer a wage ceiling - it applies to your entire income. On an $85,000 salary, SDI costs $935 per year. SDI funds disability benefits and Paid Family Leave.

Does California tax Social Security benefits?

No. California does not tax Social Security benefits. This is one tax break for California retirees. However, all other retirement income (401k, IRA, pensions) is fully taxable at regular state rates.

What is the California standard deduction?

The 2024 California standard deduction is $5,540 for single filers and $11,080 for married filing jointly. This is much lower than the federal standard deduction ($14,600/$29,200), which means more of your income is taxable at the state level.

How does California tax compare to other states?

California has the highest state income tax in the nation at 13.3%. On a $100,000 salary, a CA worker takes home about $6,065 less per year than a Texas or Florida worker. However, CA provides SDI/PFL benefits that most other states do not offer.

What is the California minimum wage?

California's minimum wage is $16.00/hour statewide as of 2024. Fast food workers at chains with 60+ locations earn $20.00/hour. Some cities set higher rates: San Francisco ($18.67), Los Angeles ($16.78), and Mountain View ($18.75).

Do I pay California taxes if I work remotely from another state?

If you work entirely from another state for a California employer, you generally do not owe CA tax. But if you perform any work within California, that income may be taxable. The FTB aggressively enforces these rules, especially for high earners.

What is the Mental Health Services Tax in California?

The Mental Health Services Tax (Proposition 63) adds 1% on taxable income over $1 million. Combined with the top 12.3% bracket, the effective maximum rate is 13.3%. It funds community mental health programs across California.
