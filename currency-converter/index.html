Understanding Currency Exchange

Currency exchange rates represent the value of one currency in terms of another. These rates are influenced by interest rates set by central banks, inflation rates, political stability, economic performance, and trade balances between countries.

About These Rates

The rates shown in this converter are realistic reference rates based on recent market data. They are intended for estimation and educational purposes. For actual financial transactions, always verify the current rate with your bank, credit card company, or exchange service.

Tips for Currency Exchange

When exchanging currency, compare rates from multiple providers. Airport and hotel exchanges typically offer the worst rates. Online transfer services often provide rates closer to the mid-market rate. Using a credit card with no foreign transaction fee is usually the most cost-effective way to pay abroad.

Major Currency Pairs

The foreign exchange market (forex) is the largest financial market in the world with over $7 trillion in daily trading volume. The most actively traded pairs are EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, and USD/CHF, which typically have the tightest spreads and most liquidity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these exchange rates live?

These rates are realistic reference rates based on recent market data but are not live. For actual transactions, always check with your bank or exchange service for the current rate and any applicable fees.

What is an exchange rate?

An exchange rate is the price of one currency expressed in terms of another. For example, a USD/EUR rate of 0.92 means 1 US dollar equals 0.92 euros. Rates fluctuate based on economic factors, interest rates, and market conditions.

Why do banks offer different rates than market rates?

Banks add a spread (markup) to the mid-market rate to make a profit. This spread can range from 1-5% depending on the provider, currency pair, and transaction size.

What are the most traded currency pairs?

The most traded pairs are EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, and USD/CHF. Together these four pairs account for over 50% of all forex trading volume globally.
