Markup vs Margin: What's the Difference?

Markup

Markup is the percentage added to the cost price to determine the selling price. It tells you how much more you charge relative to what you paid.

Markup % = (Profit / Cost) x 100

Margin

Margin is the percentage of the selling price that is profit. It tells you what portion of your revenue is actual profit.

Margin % = (Profit / Selling Price) x 100

A 50% markup equals a 33.3% margin. A 100% markup equals a 50% margin. Margin is always lower than markup for the same transaction.

Markup vs Margin Explained

Understanding Markup and Margin in Business

Markup and margin are two fundamental pricing concepts that every business owner, accountant, and salesperson needs to understand clearly. While they both relate to profit, they calculate that profit differently and confusing the two can lead to significant pricing errors. According to Wikipedia's definition of markup, markup is the difference between the cost of a good and its selling price, expressed as a percentage of the cost. Margin, on the other hand, expresses profit as a percentage of the selling price (revenue).

The distinction matters more than most people realize. If a product costs $60 and sells for $100, the profit is $40. The markup is 66.7% ($40/$60), while the margin is 40% ($40/$100). A business that targets a 40% markup when it means a 40% margin would significantly underprice its products, earning only 28.6% margin instead of the intended 40%. This kind of confusion has real financial consequences and is one of the most common pricing mistakes small businesses make.

How to Calculate Markup

Markup percentage is calculated by dividing the profit (selling price minus cost) by the cost price, then multiplying by 100. The formula is: Markup % = ((Selling Price - Cost) / Cost) x 100. To find the selling price from a known cost and desired markup: Selling Price = Cost x (1 + Markup% / 100). For example, if an item costs $50 and you want a 75% markup, the selling price would be $50 x 1.75 = $87.50.

How to Calculate Margin

Margin percentage is calculated by dividing the profit by the selling price, then multiplying by 100. The formula is: Margin % = ((Selling Price - Cost) / Selling Price) x 100. To find the selling price from a known cost and desired margin: Selling Price = Cost / (1 - Margin% / 100). For example, if an item costs $50 and you want a 40% margin, the selling price would be $50 / 0.60 = $83.33. For more discussion on pricing formulas, see Stack Overflow pricing discussions and Hacker News threads on SaaS pricing.

Industry Standard Markups

Markup percentages vary significantly by industry. Grocery stores typically operate on very thin markups of 10-30%, relying on high volume. Clothing retailers often use markups of 100-300% (keystone pricing uses a 100% markup, doubling the cost). Restaurants generally mark up food 200-400% and beverages even higher. Software and digital products can have markups exceeding 1000% because the marginal cost of each additional unit is near zero. Understanding your industry's typical markups helps you price competitively while maintaining profitability.
