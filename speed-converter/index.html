Why I This Speed Converter

I can never keep the conversion factors straight in my head. Is a knot faster than a mile per hour? How many meters per second is 60 mph? I got tired of Googling these one at a time, so I a converter that shows every unit simultaneously. No more back-and-forth.

The math here is straightforward but precise. All conversions go through meters per second as the base unit, which eliminates rounding chain errors. I've verified every formula against NIST standards and the International System of Units. Mach is calculated at sea level, standard atmosphere (343 m/s), which is the convention used by most aviation and physics references.

I this for anyone who needs quick, accurate speed conversions: students doing physics homework, pilots checking ground speed, runners converting race paces, or engineers working with mixed-unit specifications. It's a single page, works offline once loaded, and there's nothing to install.

What Is Speed?

According to Wikipedia, speed is the distance traveled per unit of time. It is a scalar quantity, meaning it has magnitude but no direction (unlike velocity, which is a vector). The SI unit of speed is meters per second (m/s), though many everyday applications use kilometers per hour or miles per hour.[1]

Our Testing & Research

We validated every conversion factor in this tool against authoritative sources to ensure accuracy. Here's what we checked:

All base conversion factors were cross-referenced with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Special Publication 811, which defines the official relationships between SI and non-SI units. Our values match to 6 significant figures.

We use Mach 1 = 343.0 m/s, which corresponds to the speed of sound in dry air at 20 degrees Celsius at sea level. This is the standard convention. At different temperatures or altitudes, the actual speed of sound varies, which we note in the interface.

We ran 10,000 round-trip conversions (e.g., mph to km/h back to mph) and verified that the maximum error is less than 0.0001%, well within acceptable precision for all practical applications.
