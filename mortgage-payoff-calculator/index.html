Mortgage Payoff Strategies

There are several proven strategies to pay off your mortgage faster. The right approach depends on your financial situation, interest rate, and personal goals.

Extra Monthly Payments

Adding even $100-$200 extra per month to your mortgage payment directly reduces principal, which reduces the total interest paid over the life of the loan. This is the most common and flexible strategy.

Biweekly Payments

By splitting your monthly payment in half and paying every two weeks, you make 26 half-payments per year (equal to 13 full payments). This painlessly adds one extra payment per year.

Annual Lump Sum

Using a tax refund, bonus, or other windfall to make a yearly lump sum payment can significantly accelerate payoff. Even one large payment per year compounds over time.

Refinancing

If current rates are lower than your existing rate, refinancing can reduce both your monthly payment and total interest. Factor in closing costs and how long you plan to stay in the home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Performance data from our testing across common use cases

Q How much can I save by making extra mortgage payments?

Even small extra payments can save thousands in interest and shave years off your mortgage. For example, adding $200/month to a $300,000 mortgage at 6.5% can save over $80,000 in interest and pay off the loan nearly 7 years early.

Q Is it better to make biweekly or monthly mortgage payments?

Biweekly payments result in 26 half-payments per year, which equals 13 full monthly payments instead of 12. This one extra payment per year can shave 4-6 years off a 30-year mortgage and save tens of thousands in interest without a big change to your budget.

Q Should I pay off my mortgage early or invest?

This depends on your mortgage interest rate versus expected investment returns. If your mortgage rate is higher than your after-tax investment returns, paying it off early makes sense. If your rate is low (under 4-5%), investing may yield better returns. Consider your risk tolerance and the psychological benefit of being debt-free.

Q Are there penalties for paying off a mortgage early?

Some mortgages include prepayment penalties, especially in the first 3-5 years. Check your loan documents or contact your lender. Most conventional loans originated after 2014 do not have prepayment penalties due to Dodd-Frank regulations.

Q How does refinancing compare to making extra payments?

Refinancing lowers your interest rate, reducing total cost. Extra payments reduce the principal faster. The best approach depends on current rates, closing costs, and how long you plan to stay. Sometimes combining both strategies yields the greatest savings.
