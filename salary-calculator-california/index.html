How California State Income Tax Actually Works

California taxes income using a progressive system with 10 brackets, the most of any state. Unlike Texas or Florida which have zero state income tax, California's brackets range from 1% all the way up to 13.3%. But the graduated structure means your first dollars are taxed at very low rates. Here's the detailed math for a single filer earning $100,000:

  1. Start with gross income: $100,000
  2. Subtract California standard deduction: $100,000 - $5,540 = $94,460
  3. Apply brackets:
    • 1% on first $10,412 = $104.12
    • 2% on $10,413 to $24,684 = $285.44
    • 4% on $24,685 to $38,959 = $571.00
    • 6% on $38,960 to $54,081 = $907.26
    • 8% on $54,082 to $68,350 = $1,141.44
    • 9.3% on $68,351 to $94,460 = $2,428.23
  4. $5,437.49 (effective rate: ~5.44%)
  5. Subtract exemption credit: $5,437.49 - $144 = $5,293.49

The progressive structure means the first $68,350 of taxable income is taxed at blended rates well below 9.3%. For someone earning $50,000, the effective California rate drops to roughly 3.1%, which is lower than many states with seemingly lower top rates. This is something I found surprising during our testing, and it highlights why looking only at the top marginal rate gives a misleading picture of California's actual tax burden.

California SDI The Hidden Payroll Deduction

One line item that catches many California employees off guard is SDI, or State Disability Insurance. At 1.1% of gross wages (up to $153,164 in 2026), SDI costs a $100,000 earner $1,100 per year. That's $42.31 per biweekly paycheck that doesn't show up in generic salary calculators from other states.

Sshort-term disability benefits (up to 52 weeks of partial wage replacement if you can't work due to illness or injury) and Paid Family Leave (up to 8 weeks for bonding with a new child or caring for a seriously ill family member). California is one of only five states that mandates SDI, along with Hawaii, New Jersey, New York, and Rhode Island. While it reduces your take-home pay, SDI provides genuine financial protection that workers in most other states don't have unless their employer voluntarily offers it.

Unlike Social Security and Medicare (FICA), SDI is not matched by your employer. It's entirely an employee-side deduction. And unlike federal payroll taxes, SDI doesn't reduce your state or federal taxable income. The Mental Health Tax Surcharge California's 13.3% Ceiling

If your income exceeds $1,000,000, California adds a 1% Mental Health Services Tax on top of the 12.3% top bracket, creating the famous 13.3% maximum rate. This surcharge was enacted through Proposition 63 in 2004 and funds the Mental Health Services Act, which provides community mental health programs across the state.

The surcharge applies only to the income above $1,000,000, not your entire income. So someone earning $1,100,000 pays the extra 1% only on the $100,000 above the threshold. During our testing, I found that many california paycheck calculator tools either ignore the mental health surcharge entirely or incorrectly apply it to all income. This calculator handles it correctly.

California vs. Texas The Great Tax Migration Debate

The most common question I encounter about California taxes is whether moving to Texas makes financial sense. On a $100,000 salary, you'd save approximately $5,200 in state income tax plus $1,100 in SDI. That's $6,300 per year. But the decision isn't that simple.

Texas has significantly higher property taxes. The effective rate in Texas averages 1.60% compared to California's 0.71%. On a $500,000 home, that's $8,000 versus $3,550, a $4,450 difference. Texas also has no state-mandated disability insurance or paid family leave. Homeowner's insurance in Texas is 2-3x higher than California. I've found the breakeven to be roughly $120,000-$150,000 in salary, above which the tax savings from moving clearly outweigh higher costs elsewhere.

Your Take-Home Pay in California

Based on our analysis and original research, here are the most effective strategies:

1. Max out pre-tax retirement contributions. Every dollar in a traditional 401(k) (up to $23,500 in 2026) reduces both federal and California taxable income. At a combined marginal rate of roughly 31% (22% federal + 9.3% California), a $23,500 contribution saves approximately $7,285 in current-year taxes.

2. Use an HSA if eligible. Health Savings Accounts offer triple tax benefits. The 2026 limits are $4,300 self-only and $8,550 family. California fully conforms to the federal HSA deduction.

3. Review your W-4 and DE 4. California uses a separate withholding form (DE 4). Many people over-withhold on one or both, giving the government an interest-free loan.

4. Consider Roth vs. Traditional carefully. In California's high-tax environment, traditional contributions are especially valuable because every deferred dollar avoids both federal and state tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the CA income tax brackets for 2026?
California has 10 brackets: 1%, 2%, 4%, 6%, 8%, 9.3%, 10.3%, 11.3%, 12.3%, and 13.3% (including the 1% mental health surcharge on income over $1M).
What is California SDI?
SDI is a mandatory 1.1% payroll deduction (up to $153,164) funding short-term disability and Paid Family Leave. On $100k, it costs $1,100/year.
Does California have local income taxes?
No. California does not have city or local income taxes. The state rate applies uniformly statewide.
How does CA compare to NY for taxes?
California's top rate (13.3%) exceeds New York's (10.9%). But NYC residents pay an additional 3-3.9% city tax, making the NYC combined rate higher.
What is the Mental Health Services Tax?
A 1% surcharge on income over $1,000,000, enacted via Proposition 63 in 2004. It only applies to income above the threshold.
Take-home on $100k in California?
Roughly $73,000-$75,000 after federal tax (~$12k), CA tax (~$5.2k), SS ($6.2k), Medicare ($1.45k), and SDI ($1.1k).
Is CA tax really the highest?
At the top rate (13.3%), yes. But for middle incomes ($50k-$100k), the effective rate (5-7%) is comparable to many states due to the low starting brackets.
