Interbank vs. Open Market Rate Explained

Understanding the difference between interbank and open market rates is essential when converting usd to pakistani rupee amounts. I've researched this and here's what you know:

Interbank Rate

The wholesale rate used between banks and financial institutions. Set by market forces and influenced by the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP). This is the "official" rate you see on financial news sites. As of March 2026: approximately 278 PKR/USD.

Open Market Rate

The rate offered by money changers and exchange dealers. Typically 1-3 PKR higher than the interbank rate. This is what you'd get at an exchange booth in Pakistan. As of March 2026: approximately 280-281 PKR/USD.

The gap between these rates has narrowed significantly since 2026, when the SBP cracked down on the informal currency market. At the peak of the currency crisis in mid-2026, the gap was as large as 10-15 PKR. The convergence is a positive sign of market stabilization. For remittances, services like Wise and Remitly typically offer rates between the interbank and open market rates.

Economic Context Understanding the USD/PKR Relationship

I've spent considerable time analyzing Pakistan's economic situation, and the USD/PKR story is one of the most dramatic currency movements in recent memory. Here's a overview based on our testing of how macro factors affect the exchange rate:

The 2022-2026 Currency Crisis

The PKR's most dramatic period came between January 2022 and July 2026, when the rate went from 176 to 283 - a depreciation of over 60% in just 18 months. This was driven by a storm of factors: political instability (the no-confidence vote against PM Imran Khan), an energy crisis that spiked the import bill, dwindling foreign reserves (dropping below $4 billion, barely covering one month of imports), and difficult IMF bailout negotiations that delayed critical funding.

The crisis peaked when Pakistan adopted a "market-based" exchange rate as a condition of the IMF standby arrangement, effectively allowing the rupee to find its natural level. Inflation hit 38% year-over-year in May 2026, further pressuring the currency. The State Bank of Pakistan raised its policy rate to 22% - one of the highest in the world - to combat inflation and defend the currency.

Stabilization (2026-2026)

Since mid-2026, the PKR has shown remarkable stabilization, hovering between 275-280 against the dollar. Several factors contributed: successful completion of the IMF standby arrangement, rebuilding of foreign reserves (back above $8 billion), declining inflation (from 38% to under 10%), improved trade balance through import restrictions, and growing remittance inflows. Pakistan's remittance corridors - particularly from Saudi Arabia, UAE, UK, and the US - provide crucial dollar inflows that support the rupee.

Why the PKR Won't Return to 160

Many Pakistanis hope the rupee will strengthen back toward pre-2022 levels. Based on our research, this is extremely unlikely. Currency depreciation driven by persistent inflation differentials is largely permanent. Pakistan's cumulative inflation since 2020 has been approximately 80-90%, while US inflation over the same period was roughly 20%. This inflation gap of 60-70% roughly corresponds to the ~75% depreciation from 160 to 278. The math aligns with purchasing power parity theory. For the PKR to return to 160, Pakistan would need years of lower inflation than the US - a scenario no major forecaster predicts.

Key Factors Affecting USD/PKR

  • IPakistan's ongoing engagement with the IMF is the single biggest factor for currency stability. Non-compliance with fiscal targets could trigger another depreciation episode.
  • Pakistan imports virtually all its oil. Rising oil prices widen the trade deficit and weaken the rupee.
  • Over $30 billion annually. Any disruption to remittance flows (recession in Gulf states, regulatory changes) would pressure the currency.
  • The SBP's reserves level determines its capacity to intervene in the forex market. Below $5 billion is considered critical.
  • Pakistan's political environment directly affects investor confidence and FDI inflows.
  • Higher rates attract foreign portfolio investment but slow domestic growth. The SBP faces a constant balancing act.

Expert Tips for USD to PKR Transfers

Over the past few years, I've helped several friends and colleagues their dollar to pkr transfers. Here's what I've learned through our testing:

1. Don't use your bank for wire transfers. Traditional bank wires cost $25-45 per transfer plus a 2-3% rate markup. On a $1,000 transfer, you're losing $45-75 in hidden costs. Wise and Remitly can't match the convenience of your bank app, but they deliver significantly more rupees.

2. Consider JazzCash and Easypaisa for mobile wallet delivery. These Pakistani mobile money platforms partner with international remittance services. If your recipient doesn't have a traditional bank account (common in Pakistan, where bank penetration is under 30%), mobile wallet delivery is the fastest and most convenient option. The fees are slightly higher than Wise, but the convenience is worth it for smaller transfers.

3. Monitor the interbank rate. The State Bank of Pakistan publishes daily reference rates. Compare any service's offered rate against the SBP interbank rate to calculate the true markup. Anything over 2% is expensive.

4. Batch your transfers. Most services charge a flat fee per transaction. Sending $200 weekly costs more in fees than sending $800 monthly. I've calculated that batching saves $10-20 per month depending on the service. The PKR has been relatively stable since mid-2026, so you're not taking on significant exchange rate risk by consolidating transfers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current USD to PKR rate?
As of March 2026, the reference interbank rate is approximately 1 USD = 278 PKR. The open market rate is 280-281 PKR. Rates vary by provider.
Why has the PKR depreciated so much?
The PKR fell from ~160 in 2020 to ~278 in 2026 due to high inflation (peaking at 38% in 2026), IMF bailout conditions, low foreign reserves, energy import costs, and political instability. The depreciation roughly matches the cumulative inflation differential between Pakistan and the US.
What is the cheapest way to send money to Pakistan?
Wise and Remitly typically deliver the most PKR per dollar. For mobile wallet delivery (JazzCash, Easypaisa), partner services offer competitive rates. Avoid bank wires, which charge $25-45 plus 2-3% markup.
What's the difference between interbank and open market rates?
The interbank rate is the wholesale rate between banks (~278 PKR). The open market rate is what exchange dealers offer (~280-281 PKR). The gap has narrowed significantly since the 2026 SBP crackdown.
How much is $1,000 in Pakistani Rupees?
At 278 PKR/USD, $1,000 equals approximately 278,000 PKR. After provider fees and markup, expect 258,000-275,000 PKR depending on the service used.
