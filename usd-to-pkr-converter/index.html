Key Events in PKR History

The rupee's trajectory tells the story of Pakistan's economic challenges over the past decade. Here are the pivotal moments that drove the biggest moves in the USD/PKR rate.

  • 2018: The PML-N government had maintained an artificially strong rupee near Rs. 105. As foreign reserves drained, the incoming PTI government allowed a series of devaluations, sending the rate from 110 to 139 in a single year.
  • 2019: Pakistan entered a $6 billion IMF Extended Fund Facility. One condition was to let the rupee float more freely. The rate settled around Rs. 155 as the market found a new equilibrium.
  • 2020-2021: Despite the global pandemic, the rupee remained relatively stable at 160-178 thanks to reduced oil imports and strong remittance inflows, partly boosted by the Roshan Digital Account initiative.
  • 2022: Political instability (vote of no-confidence, dissolution of assemblies), soaring global oil and food prices after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and stalled IMF program talks triggered the rupee's sharpest decline, from 178 to 227.
  • 2023: The currency crisis deepened as the SBP fully liberalized the exchange rate. The rupee briefly touched 307 in the open market before stabilizing around 280-285 as a new IMF program was secured.
  • 2024-2026: With improved fiscal discipline, a new IMF program, and stabilized reserves, the rupee has remained in the 275-285 range, showing the first sustained period of stability in years.

Currency Codes and Symbols

The ISO 4217 currency code for the Pakistani Rupee is PKR. The local symbol is Rs. (from the word "rupees") or the Unicode symbol ₨. In international banking and SWIFT transfers, the code PKR is used universally. When sending wire transfers, always specify the currency code to avoid confusion with other rupee currencies (Indian Rupee INR, Sri Lankan Rupee LKR, Nepalese Rupee NPR). The word "rupee" itself derives from the Sanskrit "rupyakam," meaning wrought silver or a coin of silver.

State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)

The State Bank of Pakistan serves as the country's central bank, responsible for issuing currency, formulating monetary policy, managing foreign exchange reserves, and regulating the banking sector. Founded on July 1, 1948, the SBP is headquartered in Karachi. The Governor of the SBP is appointed by the President of Pakistan and plays a key role in interest rate decisions that affect the exchange rate. The SBP's foreign currency reserves, which hovered between $4-9 billion in recent years, are a critical factor in the rupee's stability. When reserves drop, the rupee tends to weaken as market confidence declines.

Pakistan Economy Overview

Pakistan has the 24th largest economy in the world by purchasing power parity (PPP), with a GDP of approximately $1.5 trillion PPP. However, in nominal terms, the GDP is around $370 billion, reflecting the lower price levels. The economy is driven by services (52%), agriculture (23%), and industry (25%). Key exports include textiles, rice, leather goods, sports goods, surgical instruments, and IT services.

Remittance Corridors

Pakistan is one of the world's top remittance-receiving countries. The largest corridors are from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, the United Kingdom, and the United States. In fiscal year 2024-2025, Pakistan received over $30 billion in remittances through official channels, representing roughly 8% of GDP. The Roshan Digital Account (RDA) initiative allows overseas Pakistanis to open bank accounts remotely and send money with favorable exchange rates, which has helped channel more remittances through the formal banking system.

Informal vs Formal Transfer Channels

Historically, a significant portion of remittances to Pakistan flowed through informal channels, commonly known as hawala or hundi. These operate outside the formal banking system, with agents settling balances through trade and trust networks rather than wire transfers. While hawala can offer slightly better rates, it carries risks: no legal recourse if something goes wrong, no transaction records for tax purposes, and potential legal issues as it is technically illegal under Pakistan's Foreign Exchange Regulation Act. The Pakistani government has made significant efforts to shift remittances to formal channels through incentive programs and improved banking infrastructure.

Impact on Pakistani Economy

Remittances play a vital role in Pakistan's economy. They provide a major source of foreign currency inflow, support household consumption for millions of families, reduce poverty rates in receiving communities, and partially offset the country's trade deficit. The Roshan Digital Account program alone attracted over $7 billion in deposits since its launch in 2020, demonstrating the diaspora's willingness to use formal channels when they are made convenient and competitive.

Tips for Travelers to Pakistan

If you are traveling to Pakistan from the United States, understanding the currency and financial landscape can save you money and hassle. Here are practical tips based on real travel experience.

Bringing Cash

Bring US dollars in $50 and $100 denominations. Larger bills get better exchange rates at Pakistani currency shops. Bills should be clean, undamaged, and post-2006 series (newer designs with color). Torn, stained, or pre-2006 bills may be rejected or heavily discounted. Bring enough for your first day's expenses since airport exchange counters offer poor rates. ATMs at major airports (Islamabad, Karachi, Lahore) dispense PKR and accept most international debit cards.

ATM Withdrawals

Pakistani ATMs typically dispense Rs. 25,000 to Rs. 50,000 per transaction, with a daily limit of Rs. 100,000 to Rs. 150,000 depending on the bank. Your US bank may charge $3-5 per international ATM withdrawal, and the Pakistani bank may add a fee of Rs. 200-500. Despite the fees, ATMs often offer better exchange rates than currency exchange counters. HBL, MCB, and UBL ATMs are the most reliable for international cards. Always choose to be charged in PKR (not USD) to avoid dynamic currency conversion markups.

Credit Cards

Visa and Mastercard are accepted at upscale hotels, major restaurants, and shopping malls in larger cities. However, Pakistan remains a heavily cash-based economy, and most small shops, markets, local restaurants, and transportation do not accept cards. Your credit card company will apply a foreign transaction fee (typically 1-3%) and use the Visa or Mastercard network exchange rate, which is usually close to the mid-market rate. Always carry cash as your primary payment method.

Mobile Payments in Pakistan

Mobile payment apps like JazzCash and Easypaisa are widely used in Pakistan. However, they require a Pakistani phone number and CNIC (national ID) to set up, making them impractical for short-term visitors. Some hotels and services may accept payment via these apps if a local friend or contact initiates the transfer. For travelers planning extended stays, setting up a local mobile wallet through a local contact can provide more flexibility.

How Exchange Rates Work

The USD/PKR exchange rate is determined through a combination of market forces and central bank policy. The State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) moved toward a more market-based exchange rate system in 2023 under IMF program conditions. Several factors influence where the rate settles on any given day.

Supply and Demand

When more people want to buy US dollars in Pakistan (to pay for imports, travel, or capital flight), the demand pushes the dollar higher and the rupee lower. When Pakistan receives large inflows (exports, remittances, foreign investment, or IMF disbursements), the rupee strengthens as more dollars enter the market.

Interest Rates and Inflation

Higher interest rates in the US relative to Pakistan make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, pulling capital toward the US and weakening the rupee. Pakistan's high domestic inflation (which exceeded 30% in 2023) erodes the rupee's purchasing power and drives long-term depreciation. The SBP adjusts its policy rate to manage inflation, which indirectly affects the exchange rate.

Interbank vs Open Market Rate

Pakistan has two exchange rate markets. The interbank rate is used between banks and for official transactions. The open market rate (kerb rate) is what you see at exchange shops and is typically 1-3 rupees higher than the interbank rate. When sending remittances through formal banking channels, you generally get close to the interbank rate, which is more favorable.

Trade Balance and Current Account

Pakistan's trade deficit is one of the primary drivers of rupee weakness. The country imports significantly more than it exports, creating persistent demand for US dollars in the market. Major imports include petroleum products, machinery, food items, and chemicals. When oil prices rise globally, Pakistan's import bill swells and the rupee faces additional depreciation pressure. The current account deficit, which includes trade, services, and income flows, has averaged 2-4% of GDP in recent years, occasionally spiking higher during economic crises.

IMF Programs and Currency Policy

Pakistan has had over 20 IMF programs since 1958, more than almost any other country. Each program typically comes with conditions related to fiscal austerity, tax reforms, and exchange rate flexibility. The most recent program required Pakistan to allow the rupee to float more freely, ending the practice of the SBP artificially supporting the currency through reserve spending. This is a major reason for the sharp depreciation in 2022-2023. While painful in the short term, a market-determined exchange rate is generally considered healthier for long-term economic stability.

Currency Forecasts

Predicting exchange rates is inherently uncertain. Analyst forecasts for the PKR vary widely depending on assumptions about Pakistan's fiscal policy, IMF compliance, foreign investment flows, and global commodity prices. Most analysts expect the rupee to remain under pressure against the dollar in the medium term, with gradual depreciation of 3-7% per year being the consensus view. However, significant improvements in exports, foreign direct investment, or a sustained period of political stability could slow or reverse this trend. US Tax Implications of Sending Money Abroad

Americans who send money to Pakistan should be aware of the tax and reporting requirements. While the transfers themselves are generally not taxable, several rules apply.

Gift Tax Considerations

Money sent to family members in Pakistan is considered a gift for US tax purposes. The annual gift tax exclusion for 2026 is $18,000 per recipient. You can give up to this amount to each person without any filing requirement. If you exceed $18,000 to a single recipient, you must file IRS Form 709 (Gift Tax Return), but you typically will not owe tax due to the lifetime gift tax exemption of approximately $13.6 million. Married couples can combine their exclusions, allowing $36,000 per recipient per year without filing.

FBAR and FATCA Reporting

If you maintain financial accounts in Pakistan (bank accounts, investment accounts) with a combined balance exceeding $10,000 at any point during the year, you must file FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR) annually. Under FATCA, US persons with foreign financial assets exceeding $50,000 on the last day of the year (or $75,000 at any point) must report them on IRS Form 8938. These are reporting requirements, not additional taxes, but the penalties for non-compliance are severe, starting at $10,000 per year for an unfiled FBAR.

Roshan Digital Account Tax Treatment

If you use Pakistan's Roshan Digital Account (RDA), be aware that interest earned on RDA deposits is considered US-taxable income and must be reported on your federal tax return. Pakistan may also withhold tax on the interest at source. You can typically claim a Foreign Tax Credit on your US return for taxes paid to Pakistan, preventing double taxation. Consult a tax professional familiar with cross-border tax issues for specific guidance.

Banking in Pakistan

Pakistan's banking sector has grown significantly in recent years, with over 30 commercial banks operating across the country. Understanding the banking landscape helps with making informed decisions about how to send, receive, and manage money in Pakistan.

Major Banks

The largest banks by assets and branch network include Habib Bank Limited (HBL), National Bank of Pakistan (NBP), United Bank Limited (UBL), MCB Bank, and Allied Bank. For Islamic banking, Meezan Bank is the largest and most established, followed by Bank Islami and Dubai Islamic Bank Pakistan. Most major banks offer mobile banking apps, internet banking, and integration with remittance services. HBL and UBL have the widest international presence with branches and representative offices in major world cities.

Opening a Bank Account

Pakistani citizens and overseas Pakistanis can open bank accounts with a valid CNIC (Computerized National Identity Card) or NICOP (National Identity Card for Overseas Pakistanis). Standard accounts require proof of address, passport-size photographs, and an initial deposit (typically Rs. 1,000 to Rs. 10,000 depending on the bank). For overseas Pakistanis, the Roshan Digital Account program allows fully remote account opening with participating banks, requiring only a valid Pakistani passport and proof of residence abroad.

Digital Banking Growth

Pakistan has seen rapid growth in digital financial services. As of 2025, there are over 100 million mobile wallet accounts in the country, driven primarily by JazzCash (by Jazz/Mobilink) and Easypaisa (by Telenor Microfinance Bank). SadaPay and NayaPay are newer digital-only banks targeting younger, tech-savvy users with features like instant card issuance, zero-fee transfers, and modern mobile interfaces. The State Bank of Pakistan has been actively promoting digital payments to reduce cash dependency and increase financial inclusion, with a target of digitizing 50% of payments by 2028.

Interest Rates

The State Bank of Pakistan's policy rate stood at 15% in early 2025, down from a peak of 22% in 2023. This high-rate environment means savings accounts and fixed deposits in Pakistan offer significantly higher returns than in the US (where rates are around 4-5%). However, when adjusted for inflation (which has been running 12-25% in Pakistan), real returns on PKR deposits may be negative. This is one reason many Pakistanis and overseas Pakistanis prefer to hold savings in US dollars or gold rather than PKR-denominated accounts.

Gold Prices in Pakistan

Gold holds special cultural and financial significance in Pakistan, where it is both a traditional wedding gift and a popular savings vehicle. Gold prices in Pakistan are directly linked to the international gold price and the USD/PKR exchange rate.

How Gold Prices Are Set

Pakistan's gold price is calculated as: International gold price per ounce (in USD) multiplied by the USD/PKR rate, divided by 31.1035 (grams per troy ounce). The Karachi Bullion Association publishes daily gold prices per tola (11.664 grams) and per 10 grams. As of March 2026, with gold near $2,900/oz and the rate at 278.50, one tola of 24-karat gold costs approximately Rs. 302,000 ($1,085 USD).

Gold as a Hedge Against PKR Depreciation

Many Pakistani families buy gold as protection against rupee depreciation. Since gold is priced in USD internationally, when the rupee weakens, the PKR price of gold rises, preserving purchasing power. Over the past decade, gold priced in PKR has appreciated approximately 400%, closely tracking the rupee's depreciation. This makes gold a de facto dollar-denominated asset that is culturally acceptable and easy to buy and sell in Pakistan's extensive gold market.

Understanding Conversion Fees

When converting USD to PKR through any service, there are typically two costs: the upfront fee and the exchange rate markup. Understanding both is essential to getting the best deal.

Exchange Rate Markup

The mid-market rate (also called the interbank rate or spot rate) is the true exchange rate used between banks. Consumer services add a markup to this rate, which is the hidden cost of the transaction. If the mid-market rate is 278.50 and your service offers 272.00, the markup is approximately 2.3%. On a $1,000 transfer, that costs you Rs. 6,500 in lost value, which is far more expensive than any flat fee. Always compare the offered rate to the mid-market rate to see the real cost.

Flat Fees vs. Percentage Fees

Some services charge a flat fee ($3, $5, $10 per transfer) while others charge a percentage (1%, 2%, 3%). Flat fees favor larger transfers. A $5 fee on a $100 transfer is 5%, which is expensive. The same $5 fee on a $5,000 transfer is just 0.1%. Percentage-based fees are more consistent regardless of transfer size. Most services use a combination: a small flat fee plus an exchange rate markup. The total cost for a $500 transfer typically ranges from $3 to $25 depending on the service and delivery speed.

Transfer Speed vs. Cost Tradeoff

Faster transfers generally cost more. Same-day or instant cash pickup through Western Union or MoneyGram carries higher fees and worse exchange rates than a 1-3 day bank transfer through Wise or Remitly. If the transfer is not urgent, choosing the slower option can save 2-5% of the transfer value. For a $1,000 transfer, that savings of $20-50 is meaningful. Plan ahead when possible to avoid paying a premium for speed.

CPEC and Chinese Investment

The China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC), a flagship project of China's Belt and Road Initiative, has brought significant Chinese investment into Pakistan since 2015, particularly in energy, infrastructure, and the Gwadar Port. While CPEC projects brought some dollar inflows during construction, the repayment obligations (estimated at $3-4 billion per year in principal and interest) now represent a significant outflow. The net effect on the rupee is debated, but the debt servicing requirement adds to Pakistan's foreign currency needs and puts additional pressure on reserves.

Cryptocurrency and Pakistan

Cryptocurrency has gained significant traction in Pakistan despite a lack of clear regulatory framework. An estimated 15-20 million Pakistanis hold some form of cryptocurrency, making Pakistan one of the highest adoption countries globally by user count.

Crypto as a Dollar Alternative

Many Pakistanis use stablecoins (USDT, USDC) as an alternative way to hold dollar value without needing a US bank account. The peer-to-peer (P2P) market for USDT-to-PKR trades on platforms like Binance P2P is very active, with daily volumes estimated in the tens of millions of dollars. The P2P rate for USDT-PKR often closely tracks the open market dollar rate. Some freelancers and IT service exporters receive payment in USDT and convert to PKR through P2P platforms, sometimes getting better rates than formal banking channels.

Regulatory Status

The State Bank of Pakistan has not formally legalized cryptocurrency transactions but has also not explicitly banned them. The Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) has indicated that crypto gains are taxable income. The legal ambiguity means that while millions of Pakistanis trade crypto, they do so without formal consumer protections. The SBP has repeatedly warned financial institutions against facilitating crypto transactions, though enforcement has been limited. For overseas Pakistanis, using established remittance services remains the safer and more transparent option for sending money home.

Pakistan's IT Industry and Freelancing

Pakistan has become a significant player in the global freelancing and IT services market, which directly affects USD inflows and the exchange rate landscape.

IT Export Growth

Pakistan's IT exports have grown from under $1 billion in 2015 to approximately $2.5-3 billion in 2025-2026, making it one of the fastest-growing sectors. The country has over 300,000 registered freelancers on platforms like Upwork, Fiverr, and Freelancer.com, with Pakistan consistently ranking among the top 5 countries for freelancer supply. Key specializations include web development, mobile app development, graphic design, content writing, virtual assistance, and data entry. Average hourly rates for Pakistani freelancers range from $8-15 for entry-level work to $30-80 for senior developers and specialized consultants.

Freelancer Payment Methods

Pakistani freelancers receiving USD face unique challenges in converting their earnings to PKR. Common payment methods include PayPal (not directly available in Pakistan, though some freelancers use workarounds through third countries), Payoneer (widely used, with direct withdrawal to Pakistani bank accounts at competitive rates), Wise (growing in popularity for its low-cost transfers), and direct bank wire transfers. Freelance income received through formal banking channels is subject to a reduced income tax rate under Pakistan's IT export incentives, making it advantageous to use legitimate channels.

Special Economic Zones

Pakistan has established IT parks and special economic zones to attract technology companies and boost exports. The most notable is the Pakistan Software Export Board (PSEB), which provides tax incentives, infrastructure, and marketing support to IT companies. Companies operating in IT-designated zones may receive tax holidays, reduced import duties on equipment, and simplified foreign exchange procedures. These policies aim to increase IT exports to $15 billion by 2030, which would significantly improve Pakistan's dollar earnings and support the rupee.

Pakistan Tax on Incoming Remittances

Understanding Pakistan's tax treatment of incoming remittances is important for both senders and recipients.

Tax-Free Status of Remittances

Remittances received from abroad through official banking channels are not subject to income tax in Pakistan. The recipient does not need to pay any withholding tax, income tax, or capital gains tax on money received from overseas. This tax-exempt status is a deliberate policy to encourage remittances through formal channels rather than hawala. However, if the recipient invests the remitted funds (in real estate, stocks, or business), any profits from those investments are taxable under normal Pakistani tax rules.

Documentation Requirements

While remittances are tax-free, Pakistani banks are required to maintain records of incoming transfers. For amounts over $10,000, additional documentation may be requested by the receiving bank, including the source of funds, relationship between sender and receiver, and purpose of the transfer. This is part of Pakistan's anti-money laundering (AML) compliance requirements. Keeping transfer receipts and bank statements is advisable for both parties, particularly if the funds are later used for property purchases or business investments where proof of legitimate source is required.

Zakat and Withholding

Pakistan's banking system automatically deducts Zakat (an Islamic wealth tax of 2.5%) on qualifying account balances that exceed the nisab threshold (approximately Rs. 88,000 in savings accounts) on the 1st of Ramadan each year. Non-Muslim account holders and those who file a Zakat exemption declaration (CZ-50 form) are exempt. Additionally, banks withhold 0.6% on cash withdrawals exceeding Rs. 50,000 per day for filers and higher rates for non-filers (tax return filers). These withholdings apply to the recipient's account operations, not directly to incoming remittances.

Pakistan Banking Holidays and Transfer Timing

When planning transfers to Pakistan, be aware that Pakistani banks operate on a different calendar than US banks. Pakistan's weekend is Saturday and Sunday (changed from Friday-Saturday in 2024 for government offices, though some private businesses still observe Friday as a half-day). Major holidays when banks are closed include all national public holidays and Islamic holidays that shift annually on the Gregorian calendar.

Key Holidays When Banks Are Closed

  • Eid ul-Fitr (3 days, end of Ramadan) - April/May
  • Eid ul-Adha (3 days) - June/July
  • Shab-e-Meraj (1 day)
  • 12 Rabi ul-Awal / Eid Milad un-Nabi (1 day)
  • Pakistan Day - March 23
  • Labour Day - May 1

During Eid holidays, banks may be closed for up to 5 consecutive days (including the weekend). If you need money to arrive before Eid (a common need since Eid involves gift-giving and celebrations), initiate the transfer at least 5-7 business days in advance for bank deposits, or use cash pickup or mobile wallet services which may still be operational during holidays. Western Union and MoneyGram agents often operate during holidays at reduced hours.

Visa and Travel Information

US citizens traveling to Pakistan need to be aware of visa requirements, currency regulations, and practical travel considerations.

Visa Requirements

US citizens require a visa to enter Pakistan. Options include an e-visa (available through the Pakistan Online Visa System at visa.nadra.gov.pk), a traditional visa from the Pakistan Embassy or Consulate in the US, and a visa-on-arrival (available for business visa holders from select countries but not generally for US tourist visitors). The e-visa process typically takes 7-10 business days and costs approximately $20-100 depending on visa type and duration. Tourist visas are typically issued for 3 months with single or multiple entry options.

Currency Import and Export Rules

You can bring unlimited US dollars into Pakistan, but you must declare amounts exceeding $10,000 at customs. When departing Pakistan, you can carry up to $10,000 in US currency without documentation. Amounts exceeding $10,000 require proof that the funds were brought into Pakistan or earned locally. It is illegal to export more than Rs. 10,000 in Pakistani currency from the country, though this limit is rarely enforced for small amounts. Keep all exchange receipts as they may be needed to reconvert PKR to USD when departing.

Safety and Travel Advisory

The US State Department maintains travel advisories for Pakistan that vary by region. Major cities like Islamabad, Lahore, Karachi, and tourist destinations in northern Pakistan (Gilgit-Baltistan, Hunza Valley, Swat Valley) are generally safe for travelers exercising normal precautions. Border areas with Afghanistan, Balochistan, and certain tribal regions have higher-level warnings. Always check the current travel advisory on travel.state.gov before planning your trip. Register with the US Embassy through the STEP (Smart Traveler Enrollment Program) for security updates during your stay.

SIM Cards and Connectivity

Purchasing a local SIM card in Pakistan requires your passport and biometric verification (fingerprint) at an authorized retailer. Major carriers are Jazz (Mobilink), Telenor, Zong, and Ufone. Prepaid SIM cards cost Rs. 100-500 and data packages are very affordable: 30GB of 4G data can cost as little as Rs. 500-1,000 ($1.80-$3.59 per month). Having a local SIM is essential for using JazzCash, Easypaisa, ride-hailing apps (Careem, InDrive), and food delivery services (Foodpanda) that have become integral to daily life in Pakistani cities.
