How to Use This GPA Calculator

This GPA calculator is designed for college and high school students who want to accurately calculate their grade point average. Whether you are tracking a single semester or planning your entire academic career, this tool handles it all.

Start by adding your courses. Enter the course name, the number of credit hours, and the letter grade you received (or expect to receive). Click "Calculate GPA" to see your semester GPA instantly. You can add multiple semesters to see how each one contributes to your cumulative average.

If you already have a cumulative GPA from prior semesters or transfer credits, click "Add Existing GPA" and enter your current cumulative GPA along with the total credits earned. The calculator will factor this into your new cumulative GPA when you add additional courses.

How GPA Is Calculated

Your GPA is a weighted average of the grade points you earn in each course, weighted by the number of credit hours for that course. The formula is straightforward:

GPA = (Sum of Quality Points) / (Sum of Credit Hours)

Quality points for each course equal the grade points multiplied by the credit hours. For example, if you earn an A (4.0) in a 3-credit course, you earn 4.0 x 3 = 12.0 quality points. If you earn a B+ (3.3) in a 4-credit course, you earn 3.3 x 4 = 13.2 quality points.

Your semester GPA is then (12.0 + 13.2) / (3 + 4) = 25.2 / 7 = 3.60.

Cumulative GPA Calculation

Cumulative GPA follows the same formula but includes all semesters. If you had 30 credits with a 3.4 GPA (102 total quality points) and then completed 15 new credits with a 3.6 semester GPA (54 quality points), your new cumulative GPA would be (102 + 54) / (30 + 15) = 156 / 45 = 3.47.

This calculator handles all of this automatically. When you import your existing GPA and credits, it converts them to quality points and combines them with your new courses.

Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA uses the standard 4.0 scale regardless of course difficulty. A weighted GPA gives additional grade points for advanced courses. The most common weighted scales add 1.0 point for AP and honors courses and 0.5 points for IB courses.

For example, an A in a regular course is worth 4.0 on both scales. But an A in an AP course would be worth 5.0 on a weighted scale. This means weighted GPAs can exceed 4.0, sometimes reaching as high as 5.0 or even higher depending on the number of advanced courses taken.

Weighted GPA is primarily used in high school settings. Most colleges recalculate GPA using their own scale during the admissions process. However, weighted GPA can be useful for class rank calculations and scholarship eligibility at the high school level.

Toggle the "Use weighted GPA" option in this calculator if you want to include course-level weighting. When enabled, you will see a dropdown to classify each course as regular, honors/AP, or IB.

Using the Target GPA Calculator

The target GPA calculator answers one of the most common student questions: "What GPA do I need next semester to reach my goal?" This is particularly useful for students aiming for the Dean's List, maintaining scholarship requirements, or preparing for graduate school applications.

Enter your current cumulative GPA, total credits completed, your target GPA, and the number of credits you plan to take next semester. The calculator will tell you the exact GPA you need to achieve in those upcoming courses.

Keep in mind that if the required GPA exceeds 4.0, it is mathematically impossible to reach your target in a single semester with those credits. You may need to take more credits or plan across multiple semesters to reach your goal.

Practical Target GPA Example

Suppose you have a 2.8 cumulative GPA after 45 credits and want to graduate with a 3.0 GPA after completing 15 more credits. The required quality points for a 3.0 GPA over 60 total credits would be 180. Your current quality points are 2.8 x 45 = 126. You need 180 - 126 = 54 quality points from your remaining 15 credits, which means you need a 54 / 15 = 3.6 GPA next semester. That is achievable with mostly A- and B+ grades.

Tips for Improving Your GPA

  • Prioritize high-credit courses. Getting an A in a 4-credit course has more impact on your GPA than getting an A in a 1-credit course.
  • Consider retaking courses where you earned a low grade, if your school's policy allows grade replacement.
  • Use the target GPA calculator to set realistic goals for each semester and track your progress.
  • Attend office hours, form study groups, and use campus tutoring resources. Small improvements in multiple courses compound into significant GPA gains.
  • Plan your course load carefully. Mixing challenging courses with ones you are confident in can help maintain balance.
  • Take advantage of pass/fail options for exploratory courses that might hurt your GPA if graded traditionally.

GPA Requirements for Common Goals

Different academic and professional goals have different GPA expectations. Here are some common benchmarks:

  • Dean's List: Typically requires a 3.5 GPA or higher for the semester, though this varies by institution.
  • Cum Laude: Usually requires a cumulative GPA of 3.5 or higher.
  • Magna Cum Laude: Typically 3.7 or higher.
  • Summa Cum Laude: Usually 3.9 or higher.
  • Graduate school admissions: Most programs look for a 3.0 minimum, with competitive programs expecting 3.5 or above.
  • Law school: A competitive applicant typically has a 3.5 or higher, though top schools may expect 3.7 and above.
  • Medical school: Most successful applicants have a 3.5 or higher, with a strong science GPA being particularly important.
  • Scholarship maintenance: Many scholarships require maintaining a 3.0 or 3.25 GPA.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is GPA calculated?▼
GPA is calculated by multiplying each course's grade points by its credit hours to get quality points, summing all quality points, then dividing by total credit hours. For example, an A (4.0) in a 3-credit course earns 12 quality points. If you also earn a B (3.0) in a 4-credit course (12 quality points), your GPA is (12 + 12) / (3 + 4) = 3.43.
What is the difference between semester GPA and cumulative GPA?▼
Semester GPA is calculated using only the courses from a single semester. Cumulative GPA includes all courses across all semesters throughout your academic career. Your cumulative GPA is what appears on your transcript and what employers and graduate schools typically consider.
What is a good GPA for college?▼
A GPA of 3.0 or above is generally considered good. A 3.5 or higher is very good and may qualify you for the Dean's List at many institutions. A 3.7 or above is excellent. However, "good" depends on your goals, major, and school. Some competitive programs expect higher GPAs, while employers in certain fields may weight experience more heavily than GPA.
How do plus and minus grades affect GPA?▼
Plus and minus grades adjust grade points from the base letter grade. An A- is 3.7 instead of 4.0, a B+ is 3.3 instead of 3.0, and so on. These differences can have a meaningful impact over many courses. For example, getting A- grades instead of A grades across 40 courses could lower your GPA from 4.0 to 3.7.
Can I raise my GPA significantly in one semester?▼
The impact depends on how many credits you have already completed. With fewer completed credits (e.g., 15-30), one strong semester can raise your GPA noticeably. With more credits (e.g., 90+), the effect is smaller. Use the target GPA calculator to see exactly what you need. Taking a heavier course load of credits you can excel in will have more impact.
What is a weighted GPA?▼
A weighted GPA adds extra grade points for advanced courses. AP and honors courses typically receive an additional 1.0 point, and IB courses often receive 0.5 extra points. This means an A in an AP course is worth 5.0 instead of 4.0 on a weighted scale. Weighted GPA is mainly used in high school for class rank and some scholarship considerations.
How do I calculate my cumulative GPA with transfer credits?▼
Use the "Add Existing GPA" feature in this calculator. Enter your current cumulative GPA and the total number of credits earned (including transfer credits that count toward your GPA). Then add your new courses. The calculator will combine everything to produce your updated cumulative GPA. Note that some schools do not include transfer course grades in the cumulative GPA, so check your institution's policy.
Does retaking a course improve my GPA?▼
Most colleges have grade replacement policies where the new grade replaces the original in your GPA calculation. Some schools average both attempts, while others only replace if the new grade is higher. Grade replacement can be an effective strategy for improving your GPA, especially if you earned a D or F in the original attempt. Always check your specific institution's retake policy before enrolling.
