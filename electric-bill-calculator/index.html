How the Electric Bill Calculator Works

I've this calculator to give you a clear picture of where your electricity goes. Most people don't realize which appliances are the biggest energy hogs in their home. kWh = (Watts x Hours/Day x Days/Month) / 1000, then multiply by your rate per kWh to get the cost.

The US average electricity rate is about $0.16 per kWh, but rates vary wildly by state. Hawaii pays more than double that, while some states like Louisiana enjoy rates well below the national average. You can't ignore these regional differences when estimating your bill.

Top Energy Consumers in Your Home

Air conditioning and heating typically account for 40-50% of your electric bill. The next biggest consumers are usually water heaters, dryers, and refrigerators. I won't pretend that switching off a few lights will bill - the real savings come from improving your HVAC system and upgrading to energy-efficient appliances.

Our Testing Methodology

Wattage data for pre-loaded appliances is sourced from the US Department of Energy and EnergyStar.gov. Our testing confirms these values against real-world measurements using Kill-A-Watt meters. We verify calculations against utility company estimators and update the data when appliance efficiency standards change.
