How to Use This Net Worth Calculator

Enter each of your assets (things you own that have value) and liabilities (debts you owe) in the sections above. Click "Calculate Net Worth" to see your total net worth, which is simply your total assets minus your total liabilities.

Your net worth is one of the most important indicators of your overall financial health. A positive net worth means you own more than you owe, while a negative net worth indicates your debts exceed your assets. Tracking this number over time helps you measure financial progress toward your goals.

Common assets include savings accounts, checking accounts, investment portfolios, retirement accounts (401k, IRA), real estate, vehicles, and valuable personal property. Common liabilities include mortgages, auto loans, student loans, credit card balances, personal loans, and medical debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is net worth?

Net worth is the difference between your total assets (what you own) and your total liabilities (what you owe). A positive net worth means you own more than you owe, indicating financial stability.

What should I include in my assets?

Include cash and savings, investments (stocks, bonds, mutual funds), retirement accounts (401k, IRA), real estate market value, vehicles, and other valuable property like jewelry or collectibles.

What counts as a liability?

Liabilities include mortgage balances, car loans, student loans, credit card debt, personal loans, medical debt, and any other money you owe to lenders or creditors.

How often should I calculate my net worth?

Most financial advisors recommend calculating your net worth at least quarterly, or even monthly. Regular tracking helps you monitor your financial progress and identify trends over time.

What is a good net worth for my age?

A common benchmark is that by age 30 you should have a net worth equal to your annual salary. By 40, aim for twice your salary. By 50, four times. These are general guidelines and individual circumstances vary widely.
