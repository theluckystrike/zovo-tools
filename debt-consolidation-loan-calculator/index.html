About Debt Consolidation Loans

A debt consolidation loan is a personal loan used to pay off multiple debts, replacing them with a single monthly payment at a potentially lower interest rate. This approach simplifies your finances and can reduce total interest costs.

When consolidation makes sense: If your combined weighted average interest rate is higher than the rate you can get on a consolidation loan, and you commit to not accumulating new debt, consolidation can save you significant money.

Breakeven point: This calculator shows you exactly when the interest savings offset the origination fee, so you know the minimum time you need to keep the loan to benefit from consolidation.

Strategy matters: Whether you are paying off debts individually or considering consolidation, the order you tackle debts matters. The avalanche method (highest rate first) saves the most money, while the snowball method (smallest balance first) provides motivating quick wins.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between debt snowball and debt avalanche?
The debt snowball method pays off the smallest balance first for quick wins and motivation, while the debt avalanche method targets the highest interest rate first to minimize total interest paid. Avalanche saves more money mathematically, but snowball provides faster psychological wins that help some people stay on track.
What is a breakeven point for debt consolidation?
The breakeven point is the month when the cumulative interest saved by the consolidation loan equals the origination fee or other upfront costs. After this point, you are truly saving money compared to your original debt payments. This calculator shows you your exact breakeven month.
What types of debt can be consolidated?
Most unsecured debts can be consolidated, including credit cards, personal loans, medical bills, payday loans, and some student loans. Secured debts like mortgages and auto loans are typically handled separately since they are backed by collateral.
Will I actually save money with a consolidation loan?
You will save money if the consolidation loan APR is lower than your weighted average debt APR, after accounting for origination fees. The longer you keep the loan, the more the interest savings accumulate. Use this calculator to compare your specific numbers.
