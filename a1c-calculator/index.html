Understanding A1C and Blood Glucose

The A1C test measures your average blood glucose levels over the past 2-3 months. Unlike daily glucose readings, A1C provides a longer-term picture of your glucose control.

A1C Conversion Formula

Average Glucose (mg/dL) = (A1C × 28.7) - 46.7
Average Glucose (mmol/L) = (A1C × 1.59) - 2.59

Diagnostic Ranges

  • Normal: A1C below 5.7%
  • Prediabetes: A1C between 5.7% and 6.4%
  • Diabetes: A1C of 6.5% or higher

This calculator provides estimates based on standard formulas. Always consult with your healthcare provider for medical interpretation of your A1C results.

About A1C Testing

The A1C test measures the percentage of hemoglobin proteins in red blood cells that have glucose molecules attached. Since red blood cells live about 120 days, the test reflects your average glucose levels over approximately 2-3 months.

A1C testing is preferred for diabetes diagnosis because it doesn't require fasting and provides a more stable measurement than single-point glucose tests.

Factors Affecting A1C

  • Blood glucose control over the past 2-3 months
  • Red blood cell lifespan
  • Certain medical conditions (anemia, kidney disease)
  • Medications that affect red blood cell turnover
  • Recent blood transfusions
