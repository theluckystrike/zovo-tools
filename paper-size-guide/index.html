ISO A Series Paper Sizes (A0 to A10)

The ISO A series is the most widely used paper size standard in the world. It is based on the ISO 216 international standard, which defines paper sizes using a consistent mathematical ratio. Each size in the series is exactly half the area of the previous size, created by cutting the longer dimension in half. The aspect ratio of all A series sizes is 1 to the square root of 2, approximately 1:1.414.

ISO B Series Paper Sizes

The B series fills the gaps between A series sizes. Each B size has dimensions that are the geometric mean of the corresponding A size and the next larger A size. B sizes are commonly used in book publishing, envelope manufacturing, and some Asian office equipment standards.

History of Paper Sizes

Paper size standardization has a longer and more interesting history than you might expect. Before standardized sizes existed, paper was produced in whatever dimensions the individual papermaker's mold happened to be. This created chaos for printers, publishers, and anyone trying to use paper from different sources interchangeably (Paper size).

The first serious attempt at paper size standardization came from Georg Christoph Lichtenberg, a German physicist, who proposed a system based on the square root of 2 ratio in a 1786 letter. His insight was that this particular ratio preserves the aspect ratio when a sheet is folded in half, making it a paper sizing system., it took over a century for this idea to be formally adopted.

In 1922, the German Institute for Standardization (DIN) published DIN 476, which defined the A and B series paper sizes based on Lichtenberg's ratio. The starting point, A0, was defined to have an area of exactly one square meter. This standard was gradually adopted across Europe and eventually became the international ISO 216 standard in 1975. Today, the ISO A series is the official paper size standard in virtually every country except the United States, Canada, and a handful of other nations still using North American sizes.

The American system, by contrast, evolved from practical tradition rather than mathematical elegance. The 8.5 x 11 inch size likely originated from the dimensions of standard hand papermaking molds used by American mills in the 18th and 19th centuries. When the American National Standards Institute (ANSI) formalized US paper sizes in the 1990s with the ANSI/ASME Y14.1 standard, they essentially codified the sizes that were already in widespread use rather than designing a new system (ANSI paper sizes).

A4 vs US Letter A Practical Comparison

The difference between A4 and US Letter is one of the most frequently encountered paper size issues, especially for anyone who works internationally, sends documents across borders, or downloads templates from the internet. Here is a direct side-by-side comparison of the two sizes.

A4 is 210 x 297 mm (8.27 x 11.69 inches). US Letter is 215.9 x 279.4 mm (8.5 x 11 inches). That means Letter is 5.9 mm wider but 17.6 mm shorter than A4. In practical terms, Letter gives you slightly more horizontal space while A4 gives you noticeably more vertical space.

When you create a document in a word processor, the paper size you select affects margins, line counts, page breaks, and how many pages the document will be. A resume for Letter might spill onto a second page when printed on A4 if the margins are increased to compensate for the narrower width. A legal document formatted for A4 might have its footer cut off when printed on Letter due to the shorter page height.

For web developers generating PDFs, specifying the correct paper size in your CSS print stylesheet or PDF generation library is important. The default varies by locale: browsers in the US default to Letter, while browsers in most other countries default to A4. If your application serves users globally, providing an option to select paper size is the professional approach.

Scaling Between A4 and Letter

When printing an A4 document on Letter paper (or vice versa), your printer driver or PDF viewer will typically offer a "fit to page" or "shrink to fit" option. Scaling A4 content to fit on Letter reduces the content to about 94% of its original size, which is barely noticeable for text but can be significant for technical drawings where precise dimensions matter. Going from Letter to A4, the content fits without reduction because A4 is both taller and only slightly narrower after accounting for the typical margin adjustments.

Printing Tips for Different Paper Sizes

Setting Paper Size in Common Applications

In Microsoft Word, go to Layout, then Size, and select the paper size from the list. If the size you need is not listed, click More Paper Sizes at the bottom to enter custom dimensions. In Google Docs, go to File, then Page Setup. In Adobe Acrobat, the paper size is set when creating the PDF, but you can change the print size in the Print dialog. For CSS print stylesheets, use the @page rule with the size property: @page { size: A4; } or @page { size: letter; }.

Handling Mixed-Size Printing

If you print a document that contains pages of different sizes (for example, a report with A4 text pages and A3 fold-out diagrams), most advanced printers can handle this through a mixed media tray setup. In the print driver settings, look for "paper source" options that let you assign different trays to different page ranges. Some print shops call this "tabbed" or "mixed stock" printing.

DPI and Resolution at Different Paper Sizes

The pixel dimensions needed for printing depend on both the paper size and the desired print resolution (DPI). For standard documents, 150 DPI is usually sufficient. For high-quality photo printing, 300 DPI is the standard. At 300 DPI, an A4 document needs an image resolution of at least 2480 x 3508 pixels. A US Letter document at 300 DPI needs 2550 x 3300 pixels. For large format prints on A1 or A0, you can often get away with lower DPI (150 or even 100 DPI) because these prints are typically viewed from a distance (Dots per inch).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between A4 and US Letter paper?+

A4 and US Letter are close in size but not identical. A4 measures 210 x 297 mm (8.27 x 11.69 inches), while US Letter measures 215.9 x 279.4 mm (8.5 x 11 inches). So Letter is slightly wider but noticeably shorter than A4. This difference matters when printing documents for one size on the other. A document formatted for A4 printed on Letter paper will have the bottom cut off unless you enable shrink-to-fit in your print settings. Going the other direction, a Letter-formatted document printed on A4 will have extra space at the bottom. A4 is the international standard used in virtually every country except the United States, Canada, and parts of Mexico. The difference traces back to two independent standardization efforts that happened on different continents.

How does the ISO A series paper sizing system work?+

The ISO A series is based on a mathematical principle: each size is exactly half the area of the next larger size, achieved by cutting the paper in half along its longer dimension. A0 is defined as having an area of exactly one square meter, with sides in a ratio of 1 to the square root of 2 (approximately 1:1.414). This ratio is special because when you fold or cut the paper in half along the long side, the resulting half sheet has exactly the same proportions as the original. A1 is half of A0, A2 is half of A1, and so on down to A10. The dimensions of A0 are 841 x 1189 mm. This system was first adopted as a German standard in 1922 and later became the international ISO 216 standard. The mathematical elegance means scaling between sizes is straightforward: A4 to A3 is always 141% enlargement, and A4 to A5 is always 71% reduction.

What paper size should I use for printing posters?+

The paper size for posters depends on where and how they will be displayed. For small event posters or flyers meant for bulletin boards, A3 (297 x 420 mm) is a common choice. It is readable from a few feet away while still printable on many office printers. For medium-sized posters in store windows or classrooms, A2 (420 x 594 mm) provides good visibility. Standard movie posters use 27 x 40 inches (686 x 1016 mm), closest to A1 in overall area. For conference or academic poster sessions, A0 (841 x 1189 mm) and ANSI E (34 x 44 inches) are most common. Check with the venue or event organizer for their specific size requirements before designing your poster, as many conferences have strict limits for the poster boards they provide.

What is Legal size paper used for?+

Legal size paper measures 8.5 x 14 inches (215.9 x 355.6 mm), which is the same width as US Letter but three inches longer. As the name suggests, Legal paper was historically the standard for legal documents in the United States and Canada, including contracts, court filings, deeds, and other official documents. The extra length provided more space for lengthy text and left room for signature lines, notary stamps, and legal seals. While many law firms have shifted to Letter size for routine documents, Legal size is still used for certain filings, real estate closing documents, and government forms. Most office printers have an adjustable tray that accommodates Legal size. In the design industry, Legal size is occasionally used for brochures and tri-fold pamphlets, since its length provides three roughly equal panels when folded into thirds.

Why does the US use different paper sizes?+

The United States uses different paper sizes primarily because of historical path dependency. American paper sizes were established by custom and industry practice long before international standardization efforts began. The 8.5 x 11 inch Letter size became the standard in the US during the early 20th century, derived from traditional hand papermaking mold dimensions. By the time ISO 216 was published in 1975, American paper mills, printers, filing systems, and office equipment were already around Letter and Legal sizes. Switching to A4 would have required replacing billions of file folders, binders, envelopes, and paper trays across every office in the country. The US government considered adopting A4 in the 1980s and early 1990s, and some federal agencies experimented with it, but the transition never gained enough momentum.

What is the B series of paper sizes?+

The ISO B series is a companion to the A series that provides intermediate sizes between each pair of A sizes. The dimensions of each B size are the geometric mean of the corresponding A size and the next larger A size. B0 measures 1000 x 1414 mm, larger than A0. The B series is commonly used for specific applications: B5 (176 x 250 mm) is popular for books and journals, particularly in European and Asian publishing. B4 (250 x 353 mm) is used by some Japanese office equipment as a standard print size. B series envelopes are hold A series documents without folding. The B series is less encountered in everyday office work but is standard in printing, publishing, and envelope manufacturing across Europe and Asia.

How do I set up my printer for different paper sizes?+

Setting up your printer for a different paper size involves both a physical step and a software step. Start by loading the correct paper into the tray. Most printers have adjustable guides that slide to accommodate different widths and lengths. Adjust these guides snug against the paper without bending it. Then, in your operating system or application, go to print settings. On Windows, click Print, then Properties and look for Paper Size. On macOS, go to File then Page Setup or look in the Print dialog under Paper Size. Select the correct size from the dropdown. Make sure the document page size matches the paper in the printer, or use Fit to Page if there is a mismatch. Common mistakes include forgetting to change the paper guides in the tray, which causes jams, or setting the document to A4 but loading Letter paper, which results in cropped content.

What paper size for international shipping documents?+

For international shipping documents, A4 is almost always the correct choice, even if you are based in the United States. Shipping documents like commercial invoices, packing lists, and certificates of origin will be processed by customs officials in the destination country, and most countries outside North America use A4. Customs offices, banks handling letters of credit, and freight forwarders in Europe, Asia, Africa, and South America all work with A4 filing systems. Sending documents on Letter paper can result in them not fitting properly or being partially cut off when photocopied. The extra vertical space on A4 also provides room for the additional information fields that international documents often require. If you regularly handle international shipments, keeping A4 paper alongside your Letter paper is worthwhile.
