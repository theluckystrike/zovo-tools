How Shoe Sizing Works: A Background

Shoe sizing is the process of mapping the physical dimensions of a human foot to a standardized numerical system so that footwear can be manufactured and sold without custom fitting every pair. According to Wikipedia's shoe size article, the modern shoe sizing systems date back to the 13th century in England, where King Edward II decreed that one inch equals three barleycorns (grains of barley laid end to end). This unit became the basis for the British sizing system, and the barleycorn increment of approximately 8.47 mm persists in modern US and UK shoe sizes today.

The fundamental problem with shoe sizing is that no single global standard has been universally adopted by manufacturers. The International Organization for Standardization published ISO 9407 (now superseded by ISO 19407) to define shoe sizes purely in terms of foot length measured in millimeters, but the retail industry continues to use the older US, UK, and EU systems for consumer familiarity. This converter bridges those systems so you can shop internationally without guesswork.

There are four primary sizing systems used around the world. The US system (also called the Brannock system), the UK system, the European continental system (French or Paris Point), and the metric system measured in centimeters used in Japan, South Korea, and other East Asian countries. Each one uses a different formula to convert foot length into a numbered size, which is why the numbers differ so much.

US Shoe Sizing: The Brannock System

The Brannock Device, invented by Charles Brannock in 1925 and patented in 1928, is the standard foot measuring tool used in American shoe stores. The device measures foot length, width, and arch length simultaneously. US men's shoe sizes are calculated with this formula: size = 3 x (foot length in inches) - 22. For women, the formula adds 1 to the result, so US women's sizes run about 1.5 sizes higher than men's for the same foot length.

Each full US size represents one-third of an inch (approximately 8.47 mm), and half sizes split that difference. A US men's size 10 corresponds to a foot length of about 10.69 inches or 27.1 cm. The Brannock system starts at size 1 for men (which corresponds to a 7.83-inch foot) and typically goes up to size 16 for standard retail, though specialty manufacturers produce sizes up to 20 or more.

One important detail that confuses many shoppers is that Brannock also measures arch length, which is the distance from the heel to the ball of the foot. In some cases, a person might need a longer shoe not because their overall foot is long, but because their arch is long. A good fitter will check both measurements and use whichever is larger to determine the correct size.

UK Shoe Sizing

The UK system uses the same barleycorn increment as the US system (one size equals one-third of an inch), but it starts from a different reference point. The origin of the UK system is size 0 for children, which corresponds to a foot length of 4 inches (the smallest practical infant shoe). Men's UK sizes run approximately 0.5 to 1 size lower than US men's sizes, so a US 10 is roughly a UK 9.5.

Unlike the US system, the UK does not have a standard difference between men's and women's sizes in the traditional sense. Historically, British women's shoes used the same scale as men's, but modern retailers have started adopting a separate women's scale that runs about one size higher than the men's scale, similar to the US convention. This inconsistency means you should always verify which scale a British retailer is using.

Australia and New Zealand generally use the UK system for men's shoes, though women's shoes in those countries sometimes follow the US women's scale. If you are ordering shoes from an Australian retailer, check the product description to see which system they reference.

EU Shoe Sizing: The Paris Point System

The European shoe size system, also called the Continental system, is based on the "Paris Point" unit introduced by French shoemakers. One Paris Point equals two-thirds of a centimeter (6.67 mm). EU sizes are calculated as: size = 1.5 x (foot length in cm) + 2. The "+2" represents the standard amount of space added beyond the foot length for toe room (known as the "functional surplus" or "toe allowance").

One of the advantages of the EU system is that it is unisex. There is no separate scale for men and women. A EU 42 is a EU 42 regardless of the gender of the shoe. This simplicity is one reason why the EU system is often preferred for international size charts. The system runs continuously from small children's sizes (starting around EU 16 for infants) through adult sizes (up to EU 50 or higher for large feet).

In practice, the toe allowance built into the EU formula varies by manufacturer. Some brands add more room, some add less, which is why a EU 43 from Nike might fit differently than a EU 43 from Clarks. The standard 2-size surplus assumed by the formula does not account for these manufacturing differences, so it is always worth trying shoes on or consulting brand-specific size guides when ordering online.

How to Measure Your Feet at Home

Accurate foot measurement is the foundation of finding the right shoe size. Here is the step-by-step process that podiatrists and professional shoe fitters recommend:

  • Gather a sheet of paper larger than your foot, a pen, a ruler or tape measure, and a hard flat surface
  • Place the paper on the floor against a wall
  • Stand on the paper with your heel firmly against the wall, wearing the type of socks you plan to wear with the shoes
  • Put your full body weight on the foot you are measuring (feet spread under load, so sitting measurements will be inaccurate)
  • Have someone mark the tip of your longest toe on the paper with a pen held perpendicular to the floor
  • Measure from the edge of the paper (where the wall was) to the mark in centimeters
  • Repeat with the other foot and use the larger measurement

Measure in the afternoon or evening because feet swell throughout the day. If you measure first thing in the morning, you may end up with shoes that feel tight by evening. The difference between morning and evening foot size can be as much as 5 to 8 mm, which is nearly a full size in some systems.

Brand-Specific Sizing Differences

Even with accurate foot measurements and standardized conversion charts, shoe fit varies noticeably between brands. After years of fitting hundreds of customers for running shoes, here are the patterns I have observed:

Nike shoes tend to run about half a size small. If you wear a US 10 in most brands, you will likely need a US 10.5 in Nike. Their shoes also tend to run narrow, particularly in the forefoot area. Adidas runs fairly true to size for most models, though their Yeezy line is notoriously small and typically requires going up a full size. New Balance is generally true to size and offers some of the best width options in the industry, with sizes available from 2A (narrow) to 6E (extra extra wide). Converse Chuck Taylors run about a full size large because of the long toe cap, so most people size down one full size.

For European brands like Birkenstock, the EU size on the box corresponds closely to the standard chart, but their sizing is based on the German DIN standard which includes a generous toe allowance. If you are between sizes in Birkenstocks, go with the smaller size. Italian brands like Gucci and Prada use Italian sizing which is typically one full EU size larger than the standard chart predicts, so a Gucci EU 42 fits more like a standard EU 41.

Kids Shoe Sizing Guide

Children's shoe sizing is especially confusing because of the scale reset that happens around age 6 in the US and UK systems. In the US, baby and toddler sizes run from 0 to 13. After size 13, the scale resets to 1 for "Youth" sizes, which run from 1 to 7. After youth size 7, the child moves into adult sizes. This means there are two completely different shoes labeled "size 5" in a children's shoe department, one for toddlers and one for older kids.

Children's feet grow at an average rate of about 1 mm per month between ages 1 and 3, slowing to about 0.5 mm per month by age 6. This means toddlers can outgrow their shoes every 2 to 3 months, while school-age children may need new shoes every 4 to 6 months. Always leave about a thumb's width of space between the longest toe and the front of the shoe to allow for growth. Too much room, however, causes the foot to slide around and can lead to blisters.

The EU sizing system is simpler for kids because it does not reset. A toddler wearing EU 24 grows into EU 25, then 26, and so on continuously into adult sizes. Many parents find it easier to track growth using the EU system even if they live in the US or UK.

Understanding Width Sizing

Width is the overlooked dimension in shoe sizing. Most conversion charts only address length, but a shoe that matches your length perfectly can still be uncomfortable if the width is wrong. In the US, width is indicated by letter codes: AAA or 3A is extra narrow, AA or 2A is narrow, B is narrow/standard for men (standard for women), D is standard for men (wide for women), E or EE is wide for men, and EEEE or 4E is extra wide.

About 28% of people have feet that are wider than the standard width, and about 10% have narrower feet than standard. If you consistently find that shoes feel tight across the ball of your foot even when the length is correct, you likely need a wider width rather than a longer size. Going up in length to compensate for width creates excess toe room and causes heel slippage. New Balance, Brooks, and ASICS are the brands most commonly recommended by podiatrists for customers who need non-standard widths.

Community Questions

What developers and shoppers ask about shoe sizing

How do I convert EU shoe size to US programmatically?

The standard formula for men is: US = (EU - 31.5) / 1.0 approximately, though lookup tables are more accurate than formulas because of rounding. Source: Stack Overflow - Converting between shoe size systems

Why do different shoe brands use different size charts?

Manufacturers use different "lasts" (foot-shaped molds) and apply different toe allowances, which shifts the effective size. The ISO standard defines sizes by foot length, not shoe internal length, so the gap between brands comes from how much extra space each adds. Is there a REST API for shoe size conversion?

There is no widely adopted public API, but the conversion is straightforward to implement with a lookup table based on ISO 19407 foot-length-to-size mappings. Many developers hard-code the table into their apps. Source: Stack Overflow - Shoe size conversion API

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure my foot size at home?
Place a sheet of paper on a hard floor against a wall. Stand on the paper with your heel touching the wall and your full weight on the foot. Mark the longest point of your foot with a pen, then measure from the edge of the paper to the mark in centimeters. Do this for both feet and use the larger measurement. Measure in the afternoon when your feet are slightly swollen for the most accurate result. This centimeter measurement can be entered into the converter above to find your size in any system.
What is the difference between US, UK, and EU shoe sizes?
US, UK, and EU shoe sizes use completely different numbering systems and starting points. US sizes are based on the Brannock system, where each whole size represents one-third of an inch. UK sizes use a similar increment but start about one size lower than US sizes for men and about two sizes lower for women. EU sizes follow the Paris Point system, where each size equals two-thirds of a centimeter. Because of these different baselines, a US men's 10 is roughly a UK 9.5 and an EU 43-44. The converter on this page handles all of those offsets automatically.
Why are women's shoe sizes different from men's?
Women's shoe sizes are offset from men's sizes in the US and UK systems because the scales were historically calibrated to different foot length ranges. In the US, women's sizes run about 1.5 sizes higher than men's, so a US women's 8 is roughly equivalent to a US men's 6.5. In the UK, the offset is about one full size. The EU system does not have separate scales for men and women. The same EU number corresponds to the same foot length regardless of gender. When shopping across brands, always check whether the listed size is men's or women's to avoid ordering the wrong fit.
How accurate are shoe size conversion charts?
Standard shoe size conversion charts give you a reliable starting point, but they are not perfectly exact across every brand. The ISO 19407 standard defines shoe sizing based on foot length in millimeters, but many manufacturers deviate slightly based on their own lasts and target demographics. Nike shoes tend to run about half a size small, while New Balance and Adidas often fit more true to size. Italian and designer brands frequently use their own proprietary EU sizing that can differ by one full size from the standard chart. The converter on this page uses the internationally recognized ISO mappings and will get you within half a size of the correct fit for most brands.
What does shoe width mean and how does it affect sizing?
Shoe width is measured separately from length and is denoted by letters. In the US system, standard width is D for men and B for women. Narrow widths are labeled B or AA for men and AA or A for women. Wide widths are labeled E or EE for men and D or E for women. Extra wide goes up to EEEE or 4E. Width matters because a shoe that is the correct length but too narrow will feel tight across the ball of the foot, and many people mistakenly size up in length to compensate. If you find that shoes in your measured size pinch the sides of your foot, try the same length in a wide width rather than going up a full size.
Do shoe sizes change with age?
Yes, adult shoe sizes can change over time. Feet tend to get slightly longer and wider as people age due to the gradual loosening of ligaments and tendons, along with the flattening of the arch. Pregnancy can also permanently increase foot size by half a size or more because of hormonal changes that relax connective tissue. Weight gain puts additional pressure on the feet, which can spread them. Podiatrists recommend measuring your feet every couple of years rather than assuming your size from a decade ago still applies. Many people find they need shoes half a size to a full size larger by their 50s compared to their 20s.
How do kids shoe sizes work compared to adult sizes?
Children's shoe sizes in the US start at size 0 for newborns, which corresponds to roughly 7.9 cm in foot length. The kids scale runs from 0 to 13, and then resets to 1 for youth sizes, which go up to about 7. After youth size 7, the child transitions to adult sizing. A youth size 7 is roughly equivalent to a US women's 8.5 or a US men's 7. The EU system does not reset and runs continuously, so a child wearing EU 33 simply moves to EU 34 and so on into adult sizes. This continuous numbering makes the EU system less confusing for kids. Children's feet grow rapidly, and parents should check sizing every two to three months for children under five.
Is there a universal shoe sizing standard?
The closest thing to a universal standard is the Mondopoint system, defined by ISO 9407. Mondopoint expresses shoe size as the foot length in millimeters, sometimes followed by the width in millimeters. For example, a Mondopoint size of 270/100 means a foot that is 270 mm long and 100 mm wide. This system is used by NATO for military footwear and by some ski boot manufacturers, but it has not been widely adopted in consumer retail. The centimeter measurement in the converter above is essentially a simplified version of Mondopoint. Japan and South Korea use centimeter-based sizing as their primary system, which is why their sizes are often the easiest to understand internationally.
