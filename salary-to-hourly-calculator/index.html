How Salary-to-Hourly Conversion Works

The basic formula is straightforward: divide your annual salary by the total number of working hours in a year. For a standard full-time position, that is 40 hours per week multiplied by 52 weeks, which equals 2,080 hours.

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ (Hours per Week × Weeks per Year)

For example, a $60,000 salary divided by 2,080 hours gives you $28.85 per hour. If you work fewer weeks (say 50, accounting for 2 weeks unpaid vacation), the hourly rate increases to $30.00.

Going the other direction — from hourly to salary — you multiply: Hourly Rate × Hours per Week × Weeks per Year = Annual Salary. An hourly worker earning $25/hour at 40 hours/week for 52 weeks makes $52,000 per year before taxes.

Why take-home pay matters more: Your gross hourly rate is helpful for comparison, but the number that actually hits your bank account is lower. Federal income tax, state income tax, and FICA contributions (Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%) all reduce your paycheck. Use the tax breakdown section above to see the difference.

For more detail on how salary and wages work in the United States, see the Wikipedia article on Salary.

Frequently Asked Questions

60k a year is how much an hour?

A $60,000 annual salary equals approximately $28.85 per hour based on a standard 40-hour work week (2,080 hours per year). Before taxes, that is about $2,307.69 per biweekly paycheck or $5,000 per month. After federal and state taxes, your actual take-home hourly rate will depend on your filing status and state of residence.

How do I convert salary to hourly rate?

Divide your annual salary by the total hours worked in a year. For full-time: Annual Salary ÷ (40 × 52) = Hourly Rate. Adjust the 40 and 52 if you work different hours or take unpaid time off. The formula works in reverse too: multiply your hourly rate by hours per week and weeks per year to get your annual salary.

Does pay frequency affect my hourly rate?

No. Whether you are paid weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly, your hourly rate stays the same. However, biweekly pay gives you 26 paychecks per year while semi-monthly gives 24. This means individual biweekly paychecks are smaller, but you get two additional payments throughout the year.

How much does a $100,000 salary equal per hour after taxes?

Before taxes, $100,000/year equals about $48.08/hour. After federal taxes (single filer, standard deduction) and FICA, your effective take-home is roughly $35 to $40 per hour, depending on your state. States with no income tax like Texas and Florida give you a higher take-home compared to states like California or New York.

What counts as total compensation beyond salary?

Total compensation includes base salary plus employer-paid health insurance ($6,000-$20,000/year), 401(k) match (typically 3-6% of salary), paid time off (worth your daily rate times the number of days), life and disability insurance, HSA/FSA contributions, and other perks. These benefits can add 20% to 40% on top of your base salary.
