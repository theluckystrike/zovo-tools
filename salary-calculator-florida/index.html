How to Use This Tool

Getting your Florida salary breakdown takes less than a minute. Here is how:

  1. Enter your annual gross salary - Your total compensation before any deductions. If you are paid hourly, multiply your rate by 2,080 (40 hours times 52 weeks) for the annual equivalent.
  2. Select your filing status - Single, Married Filing Jointly, or Head of Household. This determines your standard deduction and the income thresholds for each tax bracket.
  3. Choose your pay frequency - How often you receive your paycheck: weekly (52), biweekly (26), semi-monthly (24), or monthly (12).
  4. Add any pre-tax deductions - Annual 401(k) contributions, HSA contributions, or other pre-tax benefits that reduce your taxable income.
  5. Click Calculate - See your federal tax, FICA, and take-home pay broken down by year, pay period, month, and hourly rate.

Since Florida charges zero state income tax, your deductions are simpler than in most states. The calculator uses 2024 federal tax brackets and current FICA rates. Your actual paycheck may differ slightly based on W-4 elections, employer benefits, and insurance premiums.

Florida Has No State Income Tax

Florida's lack of a personal income tax is written directly into the state constitution. Article VII, Section 5 of the Florida Constitution states: "No tax upon estates or inheritances or upon the income of natural persons who are residents or citizens of the state shall be levied." Changing this would require a constitutional amendment approved by voters - something that has never come close to happening.

For a Florida worker earning $70,000 per year:

  • Social Security: $70,000 x 6.2% = $4,340/year ($166.92 per biweekly paycheck)
  • Medicare: $70,000 x 1.45% = $1,015/year ($39.04 per biweekly paycheck)

The Social Security wage base is adjusted annually. For 2024, it is $168,600 (up from $160,200 in 2023). Once your earnings exceed this amount in a calendar year, Social Security withholding stops for the rest of the year, which means larger paychecks in the latter months. Common Deductions Guide

Understanding what comes out of your Florida paycheck helps you plan your budget and reduce your tax bill:

Pre-Tax Deductions (Reduce Taxable Income)

  • 401(k) / 403(b) Contributions - Up to $23,000 in 2024 ($30,500 if age 50+). At a 22% federal bracket, saving $23,000 reduces your tax by $5,060. In Florida, this is the single best tax-reduction strategy available.
  • HSA (Health Savings Account) - $4,150 individual / $8,300 family. Triple tax advantage in Florida: pre-tax contributions, tax-free growth, and tax-free qualified withdrawals. Since Florida has no state tax, there is no additional state benefit, but the federal savings alone are significant.
  • Traditional IRA - Up to $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+). Deductible if you meet income limits or do not have an employer plan.
  • FSA (Flexible Spending Account) - $3,200 for healthcare, $5,000 for dependent care. Use-it-or-lose-it rules apply.
  • Health Insurance Premiums - Employer-sponsored premiums paid pre-tax through Section 125 cafeteria plans.

Post-Tax Deductions

  • Roth 401(k) / Roth IRA - Paid with after-tax dollars, but withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free. In a no-income-tax state like Florida, Roth contributions are especially attractive because you are already paying a lower total tax rate.
  • Disability Insurance - Florida does not have mandatory state disability insurance (unlike California). Some employers offer voluntary plans deducted post-tax.
  • Garnishments - Court-ordered child support, student loan garnishments, or tax levies.

Other Taxes in Florida

While Florida does not tax your income, the state generates revenue through other taxes. Here is the full picture of what Florida residents pay:

Sales Tax

Florida has a 6% state sales tax, with counties allowed to add up to 1.5% more. Most counties charge the full amount, putting the combined rate at 7-7.5%. Some items are exempt: groceries (unprepared food), prescription drugs, and residential rent. Florida also has two annual sales tax holidays for back-to-school supplies, disaster preparation, and recreational activities.

Property Tax

Florida's average effective property tax rate is about 0.86%, which is actually below the national average of 1.1%. On a $400,000 home, that is roughly $3,440 per year. Florida offers a Homestead Exemption that reduces your assessed value by up to $50,000 for your primary residence. There is also a Save Our Homes cap that limits annual assessment increases to 3% or CPI (whichever is lower).

Tourist Development Tax

Florida counties charge a tourist development tax (hotel tax or bed tax) of 2-6% on short-term accommodations. This affects visitors, not residents paying regular rent. It generates billions in revenue, effectively having tourists help fund local services that benefit residents.

Documentary Stamp Tax

When you buy a home in Florida, you pay a documentary stamp tax of $0.70 per $100 of the sale price (or $0.60 in Miami-Dade). On a $400,000 home, that is $2,800. There is also a small intangible tax on mortgages ($2 per $1,000 of mortgage value).

Corporate Income Tax

Florida does have a corporate income tax of 5.5% on net income over $50,000. This applies to C-corporations but not to sole proprietors, partnerships, or S-corps (which pass income through to individual returns). The Florida Department of Revenue administers this tax.

Florida Reemployment Tax

Retirement in Florida

Florida is the number one retirement destination in America, and the tax structure is a major reason why. Here is why retirees flock to the Sunshine State:

Tax-Free Retirement Income

  • Social Security - Not taxed by Florida (or the federal government for many retirees with moderate income).
  • 401(k) and IRA Distributions - No state tax on withdrawals. A $60,000 annual 401(k) distribution is state tax-free in Florida but would cost roughly $2,100+ in California state tax.
  • Pensions - Fully exempt from Florida state tax. This includes military pensions, government pensions, and private pensions.
  • Capital Gains - No state tax on investment gains. This is particularly valuable for retirees drawing down investment portfolios.
  • Estate Tax - Florida has no estate or inheritance tax. The federal estate tax exemption is $13.61 million per individual (2024).

Homestead Protection

Florida has one of the strongest homestead protections in the country. Your primary residence is protected from most creditors (with some exceptions for mortgages and property taxes). The homestead exemption also reduces property tax by up to $50,000 on your assessed value, and the Save Our Homes provision caps annual assessment increases at 3% or CPI.

Senior Property Tax Exemptions

Florida residents 65 and older with household income below $36,614 (2024) may qualify for an additional $50,000 homestead exemption. Some counties offer even more generous exemptions for long-term senior residents. Contact your county property appraiser's office for details.

Tax Tips for Florida Workers

Even without state income tax, there are smart strategies to reduce your overall tax bill and keep more of your earnings:

  1. Max out your 401(k) - The $23,000 limit (2024) saves you $5,060+ in federal taxes at the 22% bracket. In Florida, this is the most impactful tax-reduction move since there is no state tax to worry about.
  2. HSA contributions work perfectly in Florida - Unlike California and New Jersey which do not recognize HSA state tax deductions, Florida has no income tax at all. Your HSA gives you the full federal deduction with zero state complications.
  3. Consider Roth contributions heavily - Florida's 0% state tax rate makes Roth 401(k) and Roth IRA contributions very attractive. You pay a lower total tax rate now (federal only), and withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free at both federal and state levels.
  4. File your Homestead Exemption immediately - If you own your home, file for the homestead exemption with your county property appraiser. It saves $500-$1,000+ per year in property taxes and activates the Save Our Homes assessment cap.
  5. Take advantage of sales tax holidays - Florida has multiple sales tax holidays each year: back-to-school (July/August), disaster preparedness (June), and recreational activities (July). Plan larger purchases around these dates.
  6. No state tax on investment income - Capital gains, dividends, and interest are only taxed federally. If you invest actively, Florida's 0% state rate means more compounding growth.
  7. Review your W-4 annually - Make sure your federal withholding matches your actual tax liability. An incorrect W-4 means you are either lending the IRS money interest-free or facing a tax bill in April.

Major Florida Industries and Salaries

Florida has a diverse economy that extends well beyond tourism. Understanding the key sectors helps you benchmark your salary against the market.

Tourism and Hospitality

Florida is the top tourist destination in the US, welcoming over 140 million visitors annually. Theme parks (Walt Disney World, Universal Studios, SeaWorld), beaches, and cruise ports drive this sector. Hotel managers earn $55,000-$85,000, restaurant managers $48,000-$70,000, and theme park operations managers $60,000-$90,000. Orlando and Miami are the primary hubs.

Aerospace and Defense

Cape Canaveral and the Space Coast are at the center of America's space industry. SpaceX, Blue Origin, Boeing, and Lockheed Martin all have significant Florida operations. Aerospace engineers earn $95,000-$140,000, and the Kennedy Space Center alone supports thousands of high-paying jobs. Patrick Space Force Base and MacDill Air Force Base add to the defense sector presence.

Healthcare

Florida's large and aging population creates enormous demand for healthcare workers. Major hospital systems like HCA, AdventHealth, and Baptist Health employ tens of thousands. Registered nurses earn $72,000 median, physical therapists $82,000, and physicians $250,000+. South Florida is also a medical tourism hub, attracting international patients.

Financial Services

Jacksonville is a major financial services hub, home to Fidelity National Information Services (FIS), Black Knight, and major banking operations. Miami is the gateway to Latin American finance. Financial analysts earn $70,000-$100,000, while senior positions in asset management and banking reach $150,000-$300,000.

Technology (Growing Fast)

Miami's tech scene has exploded since 2020, with dozens of major companies and venture capital firms relocating or expanding. The "Miami Tech" movement has brought thousands of high-paying jobs. Software engineers in Miami earn $100,000-$150,000, and the combination of no state income tax plus lower costs than San Francisco makes the net compensation competitive with Silicon Valley.

Self-Employment Taxes in Florida

If you are self-employed or freelance in Florida, here is what you know about your tax obligations:

Self-Employment Tax Breakdown

Self-employed workers pay both the employee and employer portions of FICA, totaling 15.3% on net earnings up to $168,600 and 2.9% (Medicare only) above that. The Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% kicks in at $200,000 for single filers.

Florida Business Formation

Florida has favorable business formation costs. Filing an LLC costs $125, and there is no annual franchise tax or minimum fee for LLCs (unlike California's $800 annual minimum). Florida's Sunbiz.org portal makes registration fast and straightforward. Annual reports for LLCs are $138.75 and due May 1 each year.

Quarterly Estimated Payments

Self-employed Florida workers must make quarterly estimated federal tax payments to the IRS (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15) if they expect to owe $1,000+ in taxes. Since Florida has no income tax, you do not make any state estimated payments - one less administrative burden compared to states like California or New York.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Florida have a state income tax?

No. Florida's constitution explicitly prohibits a personal income tax. Florida is one of nine US states with zero state income tax. Workers only pay federal income tax, Social Security (6.2%), and Medicare (1.45%).

How much will my Florida paycheck be after taxes?

It depends on your gross salary, filing status, and deductions. For a $70,000 salary (single), expect roughly $57,000-$58,000 take-home annually. Since there is no state tax, your deductions are smaller than most states. Use the calculator above for your exact numbers.

What taxes do Florida residents pay?

Florida residents pay federal income tax (10-37%), Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), state sales tax (6% + local), and property tax (avg 0.86%). There is no state income tax, no estate tax, and no inheritance tax.

Is Florida cheaper than California?

For taxes, significantly. Florida has 0% state income tax versus California's 1-13.3%. On a $100,000 salary, a Florida worker saves over $6,000 per year versus California. Housing costs are lower in most Florida metros compared to California, though South Florida (Miami, Fort Lauderdale) is expensive.

What is the Florida minimum wage?

Florida's minimum wage is $13.00/hour as of September 2024, increasing by $1 per year to $15.00 by September 2026. This was approved by voters through Amendment 2 in November 2020. It is well above the federal minimum of $7.25.

Does Florida tax retirement income?

No. Florida does not tax any retirement income. 401(k) distributions, IRA withdrawals, pensions, Social Security, annuities, and capital gains are all free from state tax. This makes Florida one of the best states for retirees from a tax perspective.

What is the Florida Reemployment Tax?

Florida's Reemployment Tax is the state's unemployment insurance, paid by employers only. Rates range from 0.10% to 5.40% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages. Employees never see this on their paychecks. The maximum weekly benefit is $275 for up to 12 weeks.

How does Florida fund government without an income tax?

Florida relies on sales tax (6%+ generating about $30 billion annually), tourism taxes, property taxes, documentary stamp taxes on real estate, corporate income tax (5.5%), and lottery revenue. Tourism is a huge factor - visitors effectively subsidize services for residents.
